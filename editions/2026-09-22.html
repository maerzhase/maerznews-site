Abgeordnetenhauswahl · Provisional result

Die Linke wins Berlin outright, and the SPD falls to its worst result ever

A party on 12.3 per cent in 2023 has doubled its vote and come first for the first time in the city's history — on the highest turnout in over a decade.

rbb24 · Berlin · reported 21 September

Die Linke took 25.7 per cent of Berlin's second votes on Sunday, more than doubling its 2023 share and finishing first in the capital for the first time. The CDU came second on 18.8 per cent — a loss of 9.5 points, the heaviest of any party — followed by the AfD on 16.3, the Greens on 14.3 and the SPD on 12.1.

For the Social Democrats, the party of Willy Brandt and Klaus Wowereit, that is the worst result in the history of Berlin state elections; their lead candidate Steffen Krach called it "catastrophic". The BSW fell 0.3 points short of the five-per-cent threshold and stays out of parliament, as does the FDP on 2.5.

Berlin has voted left. Governing with the left will not be so simple for Elif Eralp.Jan Menzel, rbb24 commentary

Turnout was 74.2 per cent of 2.49 million eligible voters, sharply up from 62.9 per cent in 2023, and 16- and 17-year-olds could vote for the first time. Lead candidate Elif Eralp — who had never held a senior office before — reads the result as a mandate to become Berlin's governing mayor, but she needs two coalition partners: 158 members were elected, and a majority takes at least 80. A Linke–Green–SPD deal is the obvious route, and the Greens have already signalled willingness. Every party has ruled out the AfD.

The scale of the shift shows up in the map. Die Linke won Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg's five direct mandates outright, taking the original Green stronghold, and in one Wedding polling district it added 29 points to reach 50.5 per cent. It came first in both halves of the city — 27.8 per cent in the former East, 24.1 in the West — the first time the party has been strongest in territory that was once West Germany.

The books are in

Ovar's Carnival cost €1.43 million this year — and took less at the gate

The Câmara's final report puts the 2026 Carnival's total spend at €1,427,442.66, some €113,535.61 more than last year, a rise of 8.6 per cent. Income went the other way, down 17.8 per cent, so the share the municipality had to carry itself climbed to €841,000. February's rain is the explanation the report leans on: it kept crowds away and forced several events to be cancelled or moved. Ovar's Carnival is the concelho's biggest annual undertaking and one of the two or three that matter nationally, which makes a widening gap between what it costs and what it earns a live budget question rather than an accounting footnote.

The districts

Six districts for Die Linke, four for the CDU — and Marzahn-Hellersdorf for the AfD

Sunday's district-assembly (BVV) results split the city into four pieces. Die Linke came first in Pankow, Mitte, Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg, Neukölln, Lichtenberg and Treptow-Köpenick — topping 40.1 per cent in Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg, ahead of the Greens on 25.2, and winning Neukölln by more than sixteen points (32.0 to the CDU's 15.6). The CDU held four western districts: Steglitz-Zehlendorf (28.5), Spandau (27.7), Reinickendorf (27.4) and, narrowly, Charlottenburg-Wilmersdorf on 24.0 against 22.5 for the Greens. The Greens salvaged one district, Tempelhof-Schöneberg, on 22.7 to Die Linke's 20.3. In Marzahn-Hellersdorf the AfD won clearly with 33.8 per cent, thirteen and a half points clear of Die Linke — the sharpest illustration that Berlin's dividing line no longer runs east to west.

Obituary

Jorge Palma has died at 76

The singer and composer died on Monday night, ending a career of more than five decades whose songs became part of the country's collective memory. Tributes arrived from across Portuguese public life through the night and morning: the foreign minister Paulo Rangel called it "a sad day — a very sad day — for Portuguese music", and the prime minister's line, "Jorge Palma is leaving, but his music stays", was picked up everywhere. President António José Seguro remembered him as an "icon".
