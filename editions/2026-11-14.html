Germany · Federal budget · 2027

A record €560.9 billion: Germany's 2027 budget clears committee with almost €123bn of new borrowing, and still no money for cheaper electricity

After a session of more than fifteen hours, the Bundestag's budget committee approved the federal budget early on Friday. The Bundestag votes on it in the week of 24 to 27 November; whether Chancellor Merz gets his electricity-tax cut will be settled by the coalition leaders on Tuesday.

tagesschau · Bundestag · Federal Finance Ministry · as read at 06:30 on 14 November 2026

The committee's last changes, made overnight into Friday, leave the federal government with spending of about €560.9 billion next year, more than any German budget before it and some €36 billion above 2026. Net borrowing in the core budget comes to almost €123 billion, roughly €4 billion more than the government's own first draft. Add the credit-financed investment from the special funds for infrastructure and the armed forces, and tagesschau expects total new debt of more than €200 billion.

What the committee did not find was money for the cut in electricity tax for everyone that the coalition agreement promises and that Friedrich Merz has pushed. It would cost about €5 billion and, by the broadcaster's reckoning, save households only a few euros. Criticism came not only from the opposition but from inside the Union; the coalition committee meets on Tuesday to decide whether the cut can still be slipped in during budget week.

The agreement is without direction, without ambition and without vision. Sabine Henkel, ARD commentary on the budget

The Bundestag is due to pass the budget in its sitting week of 24 to 27 November. The budget sets how much each ministry may spend in 2027, and with it what is left for Berlin and Brandenburg's own demands, from culture funding to the hospitals: Berlin's would-be coalition of SPD, Greens and Left is negotiating its own city budget under the same pressure, and the city's theatres and opera houses are already asking for their share (see below).

Ovar · Carnival · Cyberattack

Another week, still no money: Ovar's carnival groups wait for their 2027 subsidy because the council has no access to its own bank accounts

Ovar's carnival groups and samba schools have still not received the first instalment of the council grant they use to start work on next year's parade, OvarNews reports. Last week they were asked to print and hand in their social-security clearance certificates again, because the council's staff have had no online access to that data since the cyberattack in October; the repeat was supposed to let the payments go through this week.

They did not. Members of several groups told the paper "there were no payments because of the cyberattack", and that the municipality still cannot reach its bank accounts. The 2027 Carnaval falls early, so costumes, floats and the rest of the parades need to start now: "It is incomprehensible that they can't pay so the work can begin."

Berlin · Districts

Eight weeks after the vote, ten of Berlin's twelve districts still have no mayor, and the Left may end up with half of them

The new district assemblies (BVV) met for the first time on 29 October, but only Reinickendorf and Spandau elected a mayor and district office straight away, rbb24 reports; everywhere else the parties are still negotiating. In Reinickendorf the CDU's Sebastian Pieper took over, and the AfD's candidate for deputy, Thomas Ruschin, fell one vote short although the CDU had signalled it could back him. In Spandau the CDU's Frank Bewig stays on; the AfD's two nominees for councillor were voted down.

On the current state of talks the Left would provide the most mayors, perhaps five or six, the Greens three and the CDU two; the SPD would get none. Still open: whether the AfD wins its first district mayor in Marzahn-Hellersdorf, and who runs Neukölln. Each district office has six members, filled in proportion to the parties' strength, but the assembly does not have to elect the nominees, so some seats stay empty.

Security · GitHub

Stolen tokens, 61,764 repos: a campaign is planting secret-stealers in GitHub workflows and wiping its tracks within a minute

Since Tuesday unknown attackers have been using stolen GitHub access tokens to push code into repositories that harvests GitHub Actions secrets and cloud credentials, heise reports. Aikido counts 4,837 hijacked accounts and 61,764 repositories attacked in the campaign it calls wfx-loot; in more than 1,800 cases the workflows have already run the payload. No flaw in Actions is involved: the tokens' write rights are abused, some of them, says Fly.io, lifted from publicly exposed .git/config files.

The pattern: a test pull request to confirm write access, then a shell script slipped into a workflow or an npm install hook, and the change and the run's logs deleted, often leaving only a missing run number. Through contributor rights the attack reaches repos whose owners lost nothing; where workflows can reach AWS or Azure, it plants backdoors in the cloud accounts. It takes keys for OpenAI, Anthropic and Cursor too, which is why heise warns that rotating secrets alone is not enough.

Potsdam · Restitution

Missing since the Red Army reached Rheinsberg in 1945, Le Brun's Holy Family returns to Frederick the Great's New Palace

Charles Le Brun, later court painter to Louis XIV, made the picture of the sleeping Christ Child with the Holy Family around 1655; Frederick the Great bought it for a guest apartment of the New Palace at Sanssouci. In 1942 it was evacuated with much of the palace art to Schloss Rheinsberg, 60 miles north-west, to escape the bombing.

Soviet troops took most of those paintings east in 1945, but not all arrived; some slipped into private hands in Rheinsberg. The Le Brun is last recorded there on 30 July 1945 and resurfaced at a Berlin auction house in 2021. With help from the Ernst von Siemens Art Foundation, the Prussian Palaces foundation unveils it in the Upper Red Chambers on Wednesday, 18 November, at 2.30pm.

United States · Aviation

America grounds every departure for more than four hours, the longest stop since 9/11, and blames an "incredibly old" air-traffic system

At 15:54 Eastern time on Friday the FAA halted all departures within the US "due to extraordinary circumstances": a communications failure hitting every air-traffic control centre. More than 3,500 planes were airborne at the time; some diverted, others flew on. By FlightAware's count more than 8,500 flights were delayed and nearly 8,000 cancelled, and Eurocontrol suspended all US-bound traffic from its 42 member states at the FAA's request; Portugal's airport operator ANA told passengers to check their flights.

Skies reopened at 20:15 Eastern. Homeland Security said there was no sign of an attack; transport secretary Sean Duffy blamed the ageing control system, and the administration blamed Congress. It was only the third nationwide ground stop ever, Ars Technica notes, and the FAA has meanwhile extended its AI traffic-advice tool to all US airspace.
