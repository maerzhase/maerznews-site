Germany · Energy

Germany's solar panels have already beaten all of 2025, with three months to spare

90.2 terawatt-hours by Sunday against 90.0 in the whole of last year. The industry calls 27 September "Solar Day", and warns that Berlin's next energy law could slow things down.

tagesschau · heise online · BSW-Solar · Fraunhofer ISE Energy-Charts · 27–28 September

By Sunday, Germany had produced 90.2 terawatt-hours of solar power this year. In the whole of 2025 it made 90.0. The figures come from the solar industry association BSW-Solar, which uses the Energy-Charts data of the Fraunhofer Institute for Solar Energy Systems. It declared 27 September "Solar Day" to mark the moment. Our own count of the grid data shows the same picture month by month: every month since March has beaten its 2025 equivalent, and July was up 44%.

BSW-Solar says two things did it: an unusually sunny year and the steady build-out of panels on homes, business roofs and open fields. More and more battery storage is being built alongside them. The sunshine has a cost too. The Rhine at Cologne fell to a record low of 40 centimetres on Monday morning (see below).

"The solar record is a reason to celebrate, but not a reason to sit back." Carsten Körnig, BSW-Solar

The association used the record to push back at the government's plans. The 2027 revision of the Renewable Energy Sources Act (EEG) and a new grid package are meant to steer where new capacity is built, region by region. From 2027, small systems under 25 kilowatts (mostly private rooftops) would also lose their permanent feed-in payment. "Right now we must not slow down people's and companies' willingness to invest," said managing director Carsten Körnig.

Berlin · Linke

The Berlin Linke files a criminal complaint against interior minister Dobrindt

Elif Eralp, the Linke's lead candidate in Berlin, and her state party have reported federal interior minister Alexander Dobrindt (CSU) for defamation and insult, their lawyer says. It goes back to Thursday's ZDF talk show Maybrit Illner. The host misspoke, saying "Elif Eralp says she is an antisemite", and at once corrected herself: "She is not an antisemite." Dobrindt answered: "Well, she is."

Lawyer Jasper Prigge argues that Dobrindt adopted the slip as his own and so spread a false statement of fact. The complaint says this could make it harder for Eralp to work in public life and could harm the forming of a government in Berlin. Her party is now in exploratory talks with the SPD and the Greens.

Berlin · Alte Nationalgalerie

"From Monet and Manet to money": Berlin shows the dealer who brought Impressionism to Germany

"Cassirer: Breakthrough of Impressionism" opens with Degas's Miss La La (1879), Renoir's Lise with a Parasol (1867) and Max Slevogt's 1901 portrait of Paul Cassirer's daughter. Cassirer ran his Kunstsalon in the Tiergarten from 1901, in rooms designed by Henry van de Velde. He hung paintings in single rows while crowded salon hangs were in fashion, and sold Monet, Manet, Cézanne, van Gogh and Munch to private buyers and to the museum itself. Many of those works are still in the Nationalgalerie. ARTnews praises the show for being open about the money behind the canon. The title comes from a caricature of the time.
