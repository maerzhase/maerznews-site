Games · Business · Culture

GTA VI takes $3.79 billion on its first day, more than three times the biggest opening weekend any film has had

Take-Two's own figure for launch day, 19 November, comes with a record 32.5 million pre-orders. Thirteen years after GTA V, the game that reportedly cost up to $1.5 billion to make has become the biggest entertainment launch on record.

heise online · ARTnews · Pitchfork · as read at 07:00 on 22 November 2026

Rockstar released Grand Theft Auto VI on 19 November for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S; there is still no date for a PC version. Take-Two says the game took $3.79 billion on that first day and was pre-ordered 32.5 million times. Before the announcement the analysts at Sensor Tower, working from purchase receipts and public player profiles, had counted at least $2.36 billion, so the publisher's number is plausible: not every player shows up in the networks' public data.

The comparisons only go so far. Take-Two did not set the figure against GTA V, which in 2013 made an estimated $800 million on day one and needed three days to pass $1 billion; GTA VI did more than three times that in 24 hours. Films are judged on opening weekends, and the best of those, Avengers: Endgame in 2019, made $1.2 billion. heise calls GTA VI the most successful entertainment product ever, and probably the most expensive: reports put its development at between $1 billion and $1.5 billion.

With this success GTA 6 is not only the most successful game, but the most successful entertainment product of all time. heise online

The culture around it is part of the draw. Vice City, the game's Miami, has a faithful copy of the Bass Museum, limestone facade and all, with Ugo Rondinone's boulder tower outside rebuilt from stacked emojis (see Arts). The soundtrack album includes Lana Del Rey's long-leaked "Crazy for You", a score by Justice, and new songs from Dua Lipa, Arca and Kali Uchis (see Sound). Why it matters: one game now outsells the film industry's biggest weekend, and the people who make culture are queueing to be in it.

Aveiro · Port

An explosion, a fire and a domino effect: the Port of Aveiro rehearses its worst day, cut back by the storm

The exercise began at 08:04 on Saturday with a simulated explosion and fire at one company in the Liquid Bulk Terminal, spreading to the firm next door. The port activated its internal emergency plan and evacuated the terminal, then the Solid Bulk Terminal and the logistics and industrial zone (ZALI); because of the scale, Ílhavo council's external emergency plan was triggered too. The first Ílhavo volunteer fire engines arrived at 08:12, and a crisis room in the port authority's headquarters ran the response.

Saturday's orange weather warning changed the plan. The drill was turned into a command-post exercise focused on coordination between the agencies, with fewer vehicles and crews in the field. Why it matters: the bulk terminals on the Gafanha side store fuels and chemicals a few kilometres from Aveiro's beaches, and a domino accident there is the scenario the region least wants to meet for real.

Berlin · Justice

Berlin frees 57 prisoners early for Christmas, the fewest in years, and won't say why

The Weihnachtsamnestie, formally a pardon, lets out early inmates whose sentences would end around the turn of the year anyway, so they can spend the holidays outside and sort out a flat, a job and their papers before offices close. This year's provisional figure from the Senate justice department is 57. In 2025 it was 66; in 2024 and 2023 about 100; in 2019 and 2022, 170 each.

The department, run by CDU senator Felor Badenberg, gave no reason for the decline and says final numbers, and the offences involved, come later; in past years they ranged widely. Releases have already begun, five weeks before Christmas Eve.

Mathematics · Lean

The Lean and Mathlib communities certify the Riemann proof: "We have full confidence" it is correct

Two days after Levent Alpöge, working with Claude, released his proof of the Riemann hypothesis as a 17-million-line Lean library, the people who maintain Lean and its mathematics library have signed off on it. In a joint statement endorsed by the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative they write: "We hereby certify that both the statement and the proof of the Riemann Hypothesis are correct."

The codebase depends only on Mathlib, they say, and volunteers spent Friday checking it against four published criteria for trusting a machine proof, all of which it meets. What a computer can check, it has. What remains is for mathematicians to understand why it is true; the statement looks forward to "all the activity" that will explain the new ideas. The Clay Institute's $1 million prize still requires two years of scrutiny.

Ireland · Policy

€325 a week, no strings: Ireland's basic income for artists works for the artists, if not yet for the scene

For three years, from 2022 to 2025, 2,000 Irish artists chosen for the Basic Income for the Arts pilot received €325 every week, to cut the need for unrelated jobs and buy time to make work. In February the government made it permanent; culture minister Patrick O'Donovan called it "a game changer" that is "here to stay", and thousands more artists hope to get in.

ARTnews went to Dublin Gallery Weekend this month. Recipients described the money as a major boost to their working lives, but dealers, curators and museum directors saw little sign it has changed the city's contemporary art scene as a whole. The weekend also gave out the first Francis Bacon Awards: €25,000 for painter Patrick Graham's lifetime achievement and €15,000 to Belfast-born Cathy Wilkes. Sydney announced a scheme of its own this week.

New album · Warp

Lorenzo Senni's All Ages makes trance synths play hardcore guitar riffs

Electronic · Warp · out 20 Nov · Spotify ↗︎

The Italian producer's first album since 2020 argues that trance and hardcore punk share more synapses than you would think. He stacks clipped Roland JP-8000 and SH-101 synths until they mimic guitar riffs, and brings in Xiu Xiu's Jamie Stewart for a feature.

The title borrows the door policy of hardcore shows, and perhaps the revolutionary spirit of ravers too. "Time feels short in hardcore because it is short," Senni says. "That is beautiful and interesting to me." Pitchfork put it among its five albums of the week.

Climate · COP31

Thirty hours into overtime, COP31 puts a final draft to the vote that admits 1.5C is lost and never names oil, coal or gas

Summit president Murat Kurum presented a ten-page text at about 01:00 in Antalya, saying "we believe we have presented a text all parties can agree to". Delegates from more than 190 countries reconvene at 09:00 local time (06:00 in Lisbon) to decide. For the first time a COP text recognises that the world is on track to miss the 1.5C goal.

It still does not mention "transitioning away from fossil fuels", the omission that drew walk-out threats from the EU, Germany and Pacific states, and only refers back to the 2023 Dubai decision that said so. The Africa Group gets no figure for adaptation money, which is pushed to later meetings. Without consensus the talks could be suspended.
