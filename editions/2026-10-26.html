Brazil · Run-off · Portugal's vote

Lula comes from behind to win a fourth term with 51.8%, Brazilians in Lisbon and Porto gave him 60%, and Flávio Bolsonaro wants the vote annulled

Three weeks ago Flávio Bolsonaro won the first round by almost two points. On Sunday Lula da Silva, 80, beat him by 3.6, with 64.3 million votes, the most he has ever received. No one had won a Brazilian run-off after losing the first round before. The loser says a Supreme Court judge changed the rules mid-game.

Observador · RTP · tagesschau · BBC · Lusa · 25–26 October 2026

The count began badly. The electoral court, the TSE, published its first figures with 0.25% of votes in, then stopped for two hours: Supreme Court justice Cristiano Zanin had ordered polling stations to stay open two hours longer. When the count resumed, at about 22:00 Lisbon time, Bolsonaro was ahead, by 0.06 points at 22:45 with 13% counted. Lula retook the lead at 23:30 with 56% counted, and at 00:10, with 97% in, the TSE declared the race "mathematically decided".

The final tally: 51.79% to 48.21%, 64,256,232 votes to 59,826,041. Turnout reached 80.8% of 159 million registered voters, RTP reports, the highest since 2006. Minas Gerais, the state whose winner has taken every presidency since 1989, flipped from Bolsonaro to Lula by fewer than 100,000 votes. "I never won an election in the first round, but I never lost one in the second," Lula told supporters, praising "the defeat of denialism".

"It is impossible to recognise the results of these elections in the context in which things happened." Flávio Bolsonaro, in Brasília, overnight

Bolsonaro, the senator son of the convicted former president, said he would ask for the election to be annulled. He accused Zanin, once Lula's lawyer, of changing the rules "in the middle of the game" and claimed his party's monitors were removed from the counting room. The TSE's president, Kassio Nunes Marques, said the extension did not alter the result. Lula will still face a Congress led by the right; Bolsonaro's PL holds more governorships than any other party, six.

Among Brazilians in Portugal, turnout was higher than in the first round. Lula took 59.42% nationwide, 60.10% in Lisbon and 60.13% in Porto; Bolsonaro won only in Faro. Police had to separate the two camps at Lisbon's Cidade Universitária, where a Brazilian woman was attacked after voting.

Berlin · Housing

Berlin's left wants to socialise the big landlords; Dobrindt's new law would make them pay market value

The first details of the federal "investment protection law", dubbed the anti-expropriation law, are out, and they are aimed squarely at Berlin. Socialising rented flats would be allowed only where a state proves "compelling necessity", having exhausted every other means such as building and subsidies; and compensation would have to be paid in cash, at a market value set by an expert valuation.

That collides with the exploratory paper of the Linke, SPD and Greens now sounding out a Berlin coalition: they want to take on private landlords with more than 10,000 flats and have budgeted €16 billion in compensation, probably below market value. Their bill would reach the Abgeordnetenhaus in mid-2028. Interior minister Alexander Dobrindt (CSU) calls it a "left-wing socialisation fetish" incompatible with the Basic Law.

Linke leader Luigi Pantisano says such a law would not survive the Constitutional Court and asks whether the federal SPD stands with "the rent mafia or the tenants". Whether coalition talks begin is decided on Saturday at a Linke state congress; the SPD and Greens have already said yes.

AI · Medicine

A ten-year-old image network, tuned for twenty minutes, beat two frontier chatbots at diagnosing skin cancer

Guy's Hospital dermatologist Magnus Lynch gave 200 biopsy-proven lesions from the public MILK10k set, each with a clinical photo and a dermoscopy image, to GPT-6 Astra, Claude Fable 5.1 and a fine-tuned Inception v3 from 2015. The CNN named the right one of six diagnoses for 125 (62%); the chatbots managed 84 and 85 (42%).

Each chatbot had a favourite wrong answer: GPT-6 Astra called 75 lesions basal cell carcinoma when 25 were, including 14 of the 30 melanomas. Asked as patients would ask, "do I need to see a doctor?", both said yes to nearly everything, harmless moles included. Cases, prompts and scoring code are published. One of the two models is made by Anthropic, whose Claude writes this paper.

Van Gogh · Science

The stars in The Starry Night are July's, not June's, say astronomers

Art historians have long dated Van Gogh's painting, made at the Saint-Rémy asylum and now at MoMA, to June 1889. A study in Scientific Reports, published on 22 October by the South Korean researchers Elijah Jeong-hyun Kim, Suna Kang, Jungwoo Song and Ho Jin, argues several of its celestial forms fit the sky of July that year better.

They combined star-position software, period weather models and the painter's letters, and say earlier astronomical readings, tied to the June date, had reached contradictory conclusions without ever testing the arrangement quantitatively.

Sample-based pop · EMI · radioeins album of the week from 26 Oct

"No Bad Memories", the Australians' new record, plays like a 50-minute block of TV ads from the 80s, 90s and 2000s: samples of hardware start-up sounds, the click of a diskette going into a PC and commercial jingles become songs and interludes selling invented products. Serbia · Election result

Vučić concedes: Serbia's student movement wins a majority after two years of protest

"It is clear that we have lost these elections," Aleksandar Vučić said late on Sunday, before the official count. Projections give the Student List 138 of the 250 seats, well past the 126 for a majority, against 104 for his Serbian Progressive Party, on a record turnout. Vučić, barred from a third term as president, had hoped to return as prime minister after 14 years in power.

The protests began after the canopy of Novi Sad's railway station collapsed in 2024, killing 16, a disaster that came to stand for corruption. The Student List has no obvious leaders and fielded doctors, actors, academics and athletes. Belgrade and Novi Sad danced in the streets: "Serbia is free," one student told the BBC.
