Europe · Security · Hybrid threats

"No coincidence": Europe now calls the week of hoax bombs and hacked freezers a coordinated attack, and the FBI warned Lisbon about two TAP flights

A third wave of bomb threats hit planes from Lisbon to Sofia on Friday and spread to newsrooms in Dublin. Spain's foreign minister calls it "hybrid aggression and psychological warfare"; Ireland sees "a coordinated campaign"; Portugal's security services are on a "high level of concern".

tagesschau · BBC · Observador · OvarNews · 2–6 November 2026

Friday's threats followed the now-familiar script. A Ryanair plane that had just landed in Lisbon, due to fly on to Rome, was searched by the PSP's special unit, passengers and hold baggage included, and left late. Two TAP flights, one to Paris Orly and one to Copenhagen, were already in the air when the warning arrived; Observador reports it came to the PSP directly from the FBI. Both were cleared and flew on. Elsewhere a Wizz Air flight from Prague to Rome was diverted to Venice, Prague airport counted threats against nine flights, and three planes over Hungary were escorted by fighter jets.

Then the threats jumped sector. Ireland's public broadcaster RTÉ, the Irish Independent and the radio stations Newstalk and Today FM evacuated their Dublin offices; Sky in Britain said it had received a threat too. Ireland's government spoke of "a likely coordinated campaign of disruption". In Madrid, the foreign minister José Manuel Albares went further, folding the supermarket hacks into the same pattern: actors "who act this way to weaken Europe".

"It is clear that this is no coincidence. It is an attempt at hybrid aggression and psychological warfare." José Manuel Albares, Spain's foreign minister, on RTVE

Who is behind it is still unknown. Some emails were signed by the "Male State", a Russian far-right group that says it no longer exists; two pro-Kremlin collectives claimed the refrigeration attacks; and sources in NATO's intelligence exchanges tell Observador that Iran is also under suspicion. Nothing explosive has been found anywhere. What worries Portugal's services is the next step: they name hospitals and power plants such as dams as the targets they fear. Here on the coast, OvarNews asks whether Ovar's and Feira's hacked councils belong to the same story. So far, no one official has said so.

Espinho–Aveiro · Rail

The Linha do Vouga gets its third future in a few years: the infrastructure minister now wants Metro do Porto on it

Miguel Pinto Luz has announced that the corridor of the Vouga line, Portugal's last metre-gauge railway, which starts in Espinho a few kilometres up the coast from Esmoriz, should be used for the Metro do Porto. That contradicts the national rail plan, which foresaw converting the line to standard Iberian broad gauge, and an intermediate idea of turning it into a "metrobus".

The civic movement that has campaigned for the line for years accuses the government of "levity" in taking contradictory decisions, Público reports, and JN finds the PSD and the movement on opposite sides. For the towns along the line, from Espinho through Santa Maria da Feira to Oliveira de Azeméis, the question is no longer whether something will change, but which of three plans anyone will fund.

Berlin · Coalition talks

"We no longer trust her": Berlin's Linke tells Vanessa Emde to give up her seat, or come back within a week

The party's state leaders, Kerstin Wolter and Maximilian Schirmer, say the Neukölln MP has "repeatedly and deliberately" ignored party decisions. To stay, she must delete an Instagram post, apologise for demonstrating against her own leadership on the fringe of last Saturday's party conference, and promise to work within its line. Otherwise, the next step is expulsion from the 44-strong parliamentary group, which needs a two-thirds vote.

Emde won her constituency on 20 September with 46.2% of the first votes. She signed the group's declaration on antisemitism and Israel's right to exist on 13 October, then disowned it in a video. The case has weighed on the red-green-red talks from the start: the SPD and the Greens want the Linke, which hopes to make Elif Eralp Berlin's first woman mayor, to act. They plan to present a coalition contract in January.

Lisbon & Porto · Housing protest

Moedas signs an order to clear Lisbon's housing camp from the Rossio, a day after granting it one tent

On Thursday Lisbon's council had offered the protesters a single "symbolic" tent, Renata Cambra's, inside the Christmas market. On Friday evening it reversed course: the mayor, Carlos Moedas, ordered by despacho the removal of that tent and the whole camp so the traders can set up. The PSP and the traders say the two cannot coexist; Cambra's lawyers say the council is ignoring a court ruling and want the order annulled, and the group Mães pela Acampada is holding a vigil against police charges.

In Porto, the camp in Praça D. João I has put up a tarpaulin and four big tents against storm Bruna and still refuses to leave. The council says the ice rink goes up there from 13 November and has asked the protesters to meet; more than 400 of the city's cultural workers have signed a letter backing the camps and condemning "police repression".

Apps · Rust

Discord is leaving Electron and React behind: its apps are being rebuilt on Rust, drawn by Zed's GPUI

CTO Stanislav Vishnevskiy announced on X that the desktop client will drop Electron and React for a Rust core, with the interface drawn by GPUI, the GPU-accelerated framework Zed Industries wrote for its editor. The same Rust application logic will sit under native Android and iOS interfaces, replacing React Native.

Discord has used Rust since 2018 and moved its message-reading service off Go in 2020 to escape garbage-collector latency spikes. The promise now is a client under 300 MB of RAM, untested in public. The catch: GPUI is not yet at 1.0, and its releases often break compatibility.

Arts
Potsdam · Museums

The Barberini turns ten with a show about how the Impressionists were collected

Hasso Plattner's museum opened in 2017 amid doubts about its rebuilt 18th-century shell and a single patron's power. Since 2020 his foundation's French Impressionists, now one of the largest such collections, hang there on permanent loan.

The anniversary show takes ten of them as starting points for the story of how the movement spread: Renoir's 1866 portrait of his friend Jules LeCoeur in the forest of Fontainebleau, the Hoschedés who ran special trains to their Impressionist country house, and the critic Emil Heilbutt, who brought three of his own Monets to a lecture in Weimar in 1889.
