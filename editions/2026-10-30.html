Crypto · Security · North Korea

One engineer's laptop, $6.3 billion gone: Binance suffers the biggest crypto hack on record, and analysts blame North Korea

The haul is more than four times the previous record, the $1.5 billion taken from Bybit last year. About half has been frozen; Binance says it will cover every loss.

BBC · heise online · Reuters · Hacker News · 29–30 October 2026

On Thursday Binance, the world's largest crypto exchange, confirmed that $6.3 billion in digital assets had been drained from Binance.com. Its first checks point to a single compromised laptop belonging to a DevOps engineer, which let the attacker authorise withdrawals. Deposits have restarted; withdrawals stay suspended and should resume within 48 hours. Binance.US says it is not affected. Bitcoin fell more than 4% as warnings circulated hours before the exchange confirmed anything, and Ether and Solana fell with it.

Two analytics firms, TRM Labs and Chainalysis, attribute the attack to TraderTraitor, a North Korean unit also blamed for the Bybit theft. The rest of the industry moved quickly: Tether says it froze about $1.6 billion, Circle more than $800 million, and a Trump-family crypto venture says it froze funds too. That still leaves the attackers holding billions, enough on its own to beat the more than $2 billion Pyongyang's hackers stole in all of 2025.

"A watershed moment, unlike anything the industry has seen." Ari Redbord, TRM Labs, to the BBC

Binance says it will repay every user from its SAFU emergency fund and its own money; it reported more than $160 billion in customer assets in 2024. Founder Changpeng Zhao, still its largest shareholder, wrote that the attack "hurts, but we will survive". For anyone who builds software it is a familiar lesson at an unfamiliar scale: the weak point was not a smart contract or a protocol, but a developer's machine.

Ovar · High-speed rail

The government declares urgent expropriations in Ovar and Feira for the Porto–Aveiro high-speed line

A dispatch published on Thursday in the Diário da República declares the "public utility, with urgency" of the land and rights needed for sub-section 3 of the Porto (Campanhã)–Aveiro (Oiã) stretch, which runs through the municipalities of Ovar and Santa Maria da Feira. Works on the line began about two weeks ago.

Urgency means the concessionaire, AVAN Norte, can take administrative possession of the plots straight away; it also pays the compensation. The Porto–Oiã concession, signed in July 2025, will cost about €2 billion, with €875 million from the European Investment Bank and €480 million from the EU's Connecting Europe Facility. On 1 October the same procedure covered 1,076 plots further south, in Oliveira do Bairro, Aveiro and Albergaria-a-Velha.

Brandenburg · Civil protection

In Luckenwalde, Germany opens its largest civil-protection centre: a field hospital, a tent town for 5,000, and kit for 50,000 people

The German Red Cross depot, opened on Thursday south of Berlin, is meant to prepare the region for blackouts, forest fires and floods. In giant containers it keeps a mobile shelter module that trucks can turn into a small town for up to 5,000 people, with beds, sanitation, food and medical care, and a packed field hospital that can be flown out and running within 72 hours to treat up to 250,000 people. In all, up to 50,000 people could be looked after from the site.

The Red Cross put in about €40 million and the federal government €50 million. Interior minister Alexander Dobrindt called it a model for more such centres under a €10 billion civil-protection pact running to 2029. Brandenburg's premier Dietmar Woidke pointed to "hybrid threats" against the power grid, and Red Cross president Hermann Gröhe to January's cable fire that left part of Berlin without power for five days.

Education · Rankings

Private schools top the 2025 league tables again, and the best state school only comes in 38th

Porto's Grande Colégio Universal leads for the second year running, with an average of 16.93 out of 20 across 231 national exams. Next come the Colégio Nossa Senhora do Rosário (16.59) and the Colégio Efanor in Matosinhos (16.32), both also in Grande Porto. Among 479 state and 91 private schools with more than 100 exams, the first state school is the Conservatório de Música do Porto, 38th with 14.01.

Averages rose in both sectors, the Portuguese exam went up and maths fell by almost two points. The north dominates: 61.9% of the 223 state schools above the national average are there, and JN counts a third of the top 100 in Porto. Meanwhile 61.7% of the 60 state schools averaging below 10 are in the Lisbon and Setúbal districts, many short of teachers.

Security · Supply chain

Open the repo, run the malware: a worm hides in 42 GitHub projects and fires when you start Claude Code, Gemini or Cursor

JFrog found the 42 repositories, owned by 11 legitimate accounts, seeded through hijacked logins or contributor rights. The attackers added project configuration files for developer tools: launching an AI coding agent in the folder, or opening it as a VS Code workspace with the right settings, could run a hidden .github/init.js.

JFrog ties it to the "testmetest" npm campaign, names 15 poisoned npm packages and sees a family resemblance to the Shai-Hulud worm. How many machines ran the code, and what it took, is unknown. The lesson pairs neatly with today's lead.

Europe · Central bank

Christine Lagarde will leave the ECB in February, months early, before France votes for a president

In a video message on Thursday, the day the bank held its rates at 2.5%, Lagarde said she will step down on 7 February 2027; her term ran to the end of October. The timing lets Emmanuel Macron's government help choose her successor before the spring presidential election, which a far-right candidate leads in the polls, a worry for those who fear a Rassemblement national government could block the appointment.

Dutch economist Klaas Knot and Spain's Pablo Hernández de Cos are the names most often mentioned. Commerzbank's Jörg Krämer wants someone who can stand up to finance ministers chasing low rates; CSU MEP Markus Ferber asks whether a bank proud of its independence should be timing itself to national elections.
