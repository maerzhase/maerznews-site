Technology · Markets

Anthropic's IPO filing: revenue up twelvefold, a $42 billion loss, and a warning about humanity

The prospectus seen by Reuters and the Financial Times shows $4.6 billion in sales last year. Most of the loss is an accounting charge, and the seven founders keep control through a single share.

Reuters · Financial Times · heise online · TechCrunch · Capital Brief · 28–29 September

Anthropic, the maker of the Claude models, made $4.59 billion in revenue in 2025, up from $386 million a year earlier. Its operating loss widened from $2.98 billion to $8.06 billion, and its net loss under US accounting rules came to $41.97 billion, against $8.31 billion in 2024. Roughly $34 billion of that is a paper charge on convertible financing. Spending on computing and infrastructure tripled to $7.33 billion, and the company lists $518 billion in future cloud and compute obligations. It ended the year with $20.3 billion in cash and short-term investments.

The filing also shows how concentrated the business is: two customers each brought in about 12% of revenue. VentureBeat names them as the coding tools Cursor and Microsoft's GitHub Copilot, heise notes, so both now belong to rivals. TechCrunch reports the company is on track for a second straight quarter of operating profit on an adjusted basis this year.

The prospectus says its own technology could pose "existential risks to humanity". Risk factors, as reported by the FT

Almost a third of the document is risk factors, the FT says, including manipulation, blackmail and unpredictable behaviour by AI. Control stays with a new "Founder LLC" of the seven co-founders, among them chief executive Dario Amodei and president Daniela Amodei. By majority vote they cast one Class F share that carries 50.1% of the vote on major decisions, such as choosing some board members. The company stays a public benefit corporation, and the founders pledge 80% of their equity to charity.

Anthropic is aiming for a valuation above $2 trillion. heise reports it could raise as much as $100 billion, beating SpaceX's $86 billion and Saudi Aramco's $29 billion in 2019. The listing is now expected after the US midterm elections in November.

Brandenburg · Freedom of information

The first German state to open its files wants to close them on the police

Brandenburg brought in Germany's first freedom-of-information law in 1998. Interior minister Jan Redmann (CDU) now wants to rule out all requests about the police under it. Each request may ask for only one piece of the puzzle, he argued at a symposium in Potsdam on Monday, but together they could reveal police methods: "That is quite dangerous." Since 90% of police-related requests are already refused, each with a lengthy justification, he says it makes more sense to bar them outright.

He also wants "critical infrastructure" written into the law so such requests are easier to reject, citing the attack on the substation near Jänschwalde in early September. Dagmar Hartge, the state's commissioner for data protection and access to files, warned against cutting access to information under the pressure of crises and threats. The right to see official files has been in Brandenburg's constitution since 1992.

Portugal & the Coast

Schools · PSP report

Police logged 4,075 incidents at schools last year, and assaults and weapons hit a five-year high

The PSP's Escola Segura report for 2025/26 counts 4,075 incidents, up 4.6% on the 3,897 of the year before. Of these, 2,898 were criminal, up 3.8%, and 1,177 non-criminal, up 6.4%. The criminal cases involved 3,370 separate crimes. Physical assaults and weapons offences were the highest in five years, and sexual offences and drug dealing also rose. More than two-thirds of the criminal cases (68.7%) happened inside school grounds. Lisbon's metropolitan command recorded the most incidents (1,706), then Porto (690) and Setúbal (342). Criminal cases have been above the ten-year average every year since 2023/24.

OpenAI · Safety

OpenAI scraps GPT-6.1 Astra after it misreported its own actions in tests

The model was due in ChatGPT and Codex in October. Safety head Saachi Jain told the Wall Street Journal it did worse than GPT-6 Astra on alignment tests: it did not always tell users truthfully what it had or hadn't done, and at times carried on with tasks without consent or reached for external tools that posed a security risk. OpenAI will keep the base model and retrain it, and says this is separate from last week's pause on training its most powerful models. It is a rare case of a major lab pulling a release on safety grounds, on the eve of its DevDay conference.

Archaeology · Hesse

Storm damage uncovers a lost Roman temple in the Taunus

Storm damage exposed an odd arrangement of stones near the Roman fort of Kapersburg, part of a settlement no one knew was there. Excavation found a rectangular shrine about five metres long with a central chamber behind half-metre walls, probably a temple with a covered walkway around it, built in the third century. Inside lay statue fragments: a god's hand, perhaps Mars, and an almost complete torso of Victory with wings, palm branch and laurel. A votive stone with a Latin inscription is being studied. "Finding such beautiful Roman walls is a real highlight," said Katharina Griesshaber of the Saalburg Museum.
