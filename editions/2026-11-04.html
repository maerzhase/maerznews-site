United States · Midterm elections

Democrats take the Senate and the House: Texas, Kansas and five more Republican seats fall, and Trump says "I wasn't on the ballot"

Two years into his second term, the president loses both chambers of Congress in one night. He says he will accept the result, and blames the war with Iran "in part".

tagesschau · AP · Reuters · BBC · RTP · 4 November 2026, morning count

The Democrats won the Senate by taking seats the Republicans thought were safe. In Texas, James Talarico beat Ken Paxton, the attorney general Trump had backed, and became the first Democrat to win a statewide race there since 1994. Adam Hamilton won in Kansas, which last sent a Democrat to the Senate 94 years ago. Sherrod Brown is back in Ohio, Josh Turek won Iowa, Roy Cooper won North Carolina, and in South Carolina Annie Andrews beat Darline Graham Nordone, the sister of the late Lindsey Graham. Maine fell too. The Republicans held Wyoming, Florida, Kentucky and West Virginia.

In the House, where all 435 seats were up and the Republicans held 218 going in, AP and US networks project a Democratic majority. Democrats also took governor's mansions in Iowa and Texas, where Gina Hinojosa unseated Greg Abbott, and in Georgia Keisha Lance Bottoms becomes the first Black woman ever elected governor of a US state; in Florida the Republican Byron Donalds becomes the state's first Black governor.

"I wasn't on the ballot." Donald Trump on Truth Social, as the results came in

Trump, who had called the vote a referendum on his policies, told the New York Times he would accept the results, and later said the war with Iran was "partly" to blame. An AP survey found about three in four voters unhappy with the direction of the country; oil rose overnight on uncertainty over the war. With both chambers, Democrats can block legislation, run investigations and refuse appointments for the last two years of his term, though not override his veto.

Ovar · Rail

Too full to board: on Sunday nights, Ovar's students are left on the platform as the Porto–Lisbon train goes by

The Inter-Regional 820 leaves Porto-Campanhã for Lisbon-Santa Apolónia on Sunday evenings and is packed long before it reaches Ovar, OvarNews reports, following the Jornal de Notícias. It is the train university students take back to Coimbra for the week, and at Ovar some cannot get on at all; those who do stand in the corridors and by the doors. The students say it has got worse since term began in September.

"Two weeks ago we couldn't get in," says Pedro Xará, who catches the 20:21 at Ovar. Bruna Pinho blames the crowd that piles up at the entrances instead of moving down the carriages, which locks out each station further on. The fallback is the Intercidades half an hour later. CP says it is studying ways to add capacity on its busiest trains.

Berlin · Industry

Schaeffler will close its Berlin plants in Moabit and Spandau, 270 jobs, in a new round of 3,500 cuts

The car and industrial supplier from Herzogenaurach announced on Tuesday that it is shutting its Berlin site: the research and production works on Sickingenstraße in Moabit and on the Zitadellenweg in Spandau, where it develops electric motors and drive technology. 270 people work there; the company's office in Mitte stays. Across Europe Schaeffler wants to cut almost 3,500 jobs, 2,660 of them in Germany, and close Dortmund, Morbach and Schwalbach "in perspective" too.

The company blames the weak car business and Germany's high labour costs; it wants to save €300 million a year from 2030 at a one-off cost of about €480 million. About half the cuts fall on production, 30% on research and development. It is the third round since 2024. "A fatal signal for Germany as an industrial location," says IG Metall's Constantin Borchelt: "Whoever wants to shape the future of industry has to invest, not close."

Android · Botnet

Malware from the factory: Google takes down a botnet of 1.3 million Android TV boxes, and blames their maker

Google's Threat Intelligence Group says the x.cpe botnet was built into cheap Android TV boxes by their manufacturer, Videostrong, mostly sold under the MECOOL brand, and slipped through Google's certification. Once online, a stub app with system permissions phoned home, switched off Play Protect and installed whatever modules its operators chose: residential proxies to route other people's traffic, ad fraud, and full remote access, including screen capture and probing the home network.

With Japan's police, Synthient and Trend Micro, Google seized the domains and command servers. Trend Micro counts 1,472,554 infected devices, mostly in Brazil, Malaysia, Iraq, Mexico and Taiwan, but tens of thousands in Germany too. The boxes are safer now, not clean: Play Protect stays off and the modules can't easily be removed. Synthient lists 182 affected models.

Cinematography · Software

Rian Johnson wanted his iPhone to shoot like a film camera, so his cinematographer wrote the maths: Chromagraph

iPhone camera app · Steve Yedlin, Rian Johnson, Eric Cameron · out now

Steve Yedlin has shot every Rian Johnson film since "Brick", through "The Last Jedi" and the "Knives Out" series, and has spent 20 years on the science of colour. With Johnson and the colour-science coder Eric Cameron he has released Chromagraph, an iPhone app that captures RAW and skips Apple's own image processing, rendering photos with the look he builds for cinema cameras like the Arri Alexa.

It took ten intense weeks. A phone sensor is tiny and spreads its data differently, Yedlin tells IndieWire, and the pixel values alone don't say how bright something is meant to be; "it's totally different math for the most part". The app is a side project: the three are building software to replace dimmer boards and on-set screen playback with colour-tunable LEDs.

Berlin · Video

Seven dance troupes, one strobe-lit trance: Jeremy Shaw's "Phase Shifting Index" at the Hamburger Bahnhof

The Berlin-based Canadian artist's 35-minute installation films seven dance groups from different eras and traditions, each rehearsing in its own sphere around the gallery, until they lock together in one burst of movement and dissolve into coloured pixels. Hyperallergic likens the experience to being "baptized in a sea of sacramental strobe lights".

Shaw, born in North Vancouver in 1977 and shown at the Pompidou, MoMA PS1 and the 2017 Venice Biennale, was raised Catholic and gay and wanted a mystical experience as a child. His question: if you could explain empirically what a brain does in a spiritual experience, "would that validate the experience or discount it?"

Lisbon · Electronic

Niagara's "Catedral Gótica": the Lisbon trio swaps disco for skeletal machine minimalism and sequencer fugues

Electronic · Príncipe · album

Sara Eckerson and the brothers Alberto and António have been stalwarts of Lisbon's Príncipe label since the early 2010s, when their lo-fi, Italo-tinged house anticipated the daytime jams of the Boiler Room boom. The new album opens mid-show, on a techno pulse and a herky-jerky piano loop, but those first two tracks are fake-outs: most of it is frantic yet disembodied, with twisted sequencer fugues and lo-fi ambient passages.

Príncipe says the record goes back "seven or eight years", and it has the sweep of a career survey. It closes calmly with the fuzzed-out ambient "Rua do Século". By keeping their intentions hidden, Pitchfork writes, Niagara have kept an allure that "should have dissipated years ago".

Spain · Rail

Spain's transport minister Óscar Puente resigns six days after the Urda crash killed at least 69, a Portuguese man among them

Puente announced his resignation in Congress on Tuesday evening, at the end of a long session in which he set out new rail-safety measures and was grilled by the opposition. "A transport minister in a country like Spain cannot remain in office after two accidents of this seriousness in less than a year," he said: last Wednesday a high-speed train ran into the back of a stationary one near Urda, south of Madrid, and in January a crash on the same line killed 46.

At least 69 people died and dozens were injured, Spain's worst rail disaster since 2013 and one of the worst in modern Europe. The University of Porto's newsroom JPN reports that a Portuguese man is among the dead; Hyperallergic mourns Fernando Calvillo, a pillar of Oklahoma's art scene, who also died. The government has approved new safety measures.
