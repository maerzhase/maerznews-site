Porto · Lisbon · Housing

Police end the housing camps at dawn: Porto's packs up after ten nights, Lisbon's Rossio is cleared with batons and arrests

Hours after the interior minister said "the law is to be obeyed", the PSP moved on Praça D. João I and the Rossio at the same time. Porto's campers took their own tents down under an ultimatum; in Lisbon a human chain was broken by force and several people detained. The movement has already called the next rally for tonight.

RTP · Lusa · Observador · Público · Porto and Lisbon, 20 October 2026

In Porto, shortly before 06:00, the PSP gave the people still in Praça D. João I an ultimatum: remove the dozens of tents or face arrest. The campers formed a human chain at first, then negotiated a few minutes with the officer in charge and started carrying out tents, bags and banners themselves; the Corpo de Intervenção watched from the top of the square and did not go in. Some stayed on the steps at one side, and the movement said it would move its gear to the Aliados. On Monday night 35 people had voted in a popular assembly to stay, on what would have been the eleventh day of a protest that began on 10 October.

The Rossio went differently. After three warnings, the last of them declared final, more than 50 officers of the Unidade Especial de Polícia broke the human chain, cleared the tents and used batons when some campers threw objects; several people were detained and identified, and some activists accuse the officers of assault. About a hundred protesters regrouped in Praça da Figueira, sang "Grândola, Vila Morena" and agreed to disperse, with the police blocking the way back to their belongings.

"We will leave until we can come back tomorrow." A Porto camper to the PSP, as quoted by Observador

Interior minister Luís Neves had said on Monday that the right to protest is "respected by all" but "the law is to be obeyed", and that the PSP would not go beyond its powers. The President of the Republic asked the government and opposition for urgent housing measures and for unity; PS leader José Luís Carneiro called for authority to be reconciled with liberty. Observador reports a new rally called for the Rossio at 19:00 today, while Lusa heard Lisbon's campers promise to return on Wednesday; Porto's protest notice is filed until 31 December.

Aveiro · Port

Ship-breaking comes to the port of Aveiro: Navalria's recycling yard is on the last lap of licensing

The shipyard at the port, now part of a group controlled by businessman Mário Ferreira, wants to add the dismantling of ships to its building and repair work, and has its compliance report (RECAPE) out for public consultation until the end of October, the last step before a single environmental licence. The 2024 environmental impact statement from the CCDR Centro forced changes: the site shrinks by 1,485 m² to 19,537 m², with no new buildings, confined to the slipway, the dry dock and their support areas.

The yard could hold one ship of up to 2,000 tonnes in dry dock and one of 300 tonnes on the slipway at a time. Installed capacity is two 2,000-tonne and five 300-tonne ships a year, about 5,500 tonnes, but the declared maximum is 1,250 tonnes a year, roughly 5 tonnes a day on eight-hour shifts. A new storm-drain system and a 720 m² sealed, partly covered waste park are promised.

Berlin · Coalition

Berlin's SPD votes 26 to 3, the Greens all but unanimously: red-green-red talks can start once the Left says yes

The SPD's state executive backed coalition negotiations with the Left and the Greens in an online meeting on Monday evening, by 26 votes to three, after more than 40 speakers; the doubts raised were the socialisation of big landlords and whether the Left can solve its antisemitism problem. The Greens' small party congress followed with only two votes against among 45 delegates, one critic calling the Left an unreliable partner.

The Left, which won the election with 25.7%, decides at a party congress on 31 October, along with the three parties' joint line on antisemitism and organised crime; if it agrees, negotiations begin in early November, about six weeks after the vote. The coalition would hold 91 seats, 14 above a majority, and could make the Left's Elif Eralp the party's first Governing Mayor of Berlin. Its boldest plan, socialising some 160,000 flats from five companies, faces a federal government that decided in July to stop it, though no bill exists yet.

The PS will abstain and let the 2027 budget pass, while Chega now says it will sink the government's rent law

"By our deepest convictions this budget deserved a vote against, but our first duty is to serve the country," PS leader José Luís Carneiro said in Faro on Monday, opening a national tour on the budget. Security · Linux

PwnEscape: an AI found a way out of KVM virtual machines in three days, for under $700

The flaw, CVE-2026-99999 (CVSS 9.3), sits in the kernel's networking code: a guest can use virtio metadata to tell the host where to write a packet's checksum later, and the kernel checked that offset wrongly, so the write lands elsewhere in host memory. In the lab, pwn.ai's researchers broke out of the VM repeatedly and crashed hosts with every guest on them. On a cloud host, one customer could take over the hypervisor and its neighbours.

They call it a new bug class, "deferred-metadata trust inversion", a time-of-check bug with no race needed. They found it with GPT-5.6 Sol; the fix only reached the kernel on 8 October. Debian shipped patched kernels on Monday, Ubuntu and Red Hat had not yet, and Netcup has been rebooting its hosts since the weekend. Anyone running KVM should patch and reboot: working exploits are expected within weeks.

Sheffield · Farewell

"There will be no more Cabaret Voltaire": Mallinder and Watson on their last tour

Industrial · electronic · live album "But What Time Is It Really?"

Back together since Richard H. Kirk's death in 2021, Stephen Mallinder and Chris Watson are on what is almost certainly their final UK tour, after US dates. "We're not making new tracks," Watson insists; "we don't want to be a tribute band," says Mallinder, who frames it as "for Richard". Their reason to play at all is that songs like "Sex, Money, Freaks" and "The Crackdown" mean something again.

The Quietus traces the Burroughs-style cut-up from newspaper headlines over an EMS synth in Watson's attic, through "Nag Nag Nag", to Chicago house with Marshall Jefferson. The live band adds Benge, Finlay Shakespeare and video from their DoubleVision archive. "We don't want to romanticise the past," Mallinder says.
