Germany · Berlin after the election

SPD and Greens stop Berlin's coalition talks after the Left's MPs sign a declaration of their own

The Left's new group pledged itself to Israel's right to exist, but on the basis of its own party resolutions, not the joint paper agreed last week. The other two want to know whether that deal still holds.

rbb24 · tagesschau · Berlin, 13–14 October 2026

Berlin's three-way exploratory talks were suspended on Tuesday evening. At its constituent meeting that afternoon, the Left's new group in the Abgeordnetenhaus had every member sign a declaration that includes a commitment to Israel's right to exist. The trouble for its partners is the footnote: the text names the Left's own party-congress resolutions as its basis, not the joint red-green-red paper on fighting antisemitism that the three leaderships agreed last week, and which has been attacked inside the Left ever since.

"We have noted that the Left has reached other arrangements in its group," the SPD and Greens said in a joint statement. "That means we must interrupt the talks at this point to assess whether our agreement of last week still applies." They expect the Left "to make clear how far it means to take seriously and implement" what was agreed, and turned down rbb's interview requests. How close the talks are to collapse was unclear overnight.

"From our point of view, last week's agreement stands. Why this step now, I cannot understand." Tobias Schulze, Left co-group leader, in the rbb24 Abendschau

Schulze said the group's declaration was in parts word for word the same as the joint paper. The same evening the group, led by Elif Eralp and Schulze, admitted the contested Neukölln member Vanessa Emde after a long internal debate; she too signed. Emde has made remarks that critics read as antisemitic, which she denies, and on Saturday she joined a "United4Palestine" march.

Pressure had been building from outside: on Monday Chancellor Friedrich Merz told Berlin's SPD and Greens to end the talks and called it a "radical-left party", which Eralp rejected as meddling. The Left has already postponed its state congress to 31 October, when members were to vote on moving to coalition negotiations. The fallback rbb names, CDU, Greens and SPD, would hold just two seats more than a majority.

Oliveira de Azeméis · Industry

Schmidt Light Metal closes, and 318 workers lose their jobs

The historic car-components foundry in Oliveira de Azeméis, a neighbouring concelho to Ovar, is shutting down its production. It was declared insolvent last year and bought in March for €8.5 million by its own managers, but seven months on the order book ran too thin. Workers were warned of the collective dismissal in August, all labour processes stopped on 30 September, and the administration has said the plant will be closed by the end of the year; Notícias de Aveiro expects the 318 staff to be out of work in early December.

Berlin · Budget

Berlin spends €5 billion a year more than it takes in, and its reserves run out next year

Presenting the outgoing CDU-SPD Senate's financial plan to 2030, finance senator Stefan Evers announced a hard savings course for whoever governs next. The city's gap is about €5 billion, over 11% of the budget; its debt stands at around €69 billion and interest alone costs more than €1 billion this year. Within three years Evers expects interest above €2 billion and total debt near €84 billion. Transfer payments such as basic income support and housing benefit take about €10 billion a year, a quarter of adjusted spending, and the constitutional court's ruling on civil servants' pay means back-payments of at least €1.2 billion to 2020 alone. Spending has to fall in 2027 and 2028, he says; otherwise the federal-state stability council could force EU debt rules on Berlin, letting states like Bavaria and Hesse have a say in what the capital may afford.

Economy · Prices

Inflation climbs to 3.6%, its highest in three years, on fuel

Consumer prices in September were 3.6% higher than a year earlier, the national statistics institute INE confirmed on Tuesday, up from 3.3% in August and the steepest rise since September 2023. Fuels and other energy products, up more than 15% on the year, did most of the work: in the month, according to the energy directorate DGEG, unleaded 95 rose 6.2% to about €2.07 a litre and diesel 5.1% to €2.13. Core inflation, without energy and unprocessed food, was 2.7%. The figure lands as the government argues over the 2027 budget, and the same day it announced that IRS withholding will be cut by half in November to pass on the mid-year income-tax reduction.

Web · Images

Firefox 158 renders JPEG XL, and with Chrome 155 the format is in both big engines

Mozilla's new release displays JPEG XL images, which it says usually compress better than WebP, JPEG, PNG and GIF. Chrome 155 already supports the format by default, so a <picture> source in JXL now reaches far more readers than a year ago. Firefox also stops rounding scroll positions to whole screen pixels, for smoother scrolling on high-density displays and zoomed pages, writes more accessible PDFs when saving or printing on Linux and macOS, and fixes PDFs that dropped maths symbols. Its "Smart Window" starts suggesting form entries from open tabs and saved autofill data, rolled out gradually. Mozilla has also launched Firefox Enterprise, with a central admin console, first in Germany, Austria, Switzerland and France.

Arts
Paris · Opening

Le Large opens on Île Seguin, where Renault built cars

Paris's newest contemporary art centre, at the end of Métro line 9 in Boulogne-Billancourt, is part of the €450 million Pointe des Arts on the island Renault's factory filled for most of the 20th century. RCR Arquitectes' building widens from 500 m² at its base to 1,500 m² on the fifth floor under copper-coloured aluminium scales. There is no collection: collector Laurent Dumas's centre runs on about €5 million a year, with 40% of its public space free. Cecilia Alemani's opening show "Moteur Imaginaire" follows the car "from the factory floor into the algorithm": over 100 works by 55 artists from 23 countries, from Renault's commissions to Dubuffet and Tinguely, plus 17 new pieces such as Clément Cogitore's film on the 1947 strike.

Nuno Lobo will turn the Berlin Philharmonie's foyer into a Bosch forest

Contemporary classical · premiere Wed 21 Oct, 13:00 · Philharmonie Berlin

The 30-year-old Portuguese composer, who teaches at the Amsterdam Conservatory, premieres "Leshy", a piece built on the right-hand panel of Hieronymus Bosch's Garden of Earthly Delights in which the audience takes part. Saxophonist Aina Font and percussionist Agostinho Sequeira play, and Lobo stands among the listeners giving them their cues. The work is meant to be recorded for an album linked to Bosch's painting; the hard part, he told Lusa, was writing one piece for a picture with so many details.

Vatican · Health

Pope Leo has lung cancer; surgeons removed the lower lobe of his right lung

The Vatican confirmed on Tuesday night that the lump found in a routine check was a lung adenocarcinoma. The 71-year-old had a 90-minute operation at Rome's Gemelli hospital, in which the whole lower lobe of the right lung was removed "to ensure the procedure was radical"; there were no complications, and he woke normally.
