Ovar · Cybersecurity · Local government

A cyberattack takes Ovar's town hall offline: counters, e-mail and phones down until Wednesday

The council's computer system was left "inoperable" by an attack over the weekend. Eight public services, the museums and the arts centre among them, stay shut through 21 October, and every administrative deadline is suspended. Nobody yet knows how deep the damage goes, or whether residents' data were reached.

OvarNews · Notícias de Aveiro · Jornal de Notícias · Ovar, 18 October 2026

The Câmara said in a statement on Sunday that it had been the target of an "ataque informático" during the weekend that left its IT system inoperable. The incident has been reported to the competent authorities and response procedures are running: a technical team is containing the attack, assessing the impact and restoring systems, with the aim of getting services back "as quickly as possible". It has not said what kind of attack it was, and neither the extent of the damage nor whether anyone got at residents' data is yet known.

The practical effect lands on anyone with business at the town hall this week. Closed to the public through Wednesday, 21 October, inclusive: the front desk, planning, the Ecocentro, the veterinary service, the BUPi land-registry desk, the Centro de Arte, the municipal museums and the Escola de Artes e Ofícios. The council's online services, the Ecolinha, its e-mail addresses and its landline numbers are down too.

"The Municipality of Ovar will suspend all the deadlines of administrative procedures under way, so that no resident is harmed." Câmara Municipal de Ovar, statement

Still open: the Espaço Cidadão, the market, the municipal pool, the cemetery, the library and the Espaço Empreendedor, plus the SIGA platform for school services and the planning phone line. Updates will come through the council's official social-media accounts, the only channel it has left. The news ran from JN to Correio da Manhã and SAPO by Sunday evening. For the diary below: the arts centre, museum and school of arts host three of this week's events from Thursday, the day after the announced closure ends, so check before setting out.

Aveiro · Heritage

Aveiro will build a museum of salt beside the Canal de São Roque, "not a little show with half a dozen posters"

Mayor Luís Souto de Miranda told the Diário de Aveiro the project will be devoted to salt and to the salt pans of the Ria de Aveiro, and is meant to draw more visitors to the city. "At this moment we don't yet have the maturity to talk about what it will cost, but it is something ambitious that we are going to start developing," he said. The site is the canal-side area where, he noted, the new lake is almost finished and other projects are under way: "we have to bring it added value".

He spoke at the opening of "Astutas – Salinas do século XXI", a show on the twenty-first-century salt pans in the gallery of the old Capitania do Porto building on Avenida Dr. Lourenço Peixinho. It is free and runs until 11 December, Tuesday to Sunday, 10:00–12:30 and 13:30–18:00.

Berlin · Coalition

Decision day: Berlin's SPD board and a Green party congress vote tonight on formal talks with the Left

Two days after the three parties signed their exploratory paper, the SPD's state executive meets online at 16:30 to judge the result and decide whether to enter coalition negotiations with the Left and the Greens. The Greens follow at 20:00 with a small party congress. The Left, which came first in the election, will not vote until a party congress on 31 October, after Berlin's autumn holidays.

The paper's headline items are the socialisation of housing companies with more than 10,000 flats, more cycle lanes and a cheaper social ticket. A red-green-red coalition would have 91 seats, 14 more than a majority; the alternative, CDU, Greens and SPD, would clear it by only two. Not everyone in the SPD is sold: former minister Hubertus Heil and other Bundestag members voiced doubts on ARD's "Bericht aus Berlin", while CSU leader Markus Söder told the Junge Union the Union needs a "firewall to the left".

Porto · Lisbon · Housing

Told by police they are now committing a crime, Porto's campers vote to stay, and extend their protest to 31 December

At about 01:30 on Monday a PSP officer warned the Praça D. João I camp, as a colleague had in Lisbon's Rossio an hour earlier, that past the legal hour they were committing the crime of disobedience and the tents had to go. After a vote the majority stayed. "This movement will go on," said spokeswoman Catarina Borges. On Sunday night the movement had already filed a new protest notice running until the end of the year; Porto is on its tenth day, Lisbon its seventh.

The camp has invited Mayor Pedro Duarte to come and talk in the square, asking that any meeting request reach its official e-mail rather than individuals. The Ombudsman, Luísa Neto, has asked the PSP for urgent explanations of last week's police charge. In Lisbon, a second night passed calmly; the PSP said there was "no will" for repression. PS leader José Luís Carneiro sent the prime minister four more housing measures, and Chega proposed an "emergency package".

Porto · Serralves

Stones, steel and a single brushstroke: Lee Ufan's first major show in Portugal fills the Casa de Serralves

"Fragmentos do Infinito" brings the South Korean artist, born in 1936, to the house and park: three outdoor sculptures pairing boulders with steel, three more inside the Art Deco house, and six paintings, one made for the site. Lee was the leading theorist of Japan's Mono-ha, the late-1960s "School of Things" that set raw stone, wood, metal and glass in plain juxtapositions, and is linked to Korea's monochrome Dansaekhwa painting.

His "Relatum" works treat a stone as condensed time and make the space between objects the subject. Público's profile calls him "the artist who likes not painting and not doing". Curated by Philippe Vergne, to March 2027; on 1 November the ensemble Ars Ad Hoc plays Takemitsu, Catherine Lamb and others among the works.

France · Unrest

The final count for France's Saturday: 2,397 arrests and 997 police hurt, and Macron keeps his interior minister

The interior ministry's tally of the cost-of-living protests is double the first figures: 2,397 people detained and 997 police and gendarmes injured, seven seriously. It counted 550,000 marchers; the organisers say 800,000. Some 1,500 shops and businesses were hit, with damage in the hundreds of millions of euros, and there were fresh clashes on Sunday.

In an address on Sunday night Macron spoke of "anger and shame" and promised that those who destroyed would pay. On Monday the Élysée said he had turned down his interior minister's offer to resign and asked for "new tactics" against looting and barricades. The euro weakened against the dollar in early Asian trading.
