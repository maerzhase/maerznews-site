Brazil decides · Counted in Portugal too

Bolsonaro's son wins Brazil's first round, but Lisbon and Porto vote for Lula

Flávio Bolsonaro leads President Lula 47% to 45% and the two meet again on 25 October. A record 134,797 Brazilians registered to vote in Portugal, and in Lisbon and Porto they backed Lula by about fifteen points.

RTP · Lusa · TSE · Observador · BBC · 5 October

With almost every ballot counted, Flávio Bolsonaro, 45, the eldest son of the jailed former president Jair Bolsonaro, had 47.03% of the vote to 45.16% for Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, 80, who is seeking a fourth term. Polls had shown Lula slightly ahead. Neither reached 50%, so they face a run-off on 25 October. Observador puts Lula's gap at about 2.3 million votes, his weakest first round since 2002. Turnout was 78.9% of more than 158 million registered voters.

Bolsonaro's Liberal Party won 19 of the 54 Senate seats up for election. With nine senators already sitting, it will hold 28 of 81, the largest group. Michelle Bolsonaro was elected senator for Brasília with a record vote, and two of Jair's sons and his brother won seats as well. Flávio has promised an amnesty for his father, who is serving 27 years for plotting a coup, and for the rioters of 8 January 2023.

"I admit I was convinced I would win in the first round. We are in extra time." Lula da Silva, Sunday night

In Portugal the count went the other way. In the Lisbon consular area, which had the most voters, Lula took 14,948 votes (53.51%) to Bolsonaro's 10,548 (37.76%). In Porto he won by 10,356 to 7,559. Only the small Faro consulate went to Bolsonaro, by 1,305 votes to 1,097. Lisbon had about 79,000 registered voters, Porto more than 47,000 and Faro close to 9,000. Portugal is the second-largest polling area outside Brazil, after the United States.

Most of them stayed home. Voting is compulsory in Brazil for literate adults from 18 to 70, but abstention in Portugal reached 62%, against 21% in Brazil itself. Voters abroad choose only the president and vice-president, so they will be back at the polling stations on 25 October.

Coalition · Working hours

SPD members draw their red line on the working-hours reform: no weekly limit without a union deal

German law sets an eight-hour working day, longer only in exceptional cases. On page 18 of their coalition agreement, the Union and the SPD promised to allow a weekly maximum instead. The CDU and CSU want employers and staff to share out the hours within that week as they choose. Talks resume this week, and four of the SPD's largest member groups have now set out conditions in a seven-page paper titled "Self-determined, not deregulated". The SPD women, the labour working group, the Jusos and the "60plus" group all signed it.

They accept a weekly limit only where a collective agreement covers it, a point they say no law may drop. Roughly half of German employees work under one. The paper wants an average cap of 40 hours a week and paid overtime premiums from the first extra hour. It asks for every employee's time to be recorded electronically, for working hours to be set only by mutual agreement, and for sectors prone to undeclared work to be left out. Labour minister Bärbel Bas tried almost the same union-deal rule in a draft in June and backed down after business groups and the CDU objected.

Cars · September

Nearly half the new cars sold in Portugal in September were battery-electric

Portugal sold 19,589 new passenger cars in September, 15.2% more than a year earlier, the car industry association ACAP reports. 47.7% of them ran on batteries alone. Observador puts much of the growth down to electric models and suggests that rising fuel prices played a part. Light commercial vehicles grew 4.7% and heavy vehicles 3.9%. From January to September, 188,912 passenger cars were sold, up 10.1%. Over the same period, light commercials rose 9.2% to 25,133 and heavy vehicles jumped 33.8% to 6,881.

Data centres · Disclosure

Google blacked out its data centres' water use, but you could copy and paste it

Nebraska requires data centres to report their electricity and water use each year, and Google marked the figures for all three of its sites as trade secrets. But reporters at 10/11 NOW in Lincoln found the redaction boxes were only drawn on top of the text. Highlighting them and pasting into another document revealed the numbers. The Lincoln site ("Agate LLC") drew 52.65 MW at peak and used 13.3 million US gallons of water last year, about 50,000 m³. The Papillion site used 547.88 million gallons in 2025, about 2.1 million m³. Together, the six data centres that have reported so far used 765 million gallons. The hidden boxes also showed the 2025 tax refunds Google's three sites expect: $55.8m, $39.2m and $22.6m.

Provenance · Restitution

Switzerland pays its museums to find out where their collections came from

The Swiss Federal Office of Culture is putting $2.4 million into 26 provenance projects for 2027 and 2028, picked after a public call. The focus is on objects acquired in colonial, archaeological and Nazi-era contexts. Basel's Natural History Museum and Gadjah Mada University in Indonesia get $120,000 to trace 189 Indonesian human remains in the museum's collection, how they were acquired and which communities they may come from, as groundwork for talks about restitution. Nine museums, among them the Museum der Kulturen Basel and the Bern Historical Museum, share CHF 100,000 (about $120,000) to study more than 6,300 objects from Cameroon. The Kunstmuseum Basel will re-examine 336 drawings whose provenance has been flagged, along with what happened to their sellers and how the museum collected between 1933 and 1945.

Birthday · The Quietus

Steve Reich turns 90, and the pop world still samples him

Minimalism · composer · 90 on 3 October

Reich and Philip Glass met at Juilliard in the late 1950s and founded minimalism, along with a New York removals firm, Chelsea Light Moving. Reich's reach goes far beyond concert halls. The Orb built "Little Fluffy Clouds" (1990) on his Electric Counterpoint, which led to the Reich Remixed album four years later. Madvillain sampled his tape piece "Come Out" for "America's Most Blunted", and Brian Eno credits Reich's early tape loops as an influence. The Quietus also lists Talking Heads, Aphex Twin, Jonny Greenwood and Max Richter as heirs. Its birthday piece picks ten records as ways into six decades of work.
