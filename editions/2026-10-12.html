Germany · Mecklenburg-Vorpommern

With the AfD on 32 seats and the CDU gone, SPD, Left and Greens start writing Schwerin's first three-party coalition

Talks open today in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern. Between them the three parties hold 39 of the Landtag's 71 seats, three more than a majority, and they have five weeks to agree a deal.

NDR · Michael Seidel · Landtag election of 20 September 2026

On 20 September the AfD became the strongest party in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern with 38.2% of the vote, ahead of Manuela Schwesig's SPD on 35.5%. It won 32 seats, 18 more than in 2021, but that is short of the 36 needed to govern the 71-seat Landtag alone, and every other party has refused to work with it. The CDU, which governed the state with the SPD until 2021, did not get back in at all; its state leader Daniel Peters and general secretary Katy Hoffmeister resigned the day after.

That leaves one combination. The SPD's 29 seats plus five each for the Left and the Greens make 39. After two weeks of exploratory talks behind closed doors the three agreed a paper with detailed outlines for a coalition treaty, and the Greens cleared the way at a small party congress in Rostock. Seven working groups begin today. Their plans: citizens' assemblies, including one attached to the premier, and "consultative referendums"; a parliamentary inquiry into far-right networks and their links to parliament; an expert commission on crisis preparedness; a state development bank; digitisation to cut red tape; and keeping every hospital site open.

"When the AfD comes to power, chaos and uncertainty threaten. We have the chance to do it differently." Manuela Schwesig, SPD

The clock is short. The new Landtag meets on 20 October, and the state constitution then allows four weeks to elect a premier, so by 17 November at the latest. By then the treaty must be finished and approved by party congresses of all three. Schwesig pointed to Saxony-Anhalt, where an AfD speaker was elected last week, as the alternative. The AfD, meanwhile, has already announced its own inquiry, into the state's Covid measures, and its lead candidate Enrico Schult predicts an absolute majority next time if the coalition lasts.

The Left's leader Hennis Herbst promises "a different emphasis from the federal government": no social cuts, more student and apprentice housing. The Greens' Claudia Müller calls the result a chance to govern "on an equal footing". In Berlin, a similar three-way deal is being tested by an angry Left base (below).

Castro Electrónica's owner walks free on €100,000 bail in the "VAT carousel" case

Filipe Castro, owner of the Ovar electronics firm, has 15 days to pay €100,000 bail in Operação Hermes, must report weekly to the police and may not contact three other suspects. Berlin · Coalition talks

An angry base demands a party congress to tear up the Left's antisemitism pact

At Saturday's "United4Palestine" march in Berlin, many of the loudest voices were the election winner's own supporters, protesting against their party's leadership. "Grow a backbone," one called; placards read "No coalition on our backs". The party's Berlin working group on Palestine solidarity, its youth wing and the "Linke in der Linken" current have now written to the executive: the agreement on fighting antisemitism signed with the Greens and SPD, they say, trivialises antisemitism, censors criticism of the Israeli government and breaks party decisions, and must be rejected at a party congress, which would end red-green-red before it starts. The newly elected Neukölln deputy Vanessa Emde, whose possible expulsion from the group is under discussion, shared the letter. Co-leader Kerstin Wolter calls the Left "a large, plural party" and says the national leadership backs her; on Sunday the talks turned, by rbb's account, to socialising the big housing companies.

Lisbon · Politics

Passos Coelho says he will consider a comeback if the PSD government fails

The former prime minister told SIC Notícias he is "not a candidate for anything" for now, but would "weigh it up and take the initiative" if the PSD visibly could not deliver what the country needs. He insisted the party "had an obligation" to seek a four-year deal with Chega and the Liberals, which together won more than 60% of the vote, and called governing by ad-hoc deals with the PS one day and Chega the next "an immense political trap" that blocks deeper reform. He credited André Ventura with "considerable merit", while criticising Chega's plan to lower the retirement age: the longer Chega is kept out of any solution, he argued, the less it is tested and the likelier it is to win. Observador reads the interview as Passos declaring himself a proto-candidate for prime minister.

Infrastructure · War

Ukraine's third strike in four days takes a Yandex data centre fully offline

A drone attack damaged Yandex's data centre in Vladimir, more than 170 km east of Moscow, and the company says it has shut it down completely, with some services unreachable and no one hurt. On Friday a site in the Kaluga region was hit; the night before, a fire at a Yandex site in Ryazan caused large outages in its transport and e-commerce services. Yandex runs Russia's most-used search engine plus taxi and food-delivery apps used by millions, and users across Russia and abroad report problems. A Cloudflare Radar chart shared on Hacker News shows bot traffic from Yandex's network down about 99% over the week. Russia, for its part, has been striking Ukrainian data centres for weeks, Odesa among them.

Madrid · Review

Felix Gonzalez-Torres returns to the city that took him in as a child refugee

"Sweet Revenge" at the Museo Reina Sofía is his first big solo show in Madrid, where he lived briefly as a Cuban refugee in 1971. More than 50 works fill seven galleries of the Sabatini building: candy spills, paper stacks, beaded curtains, puzzles and text portraits, hung without chronology. Curators Alejandro Cesarco and Nancy Spector, who made his 1995 Guggenheim retrospective, took the title from the blue candy spill "Untitled" (Revenge), made after he came back as an adult. Hyperallergic finds the show more interested in how the works function than in what he was fighting for, and misses many of the most political pieces.
