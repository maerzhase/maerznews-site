Berlin · Forming a government

Berlin's Left, Greens and SPD open coalition talks after signing a line on antisemitism

Two and a half weeks after the election, the three parties have written down what they expect of their members. Whether the new Left group signs up to it is next week's test.

rbb24 · tagesschau · Berlin · announced Thursday 8 October

Die Linke, the Greens and the SPD announced on Thursday that their pre-talks were over and exploratory talks on a government would start straight away. The condition was an eight-point paper on antisemitism, which the Greens and the SPD had demanded after a series of incidents in the Left party. It commits a future senate to protecting Jewish life in Berlin, calls Israel's right to exist "non-negotiable", condemns the Hamas massacre of 7 October 2023 and promises to name "Israel-related antisemitism" clearly.

It also sets a procedure. Suspected antisemitic conduct in any of the three parliamentary groups is to be handled jointly, with a three-person expert panel to assess it; the group concerned then decides, with consequences up to expulsion from the group or the party. A second paper makes the same threat over organised crime: even making or returning contact with people linked to it counts as a serious breach. The three also want the state constitution to protect against antisemitism, antiziganism and racism.

"If we form a government, we give a promise of protection to all Jewish people." Werner Graf, Green group leader

Left lead candidate Elif Eralp is ill, so party co-chair Kerstin Wolter presented the deal; the SPD's Steffen Krach, rbb noted, stood stone-faced through much of it. The German-Israeli Society's Berlin branch called the paper a "formula compromise" the SPD and Greens should be ashamed of, and Green federal leader Franziska Brantner said paper was not enough: there had to be personnel consequences.

The pressure point is Vanessa "Vedi" Emde, newly elected for the Left in Neukölln, who is accused of antisemitism for saying "states have no right to exist". Wolter says the rules apply once the groups constitute; the Left's does so next week, and its Neukölln branch says it expects Emde to be part of it. Exploratory talks are not yet coalition negotiations, but the arithmetic is comfortable: 91 of 153 seats.

Cortegaça · Buçaquinho

Papers show Ovar's council asked for the Buçaquinho trees to be cut, says +Pinhal

The local pine-forest association +Pinhal has published part of the paperwork behind May's felling of 480 trees at the Parque de Merendas do Buçaquinho and along the Vala de Maceda. It starts with a letter from the Câmara de Ovar's forestry office to the regional director of the ICNF on 11 July 2025, asking for the "urgent sale and felling of all the trees" in the picnic park, citing the safety of people and property and trees leaning over paths and the watercourse. In May the council had said the felling was the ICNF's decision and that neither it nor the Junta de Freguesia de Cortegaça could have stopped it. +Pinhal says there is still no tree-by-tree assessment of which were dangerous; among those cut were four eucalyptus and nine large pines more than 60 cm across. It asks why pruning, crown reduction or removing only the risky trees was never considered. The timber went to public auction.

Economy · Forecast

Berlin now expects 1.3% growth this year, up from 0.5% in April

Katherina Reiche's economy ministry more than doubled its 2026 forecast on Thursday, matching the leading institutes' autumn projection; the Ifo institute is at 1.4%. The Iran war and the jump in energy prices had weighed heavily at first, the ministry said, but the economy proved more resilient than expected, carried by exports, public investment and higher defence spending. "We have left the trough behind us; the German economy is growing again," Reiche said, calling it only a beginning. The 2027 forecast rises from 0.9% to 1.1%, but the ministry pencils in only 0.6% for 2028 and says a lasting end to the conflicts in the Middle East and Ukraine, with cheaper energy, would speed the recovery. On the markets the mood was flatter: the DAX closed below 25,000 points on Thursday on high rates and oil prices.

Portugal & the Coast

Lisbon · Budget 2027

The budget unfreezes university fees and ties them to inflation

The 2027 budget, handed in on Thursday, lets undergraduate fees at public universities rise with inflation from the next academic year. They have been frozen at €697 a year since 2020; the government tried the same indexation last year, and parliament voted it down in November. Health gets a smaller budget than in 2026, Antena 1 reports, which hospital administrators call a worry. Elsewhere in the 2027 proposal: income-tax brackets are updated and corporate tax cut; taxes on tobacco and alcohol should bring in €166 million more, €2.256 billion in all; and the social-security pension system is projected to close 2027 with a surplus of almost €7 billion, 2.1% of GDP, before structural deficits appear in the 2040s. The prime minister faces MPs in the fortnightly debate this morning.

Web · TLS

Let's Encrypt certificates will last 64 days from February

The free certificate authority cuts lifetimes from 90 to 64 days on 10 February 2027, and plans 45-day defaults in 2028. Opt-in test certificates start on 14 October. Clients that use ARI (ACME Renewal Information), where the CA tells the client when to renew, should not notice. Setups with fixed schedules will: Let's Encrypt says to grep cron jobs and runbooks for the old renewal points, 83, 80 and 60 days, and renew at two-thirds of the lifetime instead. Shorter certificates limit the damage of a stolen key or a mis-issued certificate, and push the last manual renewals into automation.

Making Marie Antoinette (2026): Eleanor Coppola's footage of her daughter at Versailles

Documentary · US cinemas 23 Oct · MUBI 6 Nov · RT 100%

In 2005 Sofia Coppola shot Marie Antoinette inside the Palace of Versailles, the first production ever allowed to, and her mother, the documentarian Eleanor Coppola, filmed about 80 hours on set. Twenty years on, and as her mother wished, Sofia has edited it into a film, with Eleanor's journals read in voiceover. It shows costume designer Milena Canonero at work and what it meant to be a young woman directing a period epic; The Film Stage calls it one of the year's great time capsules. After its New York Film Festival premiere it opens in US cinemas on 23 October with a 4K release of the 2006 film, and on MUBI on 6 November. Too few IMDb votes for a rating yet.

Literature · Nobel

Anne Carson, poet of the classics, wins the Nobel Prize in Literature

The Swedish Academy honoured the 76-year-old Canadian for a "bold and inventive" body of work that, "in playful dialogue with the classical tradition, has created new forms for contemporary literature". Born in Toronto in 1950 and trained as a classicist, Carson mixes poetry, essay, translation, criticism and drama: her first major book, Eros the Bittersweet (1986), was a lyric essay on desire, and her work sets Socrates or Antigone in film scenes or puts the rhetorician Longinus in conversation with Antonioni. The Academy's secretary reached her in Iceland, "listening to the wind"; she was, Ingrid Carlberg said, very surprised.
