Security · PHP

php2shell: two 20-year-old PHP flaws found with AI knock government sites offline, and Berlin's police stations are still dark

The patches came out on Wednesday afternoon. By Thursday morning the online police stations of Berlin and Brandenburg, brandenburg.de, the Abgeordnetenhaus and the BKA were still unreachable, after the BSI told anyone who could not update at once to pull their site off the net. Cloudflare says the attacks have started.

rbb24 · heise · The Verge · PHP Foundation · 22 October 2026

The PHP project shipped versions 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 and 8.2.35 on Wednesday to close two holes that, under the right conditions, let an attacker run code on a server without logging in. Both sit in parts of the language that almost every request touches: CVE-2026-109618 in memory management, where an out-of-memory error leaves half-initialised data that the clean-up then trusts, and CVE-2026-109619 in error handling, where state from one request survives into the next. Together they are called php2shell. Every release of the past two decades is affected; PHP 8.1 and older, already out of support, get no fix at all.

Exploitation is not instant. heise says it can take thousands to tens of thousands of HTTP requests, which is exactly what makes it suited to automated mass attacks; the discoverers at Calif show system commands running on a Laravel server after 38 seconds. The German BSI issued a level-3 warning, "extraordinarily high"; the NSA and the UK's NCSC told organisations to go offline if they could not update immediately. Dutch and Finnish agencies, 4chan, DeviantArt and parts of Wikipedia were among those hit on Wednesday, and in Berlin the Charité, customs, the BND and much of the city administration briefly took their sites down; those are back. Still offline this morning, rbb reports: both police forces' Internetwachen, brandenburg.de, parlament-berlin.de, the BKA, the city of Cottbus, Dahme-Spreewald district, some Berlin local courts and the Berliner Tafel.

"We consider this one of the most impactful security issues in recent memory." OpenAI, one of the teams that reported the flaws

The PHP Foundation's own account is unusually frank. The memory bug was first spotted in 2024 while fixing findings from an external audit; an exploit was "theorized", but the small security team could not agree on a fix that did not break compatibility, and the work stalled for months, "a structural failure", it writes. Then on 11 August a researcher at Calif, auditing PHP under OpenAI's "Patch the Planet" programme, and OpenAI itself independently reported working remote code execution; Wiz followed on 24 August. AI did the heavy lifting on both sides: Wiz says the exploit chain was once "at the edge of what human researchers could practically achieve", and the same tools now make it easy to rebuild from the patches. Not everyone is panicking: a widely shared post on Hacker News argues there is no easy exploit yet. Anyone running PHP, down to a small blog on shared hosting, should check the version today.

Ovar · Cyberattack

Ovar's town hall reopens its counters today, but staff will work without their computer systems

From this Thursday every municipal service that closed on Monday after the cyberattack reopens, the Câmara says, except the BUPI land-registry desk and the Ecolinha. Face-to-face service resumes at the front desk in the Paços do Concelho and in the other municipal offices, though without access to the council's IT systems and applications, so expect paper and patience.

The general phone line and the council's e-mail addresses remain unavailable. The town hall calls the arrangements "temporary and exceptional" and says it is working to recover all its systems as quickly as possible; it has still not said what, if anything, was taken, after warning on Tuesday that residents' personal data may be involved.

Brandenburg · Housing

While Berlin fights over flats, one in five stands empty in Spree-Neiße, and more than a third in Forst

New figures from the BBU, the Berlin-Brandenburg housing companies' federation, put vacancy in the Spree-Neiße district at around 20%, the highest of any district in the state. Forst on the Neisse reaches 35.8%, and Guben is also badly hit; the local housing companies blame the shrinking population. The rest of Lusatia is above the state average of 7% too: Oberspreewald-Lausitz 13.1%, Elbe-Elster 11.8%, with only the Prignitz, at 13.1%, comparable.

Around the capital the picture flips. Potsdam-Mittelmark has just 1.7% of its flats empty, Dahme-Spreewald 3.1%, and Berlin itself 1.6%, the city where a would-be red-green-red coalition has just agreed to socialise the biggest private landlords. Two hours down the line, the problem is not too few homes but too few people to live in them.

Housing · Protests

Lisbon's camp votes to drop its tents and sleep in the open, and the PSP lets the "aconchada" stay in the Rossio

Back in the square on Wednesday evening, the Lisbon movement's popular assembly voted to take down the dozens of tents it had pitched and spend the night in the open instead, huddled "em conchinha", so it could remain without defying the ban; the "acampada" became an "aconchada". In Porto, Praça D. The PSD's Hugo Soares says the protest has a "partisan motivation".

Privacy · Courts

New Mexico orders Meta to pay $25 billion for years of lying about Facebook user data

A judge set the penalty, Meta's largest ever, after a two-week trial that found the company misled the public about how it handled user data in the Cambridge Analytica era, and about how it enforced its own rules on hate speech. The lies, the judge said, go to "the heart of Facebook's core business practices". The state argued all of its roughly two million residents were affected; heise works it out at about €10,600 a head.

Meta will appeal; its chief lawyer says the ruling makes it "all but impossible to do business" in New Mexico. It barely dented the quarter: the same day Meta reported its net profit had tripled, said it will spend at least $135 billion on AI, and announced a new class of non-voting shares to pay for it, which keeps Mark Zuckerberg firmly in control.

Ukraine · Diplomacy

Putin says yes "in principle" to resuming three-way peace talks, and hours later missiles hit Kyiv

After meeting Trump's envoy Steve Witkoff in St Petersburg on Wednesday, the Kremlin said it was ready to restart the talks with the US and Ukraine that broke off in February, since when Russia has stepped up its strikes as Ukraine ran short of Western interceptors. Trump said the world was "maybe now the closest we've been" to ending the war; Zelensky hopes for new talks by early November.

Witkoff and Jared Kushner had seen Zelensky and the Norwegian and Finnish leaders in Oslo the day before, discussing security guarantees and an energy ceasefire; Putin's aide Yuri Ushakov called those ideas not very "useful". In Washington, some Republicans are now openly criticising Trump's warmth towards Putin.
