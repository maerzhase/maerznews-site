Technology · Chips · Leadership

Jensen Huang quits Nvidia with immediate effect: the stock opens 13% down, claws most of it back, and Congress wants him before Thanksgiving

The co-founder who turned a graphics-card maker into the engine of the AI boom stepped down on Thursday after admitting a relationship with a woman US officials regard as a Chinese intelligence asset. Chief financial officer Colette Kress runs the company for now, with quarterly results due on Tuesday.

tagesschau · Reuters · BBC · Wired · Gotrade (Yahoo Finance and Nasdaq data) · as read at 06:30 on 13 November 2026

Nvidia said on Thursday that Huang had resigned as president and chief executive, effective at once, and that the board would look inside and outside the company for a permanent successor. Huang, 63, co-founded the company more than three decades ago and still owns about 3.6% of it. He had admitted a romantic relationship with an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore firm that the US Commerce Department has reportedly been investigating for routing export-restricted Nvidia chips to China; the New York Times then reported an affair with a senior Nvidia executive who reported to him, and late on Thursday Reuters, citing sources, reported a further relationship with another employee.

The interim chief, Colette Kress, has been finance chief since 2013 after 13 years at Microsoft and three at Cisco. The shares fell as much as 14% after the opening bell, touched a 52-week low and finished at $166.19, down 4.3%, on volume of almost 748 million shares; rivals AMD and Broadcom rose more than 5%. In Frankfurt the DAX, which had crossed 27,000 points for the first time that morning, gave back most of the gain and closed at 26,774, up 0.42%.

We need to know to what extent the Chinese spy influenced CEO Jensen's decision. Senator Ruben Gallego, on Nvidia's request to sell high-end chips to China

Washington is not done with him. Huang has said he will testify to Congress before Thanksgiving on 26 November; the chair of the Senate Banking Committee wants both him and commerce secretary Howard Lutnick in front of it. The New York Times reports that President Trump urged Huang to fight for his job, and Trump has since mused about Apple's Tim Cook running Nvidia. Inside the company, Wired reports, employees are petitioning for Huang's return. Reuters expects tougher curbs on AI-chip sales to China to follow. Nvidia reports third-quarter results on Tuesday.

Cortegaça, Ovar & the Ria

Ovar · Ria de Aveiro · Port

A wider harbour mouth at Aveiro could put "a large part of the Marinha" under water, Ovar's mayor warns, and he wants a flood study first

The Port of Aveiro wants to widen and deepen the barra so bigger ships can come in, and its environmental impact study is about to go out for public consultation. Mayor Domingos Silva told this week's council meeting that a wider mouth means more seawater entering the Ria, and that Ovar and Murtosa, the most lagoon-facing councils, have reservations: the port's own models show the average level of the once-a-decade "super tide" rising by 4 centimetres.

"It may seem small, but it is very large given the extent of the Ria," he said; the figure is an average, 1 or 2 cm in some places and 5 to 7 in others, "and in that case a large part of the Marinha would be flooded", the low farmland on Ovar's side of the lagoon. He will demand a flood study and mitigation measures from the port authority when the consultation opens: "The port's competitiveness cannot come at the expense of the population."

Porto · Culture · Strike

Over half of Casa da Música's staff are on a three-day strike, and they say the foundation is replacing them

More than half of the Fundação Casa da Música's workers stayed out on Thursday, the first of three strike days, RTP reports. They want "fair wages and decent careers", above all recognition of seniority under the career plan introduced in April: one step up the scale a year from January 2027.

Workers and the Communist party accuse the foundation of bringing in substitutes for strikers, which Portuguese law forbids, and the PCP has already questioned the government. The walkout runs into a busy weekend: the Amazonian music festival fills the building, with a club night tonight.

Cloud · Outage

Microsoft 365 went down worldwide on Thursday evening, and by dawn it was only coming back slowly

From early Thursday evening European time "an unexpectedly large number of servers" failed, heise reports, overloading the rest. Exchange Online, Outlook, Teams, SharePoint, Copilot, Defender, Intune, Planner and Power BI were all affected; the first reports came from the US.

Microsoft said it was checking and restarting the affected servers one by one and had found "a small number" of recent configuration changes, which it was considering rolling back. By 5 am Lisbon time services were returning bit by bit, RTP said, and the company admitted the restoration was proving difficult.
