Germany · Europe · Cars

"The combustion ban is history": Berlin and Paris agree new cars need only cut CO2 by 90% in 2035

Chancellor Merz and his SPD environment minister back a Franco-German position that leaves carmakers a tenth of their 2021 emissions after 2035, in return for building cars and batteries in Europe. The Greens and Transport & Environment call it a historic mistake.

tagesschau · heise · Brussels and Berlin, 16 October 2026

Germany and France settled their joint line on the end of the combustion engine on the sidelines of Friday's EU summit in Brussels. "Let me put it quite simply: the combustion ban is then history," Friedrich Merz told reporters. "We are getting a lot of agreement for it." Under the law in force, the average emissions of each maker's new cars must fall to zero in 2035, which in practice rules out selling new petrol and diesel cars.

The Franco-German position lowers that to a 90% cut against 2021, and the carmakers would not have to compensate for the remaining 10%. Carsten Schneider (SPD), the federal environment minister, says Berlin will make up the extra emissions itself, chiefly by getting more low-carbon steel into car-building and more recycled steel into new railway investment. In return, European makers are to be favoured in subsidy schemes such as purchase premiums, and vehicles and their parts, "batteries in particular", would have to be made in Europe rather than mostly in China.

"Whoever weakens the 2035 targets now creates new uncertainty and puts the German car industry's competitiveness at risk." Swantje Michaelsen, Greens transport spokeswoman in the Bundestag

Finance minister and SPD leader Lars Klingbeil welcomed the declaration: Germany should not end up merely bolting together components from Asia, and battery-cell production belongs here too. Now, he said, the industry must invest in German plants and secure jobs. The environmental group Transport & Environment says the decision endangers Europe's place in the global race for car and battery technology. Both governments now have to carry the proposal through the EU; heise reports that the two are also preparing a wider initiative to shore up Europe's carmakers against Chinese competition.

Espinho · Animation

CINANIMA turns 50 next door: 17 days of animation in Espinho from 6 November

The international animated-film festival in Espinho, just up the coast from Esmoriz, runs its 50th edition from 6 to 22 November under the motto "O Sonho Comanda a Vida", from António Gedeão's poem. More than 2,400 films were submitted from 110 countries for the national and international competitions.

New this year is the Pequeno Golfinho prize: 38 films for young audiences, judged by a jury of children and teenagers. The anniversary programme adds a curated "50 years of CINANIMA" strand, tributes to João Machado and Henrique Alves Costa, retrospectives, cine-concerts and masterclasses, and a residency in which pupils from 11 secondary schools will make three short animated films. The festival also travels to universities, schools and social-housing neighbourhoods.

After an emergency meeting on Friday, the Left's group leadership in the Abgeordnetenhaus said Emde, who won Neukölln's first constituency outright, had in effect withdrawn her signature from the group's founding declaration, which commits every member to Israel's right to exist. Linux · AI

Linux drops the "Assisted-by" tag for AI-written patches: only one commit in ten carried it

A majority of the roughly 30 developers at the Maintainers Summit in Prague on 8 October voted to stop requiring the tag. Sasha Levin, the Nvidia engineer and stable-kernel co-maintainer who wrote the rule in the first place, filed the patch to remove it the next day, and Jonathan Corbet has taken it into docs-next. Linus Torvalds and Greg Kroah-Hartman back it; a first attempt failed in July.

Levin's numbers: of the 18,533 non-merge commits in Linux 7.3 up to rc7, only about 10% carry the tag, though AI is used far more often. Some developers never heard of the rule, others skip it, and maintainers rarely ask, so its absence proves nothing. Contributors will instead describe how they used AI tools in the patch series' cover letter.

Lisbon · Lost film

A lost Portuguese cartoon from 1931, bought for €25 at the Feira da Ladra, returns to the screen

A Lenda de Miragaia (1931) · Silhouette animation · short, 28,400 frames · Cinemateca, Lisbon, 27 Oct

Raúl Faria da Fonseca and António Cunhal's film, one of the first animations in Portuguese cinema, had been given up for lost until a regular at the Cinemateca, Duarte Veloso, and his father spotted two film cans with its name on the label at Lisbon's flea market. Inside was the nitrate negative; they donated it in May.

The Cinemateca restored it by analogue means and rebuilt the original intertitles and edit from handwritten notes on the negative, period sources and Almeida Garrett's version of the legend. It screens on 27 October, World Day for Audiovisual Heritage, in a programme of animated shorts that also closes the recovery-fund project that digitised more than 1,000 Portuguese films.

Egypt · Archaeology

A farmer's inscribed stone leads to two colossal statues of Ramses II in the Nile Delta

Egypt's antiquities ministry says the remains of two enormous statues of the pharaoh came out of the ground at Behbeit Al-Hagar, the village best known for the ruined Iseion, a temple of Isis, after a resident reported a stone with hieroglyphs on his land.

The dig produced a carved head of Ramses wearing the double crown of Upper and Lower Egypt. At about 1.6 metres, it implies a standing figure of more than 8.5 metres. Nearby lay the upper part of the statue's back pillar, inscribed with the king's Horus name, "Hor-Ka-Nakht-Mery-Maat".

Brazil · Climate

Amazon deforestation falls 41.8% in a year, the steepest drop since records began in 1988

Satellite data for the year to July 2026 show the record fall in the Brazilian Amazon, and a 23.8% fall in the Cerrado savanna. Environment minister João Paulo Capobianco called it "absolutely stratospheric"; Lula, who has pledged zero illegal deforestation by 2030, faces Flávio Bolsonaro in the run-off on 25 October, with the polls neck and neck.

Under Jair Bolsonaro, from 2019 to 2022, the Amazon lost nearly 60% more forest than in the four years before, AFP reported from official data. Lula tightened monitoring and penalties for illegal logging. Flávio Bolsonaro is expected to continue his father's policies, though he promises to fight organised crime in the region.
