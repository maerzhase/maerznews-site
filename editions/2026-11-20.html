Germany · Labour · Industry

Germany's metalworkers walk out for a whole day, the first 24-hour strikes in eight years, as the union's 5 per cent meets an offer of 1.3

BMW in Munich, Audi in Ingolstadt, ZF's supply plants and the shipyards of the north are meant to stand still until tonight. The employers call it millions in damage and an advertisement for moving production abroad; the union calls their offer a pay cut.

tagesschau · NDR · as read at 07:00 on 20 November 2026

The all-day warning strikes began at midnight, when the peace obligation of the old agreement ran out, and some plants walked out on Thursday's night shift. IG Metall last used the tool in 2018, when about 500,000 people in 250 plants stayed away for whole days; it then got through four bargaining rounds without it. The difference from the usual two-hour stoppages is money as well as time: members on a 24-hour strike draw strike pay from the union. The industry, from carmakers and machine builders to electrical goods and metal production, employs about 3.7 million people under the regional agreements, and more action is planned for early next week.

The gap has not moved in three rounds. The union wants 5 per cent; the employers offered 1.3 per cent and have not improved it, which IG Metall says would leave members poorer once prices are counted. "With their minus offer the employers have made the barrel overflow," said Horst Ott, who leads the union in Bavaria; he says they also want a blank cheque to cut pay and bonuses and lengthen hours, while refusing binding promises on investment in German plants. The employers answer that full-day strikes cause damage running into millions and "work like an advertising event for foreign locations".

The last amber phase before the red phase of an industry-wide strike. former IG Metall chief Jörg Hofmann, on all-day warning strikes

The mood is set by more than one table. Volkswagen, which bargains separately, opened its own round on Thursday by asking for no rise in 2027 or 2028, the end of a supplement worth 27.5 per cent of a month's pay and of a €167 monthly allowance, and a 35-hour week that can stretch without extra pay; the union's Thorsten Gröger called it "a knife in the back". Lufthansa Airlines told staff it will cut 1,000 full-time jobs at its Frankfurt and Munich hubs after an operating loss of €180 million in nine months. Why it matters: after 24-hour strikes the next step is a ballot for an open-ended walkout. Portugal's civil servants strike today too; see Portugal & the Coast.

Ovar · Council finances

Ovar owes €144.70 per resident, among the 20 least indebted councils in Portugal and less than a quarter of the national average

The 2025 municipal finance yearbook ranks the concelho 20th of 308 for liabilities per inhabitant. The national average is €633; the lowest is Velas in the Azores at €70.50, followed by Santa Cruz da Graciosa (€71.90) and Abrantes (€77.30), and the highest Fornos de Algodres at €5,980.90.

Three other councils in the Aveiro district make the top 20: Santa Maria da Feira is fourth with €81.10 a head, Águeda eighth with €110.60 and Arouca 13th with €123. The yearbook also measures spending commitments against revenue actually collected: the national average was 101 per cent, meaning councils promised more than they took in, while 154 of the 308 stayed below 100. Why it matters: Ovar has room to borrow just as it is pressed to spend, on the stalled housing at Avenida D. Maria II and on roads like the one below.

Berlin · Museums

The Pergamon Altar returns on 4 June 2027: most of the Pergamonmuseum reopens, and tickets go on sale in early December

The Staatliche Museen said on Thursday that "large parts of the house" will open in June, including the altar, which nobody has been able to see for more than ten years. Also back: the Hall of Hellenistic Architecture, a newly designed permanent display for the Museum of Islamic Art and highlights of the Museum of the Ancient Near East. Tickets for the first summer weeks go on sale from the start of December.

The museum has been under renovation in stages since 2013 and almost entirely shut since 2023. The south wing stays a building site; the whole complex is due in 2037, at a cost of at least €1.5 billion. The altar hall gets a new entrance and artificial light alongside its skylights, so it no longer goes gloomy on grey days. Marion Ackermann, who heads the Prussian Cultural Heritage Foundation, called it one of Germany's most important attractions.

Portugal & the Coast

National · Strike

Schools and hospitals hardest hit as the civil service strikes over a meal allowance that rises by 30 cents

Today's national strike, called by the Frente Comum, is the first of four called by different public-sector unions, and education and health are expected to feel it most. At a last meeting on Thursday the government offered to bring forward to 2027 the meal allowance planned for 2028: €6.45 a day instead of today's €6.15, rather than the €6.30 the 2026–29 pay accord had scheduled. Travel and transport allowances rise by 5 per cent.

"That is what the government has to offer the people who keep the schools, the hospitals, the rubbish collection, Social Security, the tax offices and the police working every day," said the Frente Comum's coordinator, Sebastião Santana, who called it a provocation. The government says its priorities for 2027 are a review of general careers and a new appraisal system, and will meet the unions in December; any further talks, Santana said, come after the strike.

Security · Ruby

ruby2ßhell: 21 German sharp esses are enough to overflow a buffer in Ruby, and Rails apps are exposed by default

Veria Labs found that Ruby's case functions mis-size their output when an application works in ISO-8859 or Windows-125x encodings. Uppercasing turns ß into SS, so the string grows, but Ruby reserves only the input length plus 20 bytes; pass 21 or more ß through upcase, swapcase, downcase(:fold) or casecmp? and it writes past the buffer.

That is a starting point for remote code execution, not a finished attack: each application needs its own exploit. Rails and classic-style Sinatra are affected in their default settings, so GitLab and Mastodon are among the projects at risk. Proof-of-concept code for popular Ruby software is promised in a week. Update Ruby now; PHP shipped fixes for 17 holes the same day.

Market · Stockholm

A Hilma af Klint sells for $12.7 million, sixteen times its top estimate, the first of her abstractions offered in 30 years

Ser III No. 5 (1920) fetched SEK 128.9 million at Bukowskis in Stockholm on Thursday, a record for the Swedish painter now called the mother of abstraction. It is one of 85 geometric paintings she made in a burst of a few months that year, full of theosophical triangles and circles, before she turned to naturalistic watercolours late in life.

New York's fall sales ended in a lower key the same evening: Christie's took $145.9 million for 56 lots of 21st-century art, led by a $32.9 million Basquiat, amid what ARTnews called flagging energy.
