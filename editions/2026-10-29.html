Spain · Rail · Disaster

A signalling system fails, the control room waves the trains on, and a Renfe high-speed train ploughs into a stalled Iryo train near Toledo: at least 68 dead

It is Spain's worst rail disaster since Santiago de Compostela in 2013 and its second high-speed crash this year. The transport minister calls the clearance "inexplicable"; the signal, he says, was at red.

BBC · tagesschau (ARD) · RTP · Observador · 28–29 October 2026

At 8:30 on Wednesday the automatic safety system on a 63 km stretch of the Madrid–Seville line in Castilla-La Mancha went down, and every train on it stopped. The control centre in Madrid took over. According to El País, within six minutes it had told drivers in writing and by phone to carry on at "normal" speed; the rules require moving "on sight" at 40 km/h, slow enough to brake for whatever is ahead. One Iryo train, with 147 people aboard, could not restart, its crew phoned the problem in, and the screens in Madrid showed it and a Renfe train on the same track.

Shortly after nine, between Mora and Orgaz, about 140 km south of the capital, the Renfe train with 397 passengers ran into it from behind, Spanish media say at more than 100 km/h. At least two carriages went down an embankment. Transport minister Óscar Puente says the signal was at red and an Adif employee still gave the all-clear, a "wrong decision" made "under inexplicable circumstances"; the employee has hired a lawyer, and audio between the drivers and the controller has been published.

"If there have been failures in the security system, they will be addressed with the utmost firmness." Pedro Sánchez, at the crash site

Eighty people are in hospital, 15 of them critical; Madrid's transfusion centre has asked for blood, and an investigating judge has sealed the site in Urda. Three days of mourning began today, and on Wednesday politicians suspended campaigning for next month's general election. On Wednesday evening some 1,500 people, called out by far-right groups, clashed with police outside the transport ministry. Among the survivors is a Portuguese woman, Vitória Maia, who told RTP she woke up "in the air" and climbed out through a hole in a wrecked carriage. Services between Madrid, Ciudad Real and Andalusia remain suspended.

Brandenburg · Constitutional court

Juli Zeh steps down as a Brandenburg constitutional judge, and her seat cannot be filled without the AfD

The novelist, 52, who sits on the court under her legal name Julia Finck, has asked to be released, citing the workload of her professional commitments. She was elected in December 2018 on the SPD's nomination for ten years, a term due to run to 2029; her new novel, "Pony Show", came out recently. The nine-member court in Potsdam keeps working with eight, since six can decide.

Replacing her is the problem. Judges need a two-thirds majority in the Landtag, and the AfD holds 30 of the 88 seats, just over a third. No judge has yet been elected on an AfD nomination; its whip Dennis Hohloch says the seat is now theirs and that "by proportion" two are owed. The constitution only says the political forces should be "appropriately" represented. The SPD's Björn Lüttmann calls the timing an "unfortunate surprise"; the CDU sees no rush.

Parliament · Budget

The 2027 budget passes its first vote: PSD and CDS for, the PS abstaining, Chega, IL, Livre and the PCP against

Wednesday's vote on the general principles of the Orçamento do Estado went as the PS had promised; the PAN abstained too. José Luís Carneiro said his party had spared the country a political crisis and will now try to "improve" the budget in the line-by-line committee stage, demanding the "scrupulous" delivery of what the prime minister has promised. He also accused the PSD and CDS of "attacking" the PS throughout the debate.

In the debate the government announced support of up to 80% for poorer households buying an electric car, and said the demilitarisation of the Alcochete firing range, the site of the new airport, starts in January. Outside, the housing protest went on into the night: in Porto the assembly voted to pitch tents again in the Praça D. João I, then took them down after talks with the PSP and was allowed to stay without them; in Lisbon most left the Rossio around midnight, after an assembly of 400 called a new action for Saturday.

GitHub · Octoverse

TypeScript stays GitHub's top language, and agents now draft more than 40% of pull requests

GitHub's Octoverse report, out at Universe in San Francisco, counts 1.3 billion merged pull requests in twelve months, about 3.5 million a day and three times the year before. More than 40% start as an agent's draft, up from 17% in March; projects with an AGENTS.md file see eight times as many commits. Users rose to 225 million from 180 million.

TypeScript and Python each have about 13 million contributors a month, JavaScript 7.4 million; TypeScript first overtook Python in 2025. It is about 10% more common in new repos where an agent is involved, which GitHub thinks is because typed code is easier for models to check. The most active open-source projects are OpenClaw, vLLM and llama.cpp.

Brandenburg · Kunsthalle

After 25 rent-free years in the old Brennabor works, the Kunsthalle Brennabor has to move out

West Brandenburg's largest contemporary-art space shows sculpture and painting from the region on 600 square metres of one floor of the brick Brennabor works in Brandenburg an der Havel. Because the complex was restored with EU money, the friends' association has used it without paying rent; that arrangement ends with the year.

The city gives €110,000 a year for exhibitions and running costs, not enough for rent, and has none to add. Mayor Daniel Keip says the same sum is in next year's budget. The association's 40 or so members are looking for another space while the last show runs; its chair, Melanie Ballerstein, says the city would lose its place for contemporary art and its courses and workshops.
