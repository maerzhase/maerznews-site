Berlin · After the election

A Spandau recount hands the AfD a seat and shrinks Berlin's parliament by five

The AfD's Tommy Tabor, not the CDU's Bettina Meißner, won Spandau's first constituency, by 35 votes. The knock-on cuts the Abgeordnetenhaus from 158 seats to 153.

rbb24 · tagesschau · Landeswahlleiter Berlin · 2 October

The mistake was a single phone call. In one Spandau polling station the official record, the Niederschrift, was correct, but the quick result phoned through to the district election office was not. The routine check of the provisional result caught it, and a public recount of first votes on Thursday confirmed it: Bettina Meißner of the CDU, first declared the winner of Spandau constituency 1, is in fact 35 votes behind the AfD's Tommy Tabor. The district returning officer, Katrin Zickert, notes that the AfD also leads there on second votes.

Because Berlin balances overhang seats with extra seats for the other parties, that one constituency moves the whole chamber. State returning officer Stephan Bröchler now expects 153 seats instead of the 158 announced on election night. The CDU, SPD and Greens each lose one, the Left two. Five people who had counted on a seat will not get one: Meißner, Martin Matz (SPD), Oda Hassepaß (Greens), and Coco Aglibut and Bettina Günter of the Left. The AfD stays on 29.

We are not seeing electoral chaos. We are in an orderly, public process of checks. Stephan Bröchler, Berlin's returning officer

The arithmetic of the coming government barely shifts. The Left, Greens and SPD, whose leaders began exploratory talks this week, would hold 91 of 153 seats, comfortably above the 77 needed. But Bröchler did have a warning for the parties: no one holds a mandate until the final result and the first sitting, and he called it "not a good procedure" that the CDU, SPD and AfD had already elected new group leaderships.

The figures are not yet "set in stone", he said; the state election committee decides on 12 October. After a 2021 election so flawed it had to be rerun, he wants the next one to digitise the polling station record, recounts included.

Aveiro · Culture

The Museu de Aveiro closes on Tuesday for 18 months after all

The Museu de Santa Joana, Aveiro's main museum in the former convent of Jesus, shuts to the public from 6 October while €5.2 million of works go on. The council had promised it would stay open through most of the job, which began in July, but now says safety rules that out. The work covers the historic part of the building, the Igreja de Jesus and the old cloister, and is meant to cure rising damp, roof leaks, structural cracks and the rainwater drainage. It is expected to take 18 months, so the museum would reopen in spring 2028.

Retail · Insolvency

Galeria files for insolvency for the fourth time in six years

Germany's last big department-store chain has asked the district court in Düsseldorf to open insolvency proceedings, after earlier filings in 2020, 2022 and 2024. Its 83 stores stay open for now; what happens to some 12,000 staff, and whether they get insolvency pay, is unclear. Galeria has belonged since summer 2024 to the US investor NRDC and a company of the businessman Bernd Beetz, and in July put 33 stores under review pending cheaper rents. The services union ver.di blames "hair-raising management mistakes" and owners who did not invest. Department stores' share of German retail has fallen from 4.2% in 2020 to 1.2%, according to the EHI institute; if Galeria goes, the classic Warenhaus goes with it.

Azores · Justice

Prosecutors open an inquiry into the US use of the Lajes base before the Iran war

The Ministério Público confirmed on Friday that it is investigating whether American forces used the Lajes air base on Terceira lawfully in the run-up to the US–Israeli offensive against Iran, which began on 28 February. The inquiry follows a complaint the Bloco de Esquerda filed in April, calling the use a "gross violation of international law". Foreign minister Paulo Rangel has denied any breach of the base agreement with Washington. Last week the Bloco delivered a letter with about 8,500 signatures to the prime minister's residence asking for the base to be closed to the bombing campaign.

macOS · Privacy

Apple tightens Full Disk Access on the Mac because of AI agents

Apple says some developers use the macOS Full Disk Access permission in ways that expose "files, mail, messages, and even browsing history" without users understanding it, and that the risk will grow "substantially" as agents get more autonomous. New controls are meant to make the stakes clear before anyone grants it. The move follows the columnist Jason Aten's report that Meta's Muse agent quoted one of his Apple Messages threads unprompted. Meta's CTO said Muse can read Messages only with both Full Disk Access and its own connector switched on; the security researcher Patrick Wardle replied that Full Disk Access alone makes any non-root file readable. Apple named no app.

Bought for $30 at an estate sale, a Gertrude Abercrombie sells for a record $1.3 million

Alderman Merriam's Shells (1951) — nine small shells on a dark surface, a tenth hung on a black wall by an almost invisible thread — was picked up at a Michigan estate sale in June. Its anonymous buyer diverted a family holiday to see the touring Abercrombie retrospective at the Milwaukee Art Museum, found the painting reproduced in the catalogue, and had it confirmed by the scholar Susan Weininger. At Freeman's in New York on Thursday it was estimated at up to $200,000; the hammer fell at $1.3 million, beating the Chicago surrealist's previous record of $1 million.

Elvis Costello's debut, unpacked into 104 tracks

New wave / pub rock · 1977 album · 5xCD 49th-anniversary box

My Aim Is True came out in 1977 when Costello was 22, and Pitchfork still calls it a masterpiece of "complaint-rock", with "Alison" its great soul ballad. The new five-disc set, issued with a wink on the 49th rather than the 50th anniversary, tries to explain where it came from. The second disc, The Blue Print, is the revelation: early demos and bedroom tapes from his pub-rock years with Flip City around London's Hope & Anchor, an ambitious writer steeped in Randy Newman, Warren Zevon and Lowell George. Later discs follow the change that came after Stiff's Jake Riviera suggested the name Elvis and the designer Barney Bubbles gave him a look.

Energy · Fuel

The G7 release 100 million barrels of oil and diesel, and Trump drops his export-ban threat

After a video call that included Chancellor Merz, the G7 agreed to put 100 million barrels of crude and diesel from emergency stocks on the market over four months, under the International Energy Agency. That is roughly one day of world oil demand; who supplies how much is not yet known. The leaders also pledged no energy export restrictions among themselves, which binds the US too, and Donald Trump then said he would not ban diesel exports after all. Oil fell up to 5% in New York and Brent briefly dipped below $100. The Iran war has pushed OECD oil stocks to their lowest in 36 years.
