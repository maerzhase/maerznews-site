Israel · Coalition · After the election

Herzog hands Gadi Eisenkot the mandate: 67 of 120 MPs back the former army chief, and the Netanyahu era may be ending

Neither bloc won a majority on 27 October. But Eisenkot collected the recommendations of six lists, from Bennett's Together to both Arab parties, and now has 28 days to turn them into a government. Netanyahu's camp holds 53 seats.

BBC · tagesschau · Reuters · Times of Israel · Central Elections Committee · 7–8 November 2026

Gadi Eisenkot, who ran the army as chief of staff from 2015 to 2019, founded Yashar for this election and came second with 21 seats, behind Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud on 27. On paper that should have kept Netanyahu ahead. It did not, because the parties that ran against him lined up behind one name: Naftali Bennett's Together and Yair Golan's Democrats on 2 November, then Avigdor Lieberman of Yisrael Beiteinu, then the Arab-led lists, Ra'am on 4 November and the Joint List on the 5th. That made 67 recommendations, an absolute majority, and President Isaac Herzog announced on Saturday that he would task Eisenkot rather than Netanyahu.

The hard part starts now. The Joint List told Herzog it will support an Eisenkot government from outside, without joining it, and promised not to bring it down "even amid war". Without its 11 seats, a coalition of the other five lists has 56, five short. The Zionist parties in his camp still disagree about formal cooperation with Arab parties, which Israeli governments have rarely sought.

"Stop climbing trees and face reality." Isaac Herzog to party leaders on 1 November, signalling he wants a unity government (Times of Israel)

Netanyahu's bloc of Likud, Otzma Yehudit, United Torah Judaism, Shas and the Religious Zionists fell to 53 seats, in an election with unusually high turnout, especially among Arab voters. The other routes to 61 that analysts list all run through Likud joining Eisenkot, or through the ultra-Orthodox parties switching sides. The new Knesset is sworn in on Tuesday. If Eisenkot fails, Herzog can extend his deadline or hand the mandate to someone else.

Ovar · Council · Cyberattack

"I'll answer in writing": three weeks after the hack, Ovar's mayor still won't say what failed

At the last public meeting of the council executive, streamed live, the Chega councillor Flávio Costa asked what the technical report on the 18 October attack had found. The mayor did not answer: "Depois respondo por escrito." OvarNews, in a piece published on Saturday, calls the silence "grave" and lists the questions it says residents are owed answers to: were there isolated, tested backups, a continuity and disaster-recovery plan, and when was it last tested? Were the systems up to date and monitored? Has there been an independent audit since?

The attack took the council's website offline, where it still is, and email came back only this week. The paper's point is the one security people make: being hit is not the scandal, but preparation and transparency are the council's responsibility, and residents have a right to know which services and which of their data were affected. Next door, Santa Maria da Feira's council was also knocked offline by a cyberattack on Thursday.

€1.2 billion for schools, nurseries and labs this year; €7 million has actually arrived

AI · Research

An outside auditor spent 11 weeks inside Anthropic and found its AI is now speeding up its own research

METR, a non-profit that evaluates AI models, published its assessment on Thursday after Anthropic invited it in from 12 August to 29 October. Its estimate: the company is improving its models on well-specified tasks roughly twice as fast as it did between March 2024 and October 2025, and its best guess is that about 80% of that acceleration comes from Anthropic using its own AI tools internally, not from more training compute.

The Verge reports the findings are alarming researchers, as evidence of the "AI building AI" loop safety people have long worried about. METR interviewed 15 researchers and says its attribution is largely observational; Anthropic redacted the detailed estimates and forecasts as commercially sensitive, ahead of its planned stock-market listing. Sculpture · New York

Doris Salcedo is legally blind, and says losing her sight made her work bigger and better

Diagnosed at 31 with Stargardt disease, the Colombian artist, now 67, sees only peripherally. She used to make every sculpture alone and improvise; as her vision went she had to work with assistants, and plan everything so it could be explained. "A collective statement is far more powerful than a lonely solo voice," she told Art in America in her Bogotá studios.

Her monumental "anti-monuments", like the crack through Tate Modern's Turbine Hall in Shibboleth (2007), all came after the diagnosis. This month White Cube in New York shows the culmination: a full-size house, nearly 30 feet high, woven entirely from human hair, knot by knot, by dozens of assistants across three studios.

Obituary · New York punk

Richard Lloyd, the other guitar on Marquee Moon, has died at 75

Punk · art rock · Television · Marquee Moon (Elektra, 1977)

Lloyd joined Tom Verlaine, Richard Hell and Billy Ficca in 1973, when the Neon Boys became Television, and the band built its cult at Max's Kansas City and CBGB. Where their peers played three chords, Television's sound rested on the long, interlocking lines between Verlaine and Lloyd, on Marquee Moon and on 1978's Adventure, before the two fell out and the band split.

He died of bile-duct cancer, with his wife Sheila and his dog Willie Mays at his side, his family said; a fundraiser for his treatment had launched in August. Born Richard John Alt in Pittsburgh in 1951, he also made solo albums.

Poland · Kraków

A gunman fires from his car into Kraków's Main Market Square, killing six; a Portuguese woman is among the wounded

Shortly before 3 pm on Saturday, a man drove into the square, the tourist heart of Poland's second city, and shot "at random people from his car", police said, even hitting a passing tram. Four women and two men died, one of them British; 15 were injured, 12 critically, among them people from Germany, Austria, Portugal, Russia and Britain. Police shot and arrested the suspect, a 25-year-old, within three minutes.

Three more bodies, two women and a man, probably relatives, were found at his home, Prime Minister Donald Tusk said. The BBC reports he is suspected of far-right ties; the motive is not yet known. The city has asked people to stay away from the square while the investigation continues.
