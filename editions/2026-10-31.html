Economy · Prices · Portugal & Germany

One energy shock, two directions: Portugal's inflation climbs to 3.8% while Germany's fuel-tax cut pulls its rate down to 2.8%

Both flash estimates came out on Friday. Portugal's rate is its highest in almost three and a half years, with energy up 17.4%; in Germany the Tankrabatt has taken the edge off at the pump, for now.

INE via Jornal de Negócios & CNN Portugal · Destatis via tagesschau · RTP · 30 October 2026

Portugal's statistics office estimates that consumer prices rose 3.8% in the year to October, 0.2 points more than in September and the third increase in a row. Energy did most of the work: the energy index jumped from 15.3% to 17.4%, and has stayed in double digits since August as the war in Iran and the blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, through which about a fifth of the world's oil and gas passes, keep fuel dear. Unprocessed food held at 3.7%; core inflation edged up to 2.8%. The harmonised index used for EU comparisons is at 3.9%.

Germany went the other way. Destatis puts October inflation at 2.8%, after 3.3% in September, the first reading above 3% in almost three years. The reason is the Tankrabatt, the cut in the energy tax on petrol and diesel that took effect on 1 October and runs to the end of the year: energy is still 12.2% dearer than a year ago, but that is down from almost 15%. Food rose just 0.4%, services 2.5%.

"Around the turn of the year, inflation will probably be above three percent again." Jörg Krämer, Commerzbank, to tagesschau

Neither side expects relief to last. The Bundesbank expects German inflation to climb noticeably once the fuel discount ends, and economists warn that dearer transport and raw materials will gradually push up other prices. In Portugal the October figure already has a price tag: motorway tolls are set to rise 3.82% in January, the inflation rate without housing plus a 0.1-point bonus for the concessionaires.

In front of 240 Ovar sixth-formers, the education minister promises more student grants and 17,000 new residence beds

Berlin · Coalition

Berlin's Left votes today on coalition talks, as the Eralp affair costs the SPD its economy chief

Delegates at a special party conference on Saturday decide whether the Left, the Greens and the SPD open formal talks on a red-green-red Senate; the SPD and the Greens have already said yes. The mood is tense. On Thursday "Der Spiegel" published internal notes from an interview with Elif Eralp, the Left's lead candidate and likely next Governing Mayor, suggesting she fears supporters of Palestinian terror groups. Eralp answered that she has received more threats since the election and is following the advice of the security services: "I am a politician, but I am also a human being and a mother."

The fallout reached the SPD the same evening: Franziska Giffey, the outgoing economy senator, turned down the job of leading the economy working group, citing unresolved questions on antisemitism, the Left's relations with Islamist networks and the socialisation of large landlords. At today's conference, pro-Palestinian groups plan protests outside, and motions from the youth wing and Neukölln want socialisation to cover firms with 3,000 flats, as the referendum said, not the 10,000 agreed in the exploratory paper. The SPD also demands that the disputed Neukölln MP Vanessa Emde be expelled from the Left's group first; that vote cannot come before next week.

The health service heads for a €2.68 billion gap next year, and the minister announces "another wave" of public-private hospitals

Questioned by MPs on the 2027 budget, health minister Ana Paula Martins rejected calling the SNS's projected €2,679 million deficit a loss: it is, she said, an estimate of funding needs, investment included. Security · Supply chain

The official Claude Code extension on Cursor's marketplace carried an SSH backdoor for nearly 12 hours

Versions 2.1.312 and 2.1.313 of "Claude Code for VS Code", published through Anthropic's own account on the Cursor Marketplace, were poisoned; Cursor says at most 5,000 users got them, helped by auto-updates. The code ran as soon as the editor loaded the file, with no click needed: it appended an attacker's SSH key to authorized_keys, tried to open a reverse tunnel, set up persistence on macOS, Windows and Linux, and reported hostname, Git email, public IP and the names of environment variables, handy for spotting cloud tokens.

Bugs in the malware mean the tunnel mostly failed unless Cursor ran as admin, but the key and the beacon did land. How the account was taken over is unexplained; Anthropic shipped a clean 2.1.314 within hours but had not yet warned customers on Friday. If you use Cursor, check those versions and your ~/.ssh.

São João da Madeira · Museums

The hat museum 20 minutes from Cortegaça is Portugal's Museum of the Year

The Associação Portuguesa de Museologia gave its 2026 Museum of the Year award to the Museu da Chapelaria in São João da Madeira on Friday, ahead of Lisbon's MACAM and the Museu Nacional Resistência e Liberdade in Peniche's fortress, which got honourable mentions.

Founded in 2005 in the old Empresa Industrial de Chapelaria, it is the only museum in the Iberian Peninsula devoted entirely to the history and industrial making of hats, the trade that shaped the town through the 20th century. It keeps the memory of the hatters known as "Unhas Negras", black nails, for the stains that felt and dye left on their hands.

Collision Course (2026): a bank heist in crisis-era Spain, a car crash, and five people who are not who they seem

Heist thriller · Spain · Netflix · since Fri 30 Oct · no scores yet

Daniel Sánchez Arévalo directs and co-writes with Ramón Campos of the prolific Spanish production house Bambú. The film is set more than a decade ago, when banks sold risky mortgages to poorer households and then repossessed tens of thousands of homes in the crash, and one of the bank's directors is caught up in the plot. Poland · Rule of law

Riot police at Poland's constitutional court as Tusk moves to prosecute its top judges, and the court cries "coup"

Justice minister Waldemar Żurek has asked for the court's president, Bogdan Święczkowski, and vice-president, Bartłomiej Sochański, to be stripped of immunity so that criminal charges can be brought; the government has suspended both. Police were deployed on Friday to stop protesters entering the building as supporters of Law and Justice (PiS) gathered outside. The court said anyone taking part was "committing the crime of a coup d'état".

President Karol Nawrocki, backed by PiS, turned his plane around mid-flight to Senegal and went into the court. Donald Tusk calls it restoring the rule of law after years in which PiS packed the tribunal, appointments that the European Court of Human Rights and the EU's Court of Justice later found unlawful: "There is no other way."
