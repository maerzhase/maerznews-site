Housing · Protests

Thousands march for housing, the tents go back up, and at 2 a.m. police clear Lisbon and Porto again: 31 arrests

Lisbon's organisers counted 25,000 marchers from the Marquês to the Rossio. That night 130 tents stood in the square and the camp in Porto's Praça D. João I was rebuilt. At two o'clock the PSP moved on both at once; it reports 31 arrests and seven injured, while Porto's organisers say 37 of them were hurt.

RTP · Observador · Público · Jornal de Notícias · Lusa · 24–25 October 2026

The Lisbon march left the Marquês de Pombal on Saturday afternoon and filled the Rossio by 18:30. The PSP would not give a number but admitted there were thousands; Livre, PCP and Bloco joined it, and the PS leader José Luís Carneiro, in Coimbra, demanded that the government stop its new rental law. The demands read out at the evening assembly: reverse that law, end evictions without rehousing, stop investment funds buying homes, build more public housing, cap rents and bring empty buildings back into use. In Porto marchers went "from Batalha to the Praça" and voted to pitch the tents again; about 250 met in Aveiro, 200 in Coimbra, 50 in Évora.

The police had said no tents would be allowed overnight, and gave Porto's campers a choice: stay, but without them. At about 02:00 a coordinated "national operation" moved on both squares. The PSP says it acted "to restore legality" after exhausting efforts at a voluntary solution; the 31 detained appear in court on Monday. Of the seven injured it counts, three are officers, one bitten by a protester's dog.

"Maybe there are 25,000 egoists here." João Veloso, Acampada de Lisboa, answering the prime minister

Porto's organisers tell it differently. Spokeswoman Catarina dos Santos spoke of the third night of repression since the camp began on 10 October, about 20 crowd-control dogs and weapons pointed at them; reporters saw people thrown to the ground and a taser fired at one protester. Her answer to whether they would go back to the square: "nobody moves from the praça until the demands are heard." In Lisbon the call is now for an Iberian general strike for housing.

Ria de Aveiro · Property

An island in the Ria is for sale again: Puxadoiros goes back to auction on Monday at €690,000

The Ilha dos Puxadoiros, 37 hectares in the heart of the lagoon, went to online auction for the first time this summer with a base price of €750,000. A single bid came in, for €615,000, and its four owners turned it down.

Rather than wait, they have cut the asking price, and the island returns to the same online sale on Monday. What a buyer gets is a working place, not a holiday hideaway: salt pans, fish ponds, support buildings, equipment and an aquaculture business, in what the sale notice calls a landscape "of rare natural beauty".

Potsdam · Energy

Potsdam drills 1,500 metres down for hot water to heat thousands of homes

A 30-metre rig has been boring almost round the clock since July at the Potsdam Süd heating plant. The city wants its district heating free of fossil fuels by 2035, and deep geothermal energy is meant to carry much of it; about €78 million is set aside for this site. Nobody can say in advance how much warm water the rock will give. "You never know if you'll strike it," says EWP project lead Tristan Grüttner, though early results look good; a decision on more wells is due by the end of the year.

A first plant on a former tram depot has heated a new neighbourhood since 2024, and at four megawatts it delivers twice what was expected. The principle is simple: in Germany it gets about three degrees warmer every 100 metres, and much of Brandenburg sits on the North German Basin, whose deep sandstone holds hot water. The water is pumped up, its heat drawn off, and sent back down.

Climate · Nights

The nights that don't cool down: Portugal has gained up to 40 tropical nights a year in two decades

Observador's analysis finds the country has added on average 1.4 tropical nights (a minimum of 20°C or more) every year for the last 20 years. Along the Spanish border the rate is up to four a year, nearly three times as fast; over two decades that means 14 to 40 more warm nights a year, the south worst hit.

Scientists now measure the heat more by rising minimums than by record highs, because the body cannot recover at night. Up to this October there had been 27 days in a row of above-normal minimums. The highest minimum ever recorded is 32°C, in Faro on 26 July 2004; Portalegre reached 31.5°C in June last year.

Greece · Social media

Greece bans social media for under-15s from 1 January, with a state app to prove age

Parliament passed the law after a debate of several hours, making Greece one of the first EU countries with such a ban. Platforms must check users' ages. The government's own "Kids Wallet" app, linked by parents to their child's data, confirms digitally whether the age limit is met without handing the platform the child's personal details.

"It would be very easy to do nothing and wait for the perfect European law," said digital-governance minister Dimitris Papastergiou. France was first, last July, but its Constitutional Council struck the law down in August 2026 as a disproportionate curb on free expression. Athens is betting its version holds.

Eduardo Matos wins the FLAD Drawing Prize for years of drawing Lisbon's Mouraria

The jury of the Fundação Luso-Americana's prize was unanimous, praising work that joins planning with "meticulous" drawing labour, rooted in one territory, the Mouraria, and its passage through time and social change. Porto · Obituary

Álvaro Salazar, the Porto composer who built a home for new music, has died

Contemporary classical · composer, conductor, teacher · 1938–2026

Born in Porto in 1938, Salazar studied at Lisbon's Conservatório Nacional, where he later taught composition, as he did at the ESMAE in Porto. He founded the Oficina Musical, the group devoted to studying and performing contemporary music, which announced his death on Friday.

His old school called him an "unavoidable reference" for the music of the 20th and 21st centuries, for generations of students, performers and composers, and for an open, critical musical culture in Portugal. The farewell was held on Saturday at Matosinhos.
