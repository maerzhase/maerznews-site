Politics · Budget 2027 · Constitution

Montenegro will not carry out the budget changes parliament forced on him: no zero VAT on food, no change to the rent tax

PS and Chega outvoted the government to put both into the 2027 budget. In his first big interview since the election, the prime minister told RTP the government "will not execute" what it is not obliged to, and would let the Constitutional Court decide.

RTP · Observador · Antena 1 · Lusa

The two measures were written into the 2027 budget during the committee votes, with PS and Chega voting together against the AD government: zero VAT on a basket of foods, and the reversal of the government's cut in the autonomous tax rate on rents up to €2,300 a month. Observador adds a bonus for pensioners to the list Montenegro will not apply. "The government does not agree with these decisions and will not execute, within its own competence, what it is not obliged to do," he said on RTP on Wednesday night.

His argument is constitutional. Parliament "wanted to impose on the government the obligation to legislate", which he calls "a constitutional distortion"; if it takes a ruling from the Constitutional Court, "we will not exclude that". PSD MP Hugo Carneiro points to a Court ruling that parliament cannot oblige the government to legislate, and to a gap in the law itself: the Assembleia left it to the government to say which foods would carry zero VAT. On the merits, Montenegro calls the measure "merely symbolic", worth nine cents a person and badly targeted, because it helps everyone alike.

"Don't take this as arrogance on the government's part. If anyone is arrogant, it is parliament, wanting to impose on the government something it cannot."Luís Montenegro, on RTP

The opposition reacted overnight. The PS's Marina Gonçalves called it "institutional disrespect for parliament and for the Portuguese" and said the party expects him to think again; Chega's Bruno Nunes said "the normal functioning of the institutions" was at stake, and André Ventura has called an "announcement" at the party's headquarters at noon today. Chega has not ruled out a censure motion, its first since September. Both parties want President António José Seguro to speak.

The budget's final wording is due on 9 December; then Seguro must sign it, veto it or send it to the Court. Montenegro says there is no political crisis and that he means to govern until 2029. Asked about housing, with prices up 17.6 per cent in 2025 and 16.5 per cent in the second quarter of this year, he said homes "are not built from one day to the next", and promised around €10 billion for housing by 2030.

Aveiro · Health

Aveiro's health workers write to the President: only 46.6 per cent of first appointments now come in time

A "group of professionals" of the ULS Região de Aveiro, which runs the hospital and health centres for ten concelhos around Aveiro, has sent António José Seguro an open letter asking for an urgent audience. They describe "degraded conditions and ever scarcer resources", buildings that no longer suffice and structural decisions put off for years. Their figures come from the diagnosis for the region's Plano Local de Saúde 2030: the share of first consultations held in adequate time fell from 66.3 per cent in 2018 to 46.6 per cent in 2024, and 16,496 of 343,330 registered patients had no family doctor.

The ULS answered within hours that the doctor figure is from August 2025. Today, it says, 10,152 of 343,625 eligible patients, 2.95 per cent, are without one, 38.5 per cent fewer. It did not dispute the waiting-time numbers. The Plano Local de Saúde is in public consultation now; the letter, titled Manifesto Futuro ULSRA, has since gained new signatories, two administrators among them.

Gig economy · rbb exclusive

Germany will miss the EU deadline to protect delivery riders: six days to go, and no draft law

The EU platform-work directive, meant to give riders for apps such as Uber Eats, Lieferando, Wolt and Bolt protection against exploitation, has to be in German law by 2 December. The Federal Labour Ministry told rbb24 Recherche there is not even a ministerial draft yet: "internal coordination" is under way, and the draft will come "soon". It would not give a timetable.

The Left's Pascal Meiser calls an on-time law "practically impossible" and a vote even by the end of the year "simply unimaginable". The delay also holds up a possible ban on subcontracting, which would force platforms to employ couriers directly; the then labour minister Bärbel Bas promised to examine it in December 2025, after rbb found pay far below the minimum wage, missing workers' rights and front men at the platforms' subcontractors. The platforms say their contracts oblige subcontractors to obey the law.

Mathematics · Cryptography

Google's AI proves the number field sieve's speed: factoring in L[1/3], with no unproven assumptions

Six papers went up in Google Research's repository on Wednesday, all written by Cogentic, an agent framework built on Gemini 4, with "no human interaction other than the initial prompt". The headline one gives an algorithm that provably factors any integer N in time LN[1/3, 105]. The fastest factoring method in use, the general number field sieve, has run at that 1/3 exponent since the 1990s, but only heuristically; the best proven bound was Lenstra and Pomerance's L[1/2] from 1992. A Lean 4 formalisation of the whole proof is promised in a companion release.

Nothing gets less safe: the constant 105 against the sieve's roughly 1.9 makes this far slower in practice, so RSA keys are no weaker than on Tuesday. A second paper speeds up discrete logarithms in small-characteristic fields, which modern cryptography already avoids. Scott Aaronson nonetheless writes that he is "virtually certain, from multiple reliable sources" that the AI labs are sitting on bombshells that will break deployed cryptosystems.

Trade · Tariffs

Trump's new tariffs: up to 40 per cent on China, and 14 economies on the list, the EU among them

The US Trade Representative published the proposed duties on Wednesday with reports on each partner's trade practices: up to 40 per cent for China, up to 35 for Vietnam, 20 or 25 for the others, which include the EU, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, India and Switzerland. tagesschau puts the rate for EU goods at 15 per cent; caps Trump has agreed with some partners will lower some rates. The duties need public consultation and possibly hearings, and are not expected before next year.

They replace the "Liberation Day" tariffs the Supreme Court struck down this year, and rest on the USTR's own powers rather than emergency law, a "stronger legal foundation", one economist told the BBC. Jamieson Greer says they "level the playing field"; markets, already rattled by doubts over AI, fell again on Wednesday.
