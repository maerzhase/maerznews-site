Portugal · Weather · Storm Charles

"Only leave home if you really must": storm Charles brings gusts of 100 to 120 km/h on Wednesday, and Lisbon to Faro go orange

The third named storm of the season reaches the mainland from late Wednesday morning, while Bruna is still overhead. Aveiro is on yellow for wind and rain; the government warns of floods, landslides and cuts to power and phones.

Observador · OvarNews · RTP · IPMA warnings as issued 10 November 2026

The government did not wait for the storm. Overnight the interior ministry put out a string of warnings on the state's social accounts: avoid going out during the worst rain and wind, especially on Wednesday, drive with maximum care, and keep torches, other light sources and portable batteries for phones ready in case the electricity goes. Charles could bring gusts above 120 km/h, heavy rain, urban flooding, rivers and streams bursting their banks, and landslides.

Charles is a subtropical system that formed far south in the Atlantic, where tropical cyclones are born, and it is the season's third named storm after Adam, which stayed in the Spanish Mediterranean, and Bruna, still with us. It reaches Madeira first, from Tuesday night to Wednesday morning, with more than 100 mm of rain in 12 hours possible in the mountains, and the mainland from late Wednesday morning. IPMA thinks the centre may cross the coast between Sines and Cabo Carvoeiro; the strongest winds sit on its southern side, which is why the orange runs from Leiria and Lisbon down to the Algarve.

"Only leave home if you really need to, especially at the peaks of rain and wind." Ministry of Internal Administration, 9 November

Up here the forecast is less violent but long. Aveiro district is on yellow for south-westerly gusts up to 90 km/h from noon to 9 pm on Wednesday and for persistent, at times heavy rain from 9 am to 9 pm, after a yellow for thunderstorms until noon today. The sea stays at four to five metres from the west until early Wednesday. Ferries between Madeira and Porto Santo are already cancelled for Wednesday, and IPMA has not ruled out raising warnings further.

"The sea is advancing": an Aveiro MP presses the minister on the coast from Ovar to Espinho, and gets a €1.2bn figure for 2027

In the parliamentary hearing on next year's budget, PSD deputy Carolina Marques told environment minister Maria da Graça Carvalho that coastal erosion is one of the district's biggest worries, recalling that the minister had seen for herself on the beaches of Espinho and Ovar "the obvious reality of the sea advancing". Berlin · Coalition talks

Rent cap, €9 social ticket, free museum Sundays: Berlin's red-green-red negotiators get €220m and a warning not to send wish lists

An internal "budget operating manual" handed to the more than 300 SPD, Green and Linke negotiators, seen by the rbb, sets the rule on page one in bold: every plan must come with its financing. The 24-strong steering group wants only what is "reliably fundable and actually feasible" in the coalition contract.

The priority list, agreed in the exploratory talks, is costed at about €220 million in extra spending: a rent cap at the state-owned housing companies, a four-point business plan, more cycle lanes and bus lanes, climate adaptation and the heat transition, cutting the social ticket to €9 with a matching discounted Deutschlandticket, a rent watchdog, money for addiction prevention, drug-consumption rooms and a cleaner city, a "Kiezkantine", a youth culture card, free museum Sundays and repairs to police and fire stations. Some negotiators already doubt the sum will stretch.

Bigger items sit outside it: socialising the large private landlords, for which the three parties are prepared to pay up to €16 billion in compensation and which would need borrowing, the U-Bahn extensions and the Charité children's hospital. Every other idea must be paid for inside its own policy area, by shifting, cutting or raising money, and the manual suggests combing the budget for projects of the outgoing CDU that no longer fit.

France · Cyber sabotage

France names Iran's Revolutionary Guard as the hand behind the supermarket freezer attacks and wants EU sanctions

Foreign minister Jean-Noël Barrot said state investigators had established that the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps was responsible for the October cyberattacks that made fridges and freezers heat up at 700 sites in France, spoiling tonnes of food. He called it the first large-scale sabotage attack on French soil, of "unprecedented scale and aggressiveness", and summoned Iran's ambassador.

Russia had been the first suspect when similar attacks hit several European countries; Barrot promised sanctions "against all those responsible" at EU level, and the EU has spoken of a forceful response.
