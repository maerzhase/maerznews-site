Germany · Pensions · Berlin

Germany's pension draft saves the "Rente mit 63", but leaving early will cost a third more

Labour minister Alexander Schweitzer's 287-page bill keeps penalty-free retirement after 45 years of contributions, though he says about 27 per cent of those now eligible would lose it, and brings mini-jobs, members of parliament and new self-employed into the state scheme. The Union says it is not enough.

tagesschau · ARD-Hauptstadtstudio · rbb24 Inforadio

One hundred and fifty-seven days after the pension commission reported, the ARD's Berlin studio has the labour ministry's first draft: 287 pages from the SPD-led house of Alexander Schweitzer, minister since October. Its centre is the one thing the commission wanted gone. Penalty-free retirement after 45 years of contributions, still called the "Rente mit 63" although anyone born in 1964 or later can only take it at 65, stays in substance. Fewer people will reach it: years on unemployment or sickness benefit would no longer count towards the 45, while years of raising children or caring for relatives still would. Schweitzer says about 27 per cent of those now eligible would lose the right. Today roughly three in ten new old-age pensions are of this kind.

Leaving early on reduced pay, possible after 35 years, gets dearer. From 1 July 2028 each month early would cost 0.4 instead of 0.3 per cent of the pension, so two years early means 9.6 per cent less for life instead of 7.2, and the earliest age rises from 63 to 64. From 2031 a mechanism would tie the retirement age to life expectancy. Partial retirement would start at 58 instead of 55, and its popular block model, years of full work followed by years off on pay, would end in 2030.

"This reads like a motion for the SPD party conference."Christian von Stetten, CDU, to stern

The draft also widens who pays in. Mini-jobs would be fully insured from 2028, pupils under 21 excepted, against the wishes of employers and of Markus Söder; new self-employed would follow on 1 January 2030, along with board members of public companies and, from the next parliament, members of the Bundestag. From 2028 a second contribution, shared by employer and employee, would be invested in shares on the Swedish model, rising half a point a year to two points in 2031. With all of it, the draft says, the contribution rate would be 20.0 per cent in 2032 instead of 20.4, and 20.6 per cent in 2050 instead of 21.2.

The coalition partner is not convinced. Florian Dorn of the CSU, deputy chair of the commission, says the plan does not stabilise pensions and loads the young; Union parliamentary leader Thorsten Frei sees SPD "maximum demands". The unions are split: the DGB's Yasmin Fahimi welcomes keeping the 45-year pension and insuring mini-jobs but calls the link to life expectancy a pension cut, and ver.di's Frank Werneke says the higher deductions are "a sanction". It is a working draft; the Chancellery and the finance ministry still have to agree. Portugal, which already ties its retirement age to life expectancy, learned the same day that it reaches 67 in 2028 (see Portugal).

Ria de Aveiro · Port

Widen the harbour mouth and the whole Ria feels it: mayors want the impact study to run from Mira to Ovar

The eleven councils of the Região de Aveiro (CIRA) want the environmental impact study for the Port of Aveiro's plan to improve its sea access to cover the entire lagoon, not just the harbour. Since October the port has held the execution project for the works, which would open the bar to ships of up to 228 metres. The mayors fear that a wider, deeper entrance would raise the range and speed of the tides and let more water into the Ria, bringing erosion and shifting sediment along its banks, fields, salt pans and structures, up the northern arm to Ovar. They will take a formal position soon.

They have asked for this for five years: at a CIRA meeting in March 2021 they already warned that the study must not stop at the port area, as its terms of reference say. The port administration calls the project a "strategic investment" under the National Investment Programme 2030, to receive bigger ships and stay competitive, and has promised to work with the councils; it declined to tell the Diário de Aveiro where things stand. The impact study comes next, before the works go out to tender.

Germany · Berlin & Brandenburg

Bundestag · Budget 2027

A €555.9 billion budget passes: electricity gets cheaper for a year, sugary drinks dearer from 2028

The Bundestag approved the 2027 federal budget on Friday in a roll-call vote at third reading: €555.9 billion, €31.4 billion more than this year. The labour ministry alone gets about €203.3 billion, 36.2 per cent of the whole. Defence has €109.7 billion in its regular budget, up from €82.7 billion, plus €29.9 billion from the Bundeswehr special fund, €139.6 billion in all; health and development get less.

The same day MPs cut the electricity tax for everyone in 2027 by about 2.3 cents per kilowatt-hour: a single-person household using 1,500 kWh a year saves €34.50. The cut costs nearly €5 billion, paid from renewable-energy support money the government expects not to need, a bet that power prices stay high. A sugar tax follows in 2028 for drinks with at least 5 grams of sugar per 100 ml: 25 cents a litre up to 7 grams, 30 cents up to 9, 35 cents above. Diet drinks, juices without added sugar and alcohol-free beer are exempt. The tax on spirits rises by 20 per cent next year.

Portugal & the Coast

Pensions · Retirement age

Portugal's retirement age reaches 67 in 2028, and early leavers will lose 18 per cent from January

The statistics institute INE published its provisional figures for life expectancy at 65 on Friday, and that number sets both the normal pension age in the general Social Security scheme and the "sustainability factor" that trims early pensions. By Lusa's calculation the age rises by one month, from 66 years and 11 months in 2027 to 67 in 2028. Anyone who retires early next year will see the pension cut by 18 per cent through that factor alone.

Chega answered within hours: André Ventura called the rise "truly worrying" and proposes writing a rule into the Constitution to stop what he calls an "unlimited increase" in the retirement age. It is the same arithmetic Berlin is now arguing over (see the front page), where a link between retirement age and life expectancy is only planned from 2031.

Privacy · AI agents

Signal now blocks screenshots by default, to keep the operating system's AI agents out of your chats

On iOS 27 the messenger refuses screenshots outright; on Android the block switches on as soon as an AI assistant tries to read the screen. Agents that Apple, Google and Microsoft are building into their systems can lift messages, photos and mail straight off the display, sidestepping each app's own protection, end-to-end encryption included. Signal wants binding interfaces through which apps can forbid AI processing; its strategy chief Udbhav Tiwari told Politico that billions of devices able to spy on their users permanently would make the Snowden revelations "look like a joke", and that governments could simply demand the agents' data instead of breaking encryption.

Regulators are moving too. Ireland's DPC, lead regulator for most US platforms in the EU, is looking at "third-party data", the messages of friends who never agreed to an agent reading them; Britain's ICO warned in October, and Meta signed up to voluntary monitoring when it launched its Muse agent in the UK on Tuesday. Apple, Google and Microsoft declined to comment.

Obituary · France

Jean-Claude Vannier, who arranged Gainsbourg's Melody Nelson, dies at 83

The French composer and arranger died in Paris on Friday, Libération reports. Self-taught on the piano from 18, he scored films with Serge Gainsbourg, Slogan in 1969 and Horse in 1970, before the strings and fuzz of Histoire de Melody Nelson in 1971. The album flopped against what French radio was playing and found its audience a decade later; Eminem and Massive Attack sampled him, and Beck and Mike Patton count him as an influence.

He also arranged for Brigitte Fontaine, Johnny Hallyday, Julien Clerc and Michel Polnareff. His own instrumental record L'enfant assassin des mouches, made in 1973, waited until Finders Keepers reissued it in 2005, with Jarvis Cocker and Jim O'Rourke among its champions; in 2019 came Corpse Flower with Patton. "Singular, unclassifiable, profoundly independent," his daughter Virginie wrote.

Climate · Copernicus

For the first time, the whole planet spent a week more than two degrees warmer than before industry

From 18 to 24 November the global surface temperature stayed above 2°C over the late-19th-century average for seven days running, data from the EU's Copernicus service show, and Copernicus has confirmed it to dpa. The anomaly was 1.9992 degrees on the 18th, 2.0599 the next day, and peaked at 2.467 on Monday the 23rd, almost exactly half as much again as the 1.5-degree goal. Before this week the mark had been passed on only nine days ever: three earlier this month, four in a row in February 2024 and two in November 2023.

Samantha Burgess, Copernicus's strategic lead for climate, says it is not the same as crossing the Paris Agreement's long-term two-degree threshold, which is measured over decades, "but it is a stark foretaste of where our climate is heading". A fully formed El Niño in the Pacific adds to the warming; "El Niño will come and go," she said, but the warming from greenhouse gases stays unless emissions fall.
