Technology · Spaceflight · ULA Vulcan

Ten minutes, forty satellites: ULA's Vulcan suffers the company's first total failure in twenty years and grounds Amazon's answer to Starlink

Sunday's launch was the first of 38 Vulcan flights Amazon bought to build its broadband constellation. The booster stage misbehaved, the rocket began to spin, and the satellites fell back to Earth. The bigger loss is the rocket itself, now grounded for an indefinite time.

Ars Technica · The Verge · heise online · Reuters · as read at 07:00 on 16 November 2026

Vulcan lifted off from Cape Canaveral at 17:40 UTC on Sunday with six solid boosters and 40 Amazon Leo broadband satellites, on the mission Amazon calls LV-01. The boosters did their job. The trouble came on the core stage: its two methane-burning BE-4 engines, built by Jeff Bezos's Blue Origin, should have shut down just short of the five-minute mark but kept firing for at least 30 more seconds, a sign the stage was underperforming. ULA's own telemetry animation then showed the rocket beginning to spin; the Centaur V upper stage separated and tried to steer back on course, and the broadcast ended without comment. ULA later said the vehicle "suffered an anomaly 10 minutes into the flight and failed to deliver the Amazon mission to orbit". Several sources told Ars Technica the fault lay in the booster stage, not the upper stage or the solids.

It is the first total mission failure since Boeing and Lockheed Martin merged their rocket divisions into ULA in 2006. Vulcan had survived booster problems on two of its four earlier flights, and each time months of investigation followed; this one is expected to ground it indefinitely. For Amazon the satellites matter less than the queue behind them: 37 more Vulcan launches, each for 40 or more satellites, in a network that has 396 craft in orbit against a first-generation plan of 3,292, and a factory that has already built its 1,000th. Amazon said there is "risk with any launch, especially inaugural missions".

ULA exists to provide assured access to space. Today, we did not meet that standard. United Launch Alliance statement

Why it matters: the other doors are narrow. Ariane 6 has flown three of Amazon's 24 booked launches; New Glenn, which uses a similar BE-4 engine, has been off its pad since a ground-test explosion in May; and Amazon's ten remaining Falcon 9 missions are bought from SpaceX, the rival it is trying to catch. The Space Force, which relies on Vulcan for about half its national-security launches, is expected to move more of them to SpaceX, and NASA is watching because it has chosen a version of the Centaur stage for its crewed Moon rocket from 2028. Amazon still says it has enough satellites up to start commercial service by the end of the year.

Aveiro · Health

Four operating theatres for 400,000 people: two-thirds of Aveiro hospital's department heads sign a manifesto as the ULS looks for a new board

More than two-thirds of the service directors of the Unidade Local de Saúde da Região de Aveiro, the health unit that runs which runs the district hospital in Aveiro, have put their names to a "Manifesto Futuro da ULSRA". They warn the Health Ministry not to let the crisis over the resignation of board chairman Ricardo Matos, under investigation for corruption, bury what they call the "clinical asphyxia" of a hospital with only four scheduled operating theatres for a population of almost half a million.

The doctors want the next board appointed on the strength of a medium- and long-term strategic project, and call it "politically unacceptable" that the central hospital of a district capital still works in a building that has run out of room. They also take aim at the plans to turn Aveiro into a teaching hospital with the University of Aveiro and the Egas Moniz Health Alliance: medicine courses announced "on paper", they write, mean little while students, lecturers and doctors lack offices, treatment rooms and wards.

Berlin · Housing · Constitution

A federal law to stop Berlin socialising its big landlords would itself be unconstitutional, says a new legal opinion

The federal government wants an "Investitionsschutzgesetz" that would make socialising large housing stocks a "last resort" only, require proof that nothing else works and force compensation at full market value, paid in cash. A legal opinion commissioned by Die Linke, seen first by the ARD capital studio, concludes it would fall in Karlsruhe: the Constitutional Court does not accept federal laws whose only purpose is to block the states from legislating, and Article 15 of the Basic Law explicitly lets them socialise.

The lead author, Würzburg law professor Isabell Feichtner, sat on the Berlin Senate's expert commission that found socialisation of big landlords legally possible. Interior minister Alexander Dobrindt drafted the key points and has spoken of a left "socialisation fetish"; Chancellor Merz promised in July to "prevent" it. Two months later Die Linke won the Berlin election on exactly that programme, though a Linke-led Senate does not yet exist.

Lisbon · Housing · Protest

The tents stay in Restauradores until Saturday, then march with the CGTP, while Nuno Melo calls the campers a "mob of radicals"

The Acampada de Lisboa decided on Sunday to keep its housing protest camp in the Praça dos Restauradores at least until Saturday, when it will formally stand down to join the CGTP union federation's demonstration. Spokeswoman Lou Loução told Lusa the daytime activities are "definitive", but whether anyone sleeps there will be decided night by night at a delegates' plenary by the obelisk; JN counted about 40 tents on a quiet first night.

The camp moved to Restauradores after the row over the Christmas market in the Rossio, which has now opened; Público reports the parish council demanded the removal of market rules that banned protests on the square. CDS leader Nuno Melo drew the line on Sunday between those who "sit in the Assembly" and those who think gathering "a mob of radicals" in one place gives them the right to decide, saying they want to "attack democracy".
