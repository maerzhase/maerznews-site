Portugal · Weather · After the storm

1,630 call-outs, not Kristin's 10,000: Charles split in two before landfall, and the country that shut down for it now argues over why

Proteção Civil logged 1,630 occurrences on the mainland by 6 pm on Wednesday, 881 of them floods. The state of alert ended at midnight and schools and public services reopen today. The government says better safe than sorry; employers say stopping the country cost more than the storm did.

Observador · RTP · Lusa · OvarNews · Jornal de Notícias · as read at 06:30 on 12 November 2026

As Charles neared the mainland it changed shape. IPMA's meteorologists say the depression divided into two separate low-pressure cores, one heading for Lisbon and one for the Algarve, and the energy split with it. The gusts forecast for the centre and north never came, and IPMA cut most districts from red to orange during the day. "That split did not show clearly 48 hours before," climatologist Carlos da Câmara told Observador. The fear had been that Charles would catch up with the slower depression Bruna and the two would feed each other, with most models pointing at Greater Lisbon.

The damage was real but contained: Madeira took 130 km/h winds and landslides that destroyed homes, four people in Armação de Pêra lost their flat to flooding, Faro stayed on red for rain and wind into the evening, and more than half of Lisbon airport's Wednesday flights were cancelled. Prime minister Luís Montenegro conceded the storm "did not have the impact the forecasts anticipated" but defended the shutdown: better to prevent than to cure. Interior minister Luís Neves rejected any "excess of caution", said there could have been a "colossal tragedy" and praised people for staying home "in a very civilised way". The employers' confederation CIP says stopping the country cost more than the storm.

If you like, it is not a defect; it is how the thing is made. Carlos da Câmara, climatologist, on forecast uncertainty

Former IPMA president Miguel Miranda says forecasts are ensembles of possible futures, not certainties: "It is not for meteorology to weigh up the range of forecasts; that falls to civil protection and the government." Around here the day passed quietly. OvarNews called it "a (relatively) normal autumn day", and Aveiro district reported no significant incidents. Only yellow warnings remain this morning, for south-westerly waves of four to five metres off Lisbon, Setúbal, Beja and Faro.

Linha do Norte · Ovar–Espinho

From 13 December the Alfa needs 2h59 from Lisbon to Porto, 11 minutes more, because of the slow zones on our stretch of line

CP's new timetable lengthens the fastest Alfa Pendular from 2h48 to 2h59 between Santa Apolónia and Campanhã. The cause is the many speed restrictions on the Linha do Norte, and the works are concentrated mainly between Ovar and Espinho, the section that runs past Cortegaça and Esmoriz. Even modernised stretches suffer from poor upkeep that holds trains back.

The current job is heavy maintenance on the northern end of the line and is due to finish in about three years. Don't expect faster trains afterwards: the aim is to get back the running conditions the line had before it deteriorated. The works include new passing sidings at Ovar and Esmoriz and replacing level crossings with bridges or underpasses.

Germany · Berlin & Brandenburg

Federal · Budget 2027

Late-night deal: the sugar tax slips to 2028, the plastic tax is shelved, and Merz's electricity-tax cut is off

Hours before today's final session of the Bundestag budget committee, CDU/CSU and SPD settled their open disputes. The levy on sugary drinks, colas, lemonades, iced teas and energy drinks, now starts on 1 January 2028 instead of 1 July 2027, in three sugar bands rather than two, giving makers time to change recipes. Finance minister Klingbeil's planned tax on plastic packaging will not be levied next year.

The cut in electricity tax for everyone, which chancellor Merz pushed hard, is not happening for now: the coalition found no way to pay the roughly €5.4bn it would cost. The deal came as the government's economic advisers raised their forecasts and warned of a need for action "of historic scale" on the public finances.

Portugal & the Coast

Porto · Environment

Porto counts 1,765 animal species but only 5% tree cover, and promises 15,000 new trees by 2030

A city-commissioned survey by Floradata recorded 1,765 species in the municipality between 2021 and 2025, including 201 birds and 448 pollinators, which make up 54% of the invertebrates. The council calls that "a remarkable richness" for a dense city of 41 square kilometres, with three hotspots: the Parque da Cidade, Serralves and Pasteleira, and the Parque Oriental.

The weaknesses are the shortage of green space, a tree canopy covering only 5% of the city and badly fragmented habitat. Its new biodiversity strategy sets targets for 2030: 30 more hectares of public green space, 15 hectares of sealed ground made permeable again, 15,000 trees planted, and habitats and species up 10%. Concrete measures come next.

Heidelberg · Attribution

Called a fake for decades, a "pencil drawing" turns out to be a rare Renoir test print, signed by hand

Henry Keazor, who runs Heidelberg University's forgery study collection with loans from the criminal police of Berlin, Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg, was given the sheet in 2023 from a household clearance. A New York gallery had classed it as a fake. Unpacking it, he saw at once that it was a print, not a drawing.

Paper and microscope analysis points to a lithograph of about 1899 of the actress and singer Amélie Diéterle, matching Renoir's sheet for the "Germinal" portfolio with Degas, van Gogh and Toulouse-Lautrec. Keazor thinks it is a proof pulled from one of two stones, signed in chalk by Renoir himself. It goes on show at the Kurpfälzisches Museum from 26 November.

U2's Carnaval De Luz: their first new album in nearly a decade, born of Larry Mullen Jr.'s injury

Rock · Island · 2026 · Pitchfork 6.5

A neck injury to the drummer put the future of a four-man democracy intact since 1976 in doubt; with Mullen back, the band made a concise, colourful record at lower stakes, with Brian Eno and Jacknife Lee helping. It follows two surprise EPs this year, February's Days of Ash and April's Easter Lily, and borrows its title from a Brazilian festival; about half the songs carry lyrics in Romance languages.

"No such thing as rock'n'roll," Bono sings; "we just needed it to be." Pitchfork hears a band chasing the "defiant joy" it advertised and finding a spark that was missing from the Songs of Innocence and Experience years, from the two-and-a-half-minute opener to the Achtung Baby glam of "La Reina".
