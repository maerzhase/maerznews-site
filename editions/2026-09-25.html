Berlin · After the election

Die Linke votes tonight on talks to run Berlin, and the SPD says it is ready

A Linke–Green–SPD coalition would hold 95 of 158 seats, a comfortable majority. First the party congress has to approve talks, and then the other two want a clear answer on antisemitism.

rbb24 · tagesschau · reported 24–25 September

Die Linke came first in Berlin for the first time on Sunday, with 25.7% of the second votes, and wants its lead candidate Elif Eralp to become Governing Mayor. At a party congress this Friday the delegates vote on whether to invite the Greens and the SPD to exploratory talks. The leadership supports the plan and expects a majority for it, although some members still oppose joining a government at all. If the vote goes through, the talks could start next week. They are not yet coalition negotiations, just a check on whether negotiations could succeed.

The other parties moved on Thursday. The SPD named its state chair Steffen Krach and MP Çaglar to lead its parliamentary group, and Krach said the party is ready to talk with Die Linke. Former Governing Mayors Michael Müller and Walter Momper, both SPD, have warned against it. The CDU made finance senator Stefan Evers its group leader. He is open to a CDU–Green–SPD alliance, which would also have a majority with 82 seats, but says he will wait to see what Die Linke's talks produce.

The Greens say clarity on antisemitism is a precondition for starting talks at all. rbb24, summarising the Green position

Two issues could stop a red–green–red deal. The first is antisemitism. Parts of the Berlin Linke have been criticised again and again for their positions on the Middle East, and both the Greens and the SPD want the party to say plainly that it opposes antisemitism and will protect Jewish life before talks begin. Die Linke and the Greens have already held preliminary meetings and agreed to start with security, the protection of Jewish and queer life, and the fight against antisemitism and racism. On Thursday 150 Jewish Berliners published an open letter defending Die Linke against the accusations. Leading Green Cem Özdemir has warned his party against the coalition.

The second is housing. Die Linke wants to expropriate large housing companies, with compensation, to bring 220,000 more flats into public ownership and slow rising rents. A 2021 referendum backed the idea and the Greens support it, but Krach said during the campaign that there would be no expropriations with him in charge.

Ovar · TasteAtlas

Ovar's pão de ló is named the world's best cake again

Ovar's soft, half-set sponge cake is back at the top of TasteAtlas's world ranking of cakes, the gastronomy site that compiles it from reader ratings. It scored 4.4 points. Sweden's kladdkaka also scored 4.4 but came second, and the Basque cheesecake from San Sebastián (gazta tarta) was third with 4.3. Poland's marcinek and the Czech valašský frgál completed the top five, followed by Polish sernik, Russian medovik, Polish karpatka and the French kouign-amann. This year's list drew 22,680 ratings, of which the site's system accepted 16,832 as genuine. OvarNews quotes chef Robert Kirk calling it "probably the best Portuguese sweet".

Berlin and Brandenburg fares rise 3.72%, but a single AB ticket stays at €4

Buses, trams, U-Bahn, S-Bahn and regional trains in the VBB network will cost 3.72% more on average from 1 January 2027, after a decision by the VBB supervisory board. Technology

A new attack forges RSA signatures without factoring the key

A new paper, "Forging 1024-bit RSA signatures in nearly SNFS time", shows how to forge RSA signatures on ordinary computers without factoring the key, which cryptographers did not expect. Against the already-deprecated 1024-bit keys, the attack took a few months on an academic CPU cluster. Until now, factoring such a key was thought to need resources only nations or very large companies have. Widely used RSA implementations remain safe, but the method cuts the computing needed by orders of magnitude. That is another reason to move off RSA before quantum computers arrive.
