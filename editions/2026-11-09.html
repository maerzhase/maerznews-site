Germany · Democracy · 9 November

87% of Germans call democracy a good system; only one in three thinks it works, and in Berlin and Brandenburg 70% are unhappy with it

A large ARD survey published on the anniversary of the fall of the Wall finds no state where a majority is satisfied with how democracy works. Berliners and Brandenburgers want citizens' assemblies and national referendums more than anyone.

tagesschau · rbb24 · ARD "Deine Meinung zählt!" · Infratest dimap · 9 November 2026

The headline number is reassuring: 87 per cent of people in Germany say democracy is a good form of government, and voters of every party polled agree by a majority, from 75 per cent of AfD voters to over 90 per cent of everyone else's. Four in five say elections to parliament are free and fair, and 72 per cent that minority rights are protected. For comparison, the ARD notes, only three in five Americans told Ipsos before last week's midterms that they trusted the count.

The practice is another matter. Only one in three thinks democracy works well in Germany. In the former East 72 per cent are "less" or "not at all" satisfied, in Berlin and Brandenburg 70 per cent, and even in North Rhine-Westphalia, the most content state, just 44 per cent are satisfied. AfD voters are the unhappiest (92 per cent dissatisfied), followed by Linke voters (65); the happiest are not the governing CDU and SPD's voters but the Greens'. Around a third doubt that courts treat everyone equally or that the media report freely, and only half think politics provides for people's social security; among the under-30s six in ten don't. Among 16- to 29-year-olds, 19 per cent call democracy "not such a good" system.

"Listening to other opinions is essential in a democracy." Judith Schatz, 31, from Augsburg, one of 80,000 who joined the ARD campaign (tagesschau)

Asked for the biggest danger, a third (34 per cent) name right-wing extremism, the top answer in West and East; one in six (16) names a dysfunctional state. Compulsory voting is rejected six to three. But 71 per cent want nationwide referendums and 63 per cent a randomly drawn citizens' council that could put proposals on the Bundestag's agenda, and in Berlin and Brandenburg support rises to 78 and 72 per cent. Almost everyone (94 per cent) says people who disagree must keep talking; 28 per cent say they want as little as possible to do with them.

Cortegaça & Esmoriz · The coast

New rules for 140 km of coast from Ovar to Marinha Grande: Cortegaça's campsite beach and Esmoriz's Praia Velha dos Pescadores are on the list

The government has approved a revision of the Programa da Orla Costeira Ovar–Marinha Grande, the plan that governs how the beaches on our stretch of coast are classified, used and protected. Seven years after it took effect, the environment agency APA found inconsistencies and a coastline that had moved on; this is "one of the zones of Europe most vulnerable to erosion", the government says.

Eight beaches are newly created or reclassified, and three of them are on Ovar's shore: the Praia Velha dos Pescadores in Esmoriz, Cortegaça/Parque de Campismo by the campsite, and Furadouro Sul. The beach-management regulation and the intervention plans for each beach are rewritten too, to balance safety, nature and the businesses that live off the sea. Environment minister Maria da Graça Carvalho says the update lets the state "respond more effectively to the risks of coastal erosion"; it changes only the management rules, not the councils' land-use plans.

Federal · Head of state

Merz breaks his silence and backs Ilse Aigner: the CDU presidium names her for president, and she would be the first woman in the office

For days CDU insiders had whispered her name while the chancellor insisted he was "sticking to the agreed procedure". On Sunday evening the CDU presidium met as planned and came out for the Bavarian state-parliament speaker; Merz confirmed there that he supports her nomination. The party's federal executive is due to follow today, and Merz and Aigner will appear before the press together.

Merz had wanted a candidate who could win broad support both in the Federal Convention and among the public, and after his stumbling cabinet reshuffle in the summer he wanted to get this one right. Tagesschau suggests the delay may also have been pique: Markus Söder, as CSU leader, put Aigner forward back in April without consulting him. The two know each other well from the Tegernsee, where Merz has a holiday home and Aigner her constituency.

Portugal & the Coast

Weather · Storm Bruna

Bruna stalls north of the Azores: up to 200 mm of rain by Friday, five-metre waves on the west coast, and a deeper storm may follow on Wednesday

The whole mainland is under yellow warnings for rain, thunder, wind or rough sea, with Guarda and Castelo Branco on orange overnight. The depression is barely moving, and IPMA expects its fronts to keep coming until Friday the 13th in three bursts: overnight into Monday, Monday afternoon to Tuesday afternoon, and Wednesday, when some models bring a new and much deeper low close to the Iberian Peninsula. If it is named, it will be Charles.

Expect southerly gusts to 75 km/h on the coast, mostly in the North and Centre, local gusts of 70–90 km/h and even small tornadoes in thunderstorms, and waves of four to five metres on the west coast, which takes in our beaches. Madeira has already been hit hard: civil protection logged more than 200 incidents, and a 37-year-old lifeguard is missing in Funchal.

Under Siege (2026): Netflix's biggest Colombian series since One Hundred Years of Solitude retells the 1985 Palace of Justice siege

Historical drama · limited series, 8 episodes · Spanish · Netflix since 6 Nov · no ratings yet

It landed on the anniversary: on 6 November 1985 M-19 guerrillas seized the courthouse in Bogotá, and the army's retaking of the building left at least 94 people dead or missing, Supreme Court justices among them. Known at home as Palacio, the series comes from Dynamo, the company behind One Hundred Years of Solitude, with Natalia Santa as showrunner and Jayro Bustamante (La Llorona) among the directors; Natalia Reyes, Carlos Manuel Vesga and Robinson Díaz lead.

Climate · COP31

The UN climate summit opens in Antalya with the world on course for 2.8 degrees

Delegations from almost 200 countries meet in Turkey for two weeks, until 20 November, under COP president Murat Kurum, the Turkish environment minister; Germany's delegation is led by environment minister Carsten Schneider. Ten years after the Paris Agreement set a 1.5-degree limit, the UN's own projection says current policies lead to 2.8 degrees by the end of the century.

After a year of record wildfires in Europe, deadly heat and flash floods in Nepal and China, the central question is the old one: does the world move on phasing out fossil fuels? "Everyone must do more, and now," UN secretary-general António Guterres said on the eve of the talks.
