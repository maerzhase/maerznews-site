Porto · Housing

After a 3 a.m. police charge, Porto's housing protest grew into a march of thousands, then stood down over armed infiltrators

Organisers count 39 people hurt when the PSP cleared the tents from Praça D. João I on Thursday. The government calls the operation legitimate; the opposition wants an inspection. Saturday brings new protests in Porto and Lisbon.

RTP · Jornal de Notícias · Público · Observador · Porto, 15–16 October 2026

The camp in Praça D. João I, in the middle of the Baixa, has been the centre of a new housing movement since last weekend. City police took the first tents down peacefully on Monday. On Wednesday a popular assembly voted to stay and sleep in tents again, while the mayor, Pedro Duarte (PSD/CDS/IL), promised to be "absolutely implacable" about any new ones. The tents went back up that night.

At about 03:00 on Thursday the PSP moved in. The organisers say 39 people were hurt, two of them seriously; Jornal de Notícias counted more than 30. The police confirmed one injured officer and injuries among the protesters, and blamed "resistance" to the order to leave. The new national police director, Pedro Moura, said "a more muscular intervention" had been needed; the interior minister, Luís Neves, called it legitimate, and Prime Minister Luís Montenegro, from the EU summit in Brussels, asked the protesters not to be "selfish".

"It repels me that the answer should be disproportionate violence, in a police operation that seemed to me badly prepared." Manuel Pizarro, PS councillor, Porto city council

Pizarro wants the interior ministry's inspectorate and the Ombudsman to examine who ordered the operation; a criminal complaint and an IGAI filing have been lodged, and the PCP, Bloco and Livre have put questions to the minister. On Thursday evening some two thousand people marched behind the slogan "open houses, not heads"; the rapper Capicua and Pedro Abrunhosa were among the musicians who spoke out.

The night did not end in the square. At 01:20 on Friday the spokesman, Pedro Manuel, told protesters to go home "together, in groups": the movement says it had spotted about eleven far-right infiltrators carrying knives, one of whom was taken away by ambulance, and that the police did nothing when told. Its six demands stand: repeal the rent law, end evictions and purchases by "vulture funds", build public housing, bring empty buildings back into use, and cap rents.

Ovar · Housing

Ovar's ceiling for an "affordable" two-bedroom flat is €560 a month

The government has published the maximum rents a landlord in each municipality may charge under the simplified affordable-rent scheme and still claim its tax breaks. In Ovar the caps run from €336 for a studio and €430 for a one-bedroom to €560 (T2), €690 (T3), €755 (T4) and €884 (T5).

That puts Ovar above Santa Maria da Feira (€302 to €796) and above Estarreja, Águeda and Murtosa in the Aveiro region, but below Aveiro and Ílhavo. Landlords who sign up can be exempted from IRS or IRC on the income; in return, leases for a permanent home must run at least three years, and temporary ones, for students or posted workers, at least three months. The ordinance is signed by the finance minister, Joaquim Miranda Sarmento.

Berlin · Coalition

Two days after the pause, Berlin's red-green-red talks are back, and the Greens vote on Monday

The exploratory talks that the SPD and Greens broke off on Tuesday evening resumed on Thursday at the Haus der Statistik and ran well into the evening, far longer than planned, with another round set for Friday morning. The subjects now on the table are the budget and the socialisation of large private housing companies, the Left's flagship demand.

The Greens have called a small party congress for Monday, where 49 delegates will vote on moving from sounding-out to formal coalition negotiations with the Left and the SPD. The SPD's state executive will decide the same question and could be convened "promptly", its state party manager Sven Heinemann told rbb. It is still unclear whether the Left will bring forward its own congress, planned for 31 October; its rules require longer notice for inviting delegates.

The three parties hold 91 of the 153 seats in the new Abgeordnetenhaus. The national row over the Left has not gone away: on Thursday the Constitutional Court also ordered a federal minister to pull a video attacking the party (see left).

Lisbon · Streets

Lisbon will switch off its shared e-scooters

"This is a disgrace and it is going to end," Lisbon's mayor Carlos Moedas (PSD) said in a video on Thursday, announcing that the shared-scooter system will be suspended. The city says it is collecting more than 1,200 badly parked or abandoned scooters a month, and Moedas pointed to riders going the wrong way, rising accidents and many minors riding.

He told the Now channel that the problems are both parking and safety, and that he will take an immediate suspension to the next council meeting. Moedas had already promised action against dangerous riding on 28 September; this time the plan is to stop the service outright.

AI · Open weights

Meta releases Muse Spark Open, 816 billion parameters, tuned with Apple to run on two Mac Studios

Meta published the weights of what it calls the most capable US open-weight model to date: natively multimodal, 816 billion parameters in total with 42 billion active per token, and a one-million-token context window, aimed at coding, agentic work and long multi-step tasks.

The unusual part is the partner. Meta and Apple worked for months to make it the first model of its class to run on a desk, on two M5 Ultra Mac Studios with Apple's MLX framework; Zuckerberg and Apple's new CEO John Ternus cheered each other on X. Weights are on Hugging Face, SageMaker, Google Cloud and Microsoft Foundry, and it runs in vLLM, SGLang, llama.cpp and MLX.

Black Sea · NATO

Drones set two tankers ablaze off Romania, and seven crew are missing

The San Marino-flagged chemical tanker Wine Trader was hit on Thursday morning 13 nautical miles east of the Danube mouth at Sulina, and seven of its 12 crew are missing in rough seas. A second tanker, the Yara J, was struck there on Wednesday, Romania's President Nicușor Dan confirmed, after Russian drones were detected in Romanian airspace. If Russia is confirmed as the attacker, the strikes reached inside a NATO member's territorial waters for the first time; at least seven ships have now been hit in NATO countries' economic zones. Moscow said it hit two cargo ships carrying military cargo to Odesa. After meeting Bulgaria's and Romania's leaders, NATO's Mark Rutte said counter-drone capabilities on the eastern flank have been increased.
