Berlin · Housing · Coalition

Berlin's would-be red-green-red coalition agrees to socialise every private landlord with more than 10,000 flats

The Left, the Greens and the SPD closed their exploratory talks on Saturday with a paper that raises the 2021 referendum's threshold from 3,000 flats to 10,000, caps compensation at €16 billion and promises a law by mid-2028. Coalition talks could start in early November.

rbb24 · tagesschau · Berlin, 17 October 2026

Elif Eralp (Left), Werner Graf (Greens) and Steffen Krach (SPD) presented the joint paper on Saturday evening after four weeks of talks. Its biggest change concerns the socialisation of housing: the 2021 referendum, which a majority of Berliners backed, named every company with 3,000 flats or more; the new line is 10,000. The companies would be compensated, with the total capped at €16 billion. Cooperatives and public-interest landlords stay exempt, as do flats built after 1 January 2021 or still to come.

It is a concession to the SPD, which had grown wary of the plan. "We will work on socialisation from day one," Eralp told the rbb24 Abendschau, but she warned that it takes time; meanwhile rents at the state housing companies would be capped at 2% rises, and a new authority would go after excessive rents. The paper also promises permits for 100,000 new flats, a cheaper social ticket, the return of free museum Sundays, no extension of the A100 motorway and no building on the edge of Tempelhofer Feld. Higher taxes and fees are to bring in at least €400 million more.

"A declaration of surrender by the SPD and the Greens to a radical, in parts antisemitic Left party." Stefan Evers, CDU leader in Berlin

The three also agreed on a Bundesrat initiative for an AfD ban and tighter parliamentary control of the domestic intelligence service, which the Left had wanted abolished. The antisemitism declaration that briefly halted the talks will go into the coalition treaty, with sanctions up to expulsion from the group. The Greens hold a small party congress on Monday; the Left, because of Berlin's autumn holidays, not until 31 October. Their 91 seats would be 14 more than a majority. Chancellor Merz, addressing the Junge Union on Saturday, told the SPD and Greens: "Stop going down this road."

Aveiro · Industry

Navigator wants a second tissue-paper machine at Cacia: €115 million, 41 new jobs, almost double the output

The pulp and paper group has put the environmental impact study for "paper machine 2" at its Cacia complex, on the Vouga just north of Aveiro, out to public consultation until the end of November. It would raise the site's tissue capacity from 92,345 tonnes a year (254 a day) to 176,441 tonnes (483 a day), on a 24-hour, year-round basis.

The company says it chose Cacia for the synergies: its own pulp, existing infrastructure and on-site water, gas, steam and effluent treatment. The plan adds three buildings (a pulp-bale store, a stock-preparation and machine hall and an automated reel warehouse with loading docks), a two-tier container yard and 15 lorry bays. Building and installation would take about 15 months from the first quarter of 2027.

Federal · Coalition

"There are limits for me too": Merz tells the Junge Union the coalition with the SPD is not worth any price

At the CDU youth wing's Deutschlandtag the chancellor said he wants the coalition to succeed, but will not put its survival above "every conceivable political achievement". It must be clear, he said, "who in this coalition is the bigger partner and who the smaller". He expects a cabinet decision on pensions this year and wants the pension commission's proposals implemented "as they lie on the table", including the end of penalty-free retirement after 45 years of contributions, which the SPD and three CDU state premiers oppose.

A year after a bruising row with the same delegates, this time the pension passage drew applause. Merz also warned CDU members in Saxony-Anhalt, where some may have helped elect an AfD speaker, that anyone who votes such people into office "has no place in the CDU", and turned on the SPD and Greens in Berlin for talking to the Left. JU leader Johannes Winkel was re-elected with his best result yet; SPD women's chief Wegge, meanwhile, accused the Union of "systematic sexism" in the row over Bärbel Bas's resignation.

Porto · Lisbon · Housing

The housing camp spreads: tents go up in Lisbon's Rossio as Porto's protesters return to their square

Saturday's CGTP marches drew thousands in Lisbon, Porto and Coimbra against rents, low wages and the 2027 budget. In Lisbon, dozens of tents went up in the Rossio, and an assembly of several hundred approved six demands: repeal the new rental law, stop evictions and demolitions without rehousing, curb property-fund buying, build more public housing, take empty buildings for homes and cap rents. Some hundreds stayed past the 00:30 legal limit; the PSP said it did not intend to intervene.

In Porto, the union march ended in Praça D. João I to applause for the camp, two days after the police charge that injured at least 37 people. The movement voted to camp there again, possibly beyond 20 October, and demands Mayor Pedro Duarte's resignation; he has invited them to a meeting at the town hall, and housing minister Miguel Pinto Luz, who backs him, said that if people protest "it is because there is pain". The unions' next date: 24 November, outside parliament, the day of the budget vote.

Portugal · Ideas

"Money is a disease": Yanomami shaman Davi Kopenawa brings his people's dreams to Portugal

On his first visit to the country, the leader who has fought illegal gold mining on Yanomami land for decades is presenting "A Queda do Céu" ("The Falling Sky"), the book he wrote with anthropologist Bruce Albert. He speaks at FÓLIO in Óbidos, then gives the opening lecture of the doctoral programmes at Coimbra's Centro de Estudos Sociais on Monday, 19 October, and speaks on the Yanomami "school of dreams" at NOVA FCSH in Lisbon on the 21st.

His argument: city people have stopped dreaming, a central form of knowledge for his people, because their cities are lit all night, and they chase wealth by gutting the earth. "If the forest dies, the water dies, the land dies, then our sky will burst," he told RTP. "You should dream the land."

France · Unrest

1,200 arrests and Limoges's town hall in flames as France's cost-of-living protests turn violent

More than half a million people marched on Saturday's national day of protest, joining the pupils and students who have been on the streets since late September. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu said rioters had targeted "the centres of power", the Élysée, the government's seat and the National Assembly; 150 police officers were hurt, and in Saint-Étienne a 17-year-old lost a hand to a police grenade, Le Figaro reports.

Arsonists largely destroyed Limoges's historic town hall; in Marseille rioters got into the town hall and looted shops, Lyon's tourist office was set alight, and Avignon's prefecture courtyard was stormed. The interior ministry blames black blocs of the ultra left. Macron promised the perpetrators would be prosecuted; the government has no majority for the cuts its finance minister wants.
