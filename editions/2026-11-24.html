Estarreja · Industry · Lego

Lego will make its bricks in Estarreja: an eighth factory, up to €1 billion and more than 1,000 jobs, open in 2030

The Danish toymaker chose a 60-hectare site twenty kilometres south of Cortegaça for what it calls its most automated plant yet, built to supply Europe, the Middle East and Africa.

Estarreja · Lusa · Notícias ao Minuto · OvarNews · RTP

Lego's chief operating officer, Carsten Rasmussen, told Lusa the investment "can reach €1,000 million in the coming years, up to 2035", and that the plant will employ "1,000, or maybe a few more" skilled workers once it runs at full capacity. Building is due to start in mid-2027 and finish in 2030. The project was presented on Monday in the salão nobre of Estarreja's town hall, with the mayor, the prime minister and the economy minister.

Lego Manufacturing Portugal will do the whole job, moulding, processing and packing, in a 115,000 m² building with a vertical warehouse on a 60-hectare site, about 120 football pitches by Lusa's count, laid out so it can grow later. It will be the group's eighth factory and its fourth in Europe, after Denmark, Hungary and the Czech Republic; the others are in Mexico, China, Vietnam and the United States, where a plant opens next year. Lego calls it its most advanced factory for automation and digitalisation.

"The choice of Portugal was not easy, but it was very much the right one."Carsten Rasmussen, Lego COO, to Lusa

Rasmussen named closeness to the markets, transport links, a commitment to sustainability and skilled local workers as the reasons. Lego says it will cover all of the plant's yearly energy with renewables, around 80 per cent of it from solar panels and batteries on the site, reuse rainwater, and aim for LEED Platinum certification. For anyone between Ovar and Aveiro it means a thousand factory jobs within commuting distance, but not before 2030.

Aveiro · Hospital · Budget

Parliament writes the Aveiro hospital's expansion back into the 2027 budget, twice

Two amendments passed in the budget's committee votes commit the government to keep investing in the renovation, expansion and modernisation of the Hospital de Aveiro, which the government had left out of its original 2027 proposal. The PS deputies elected for Aveiro got theirs approved although PSD and CDS voted against it; PSD and CDS then passed their own text with the same aim, which the PS supported.

The same commitment had been written into the 2026 budget on a PS proposal and was not renewed this year. The votes came days after a petition, "Mais saúde para o Hospital de Aveiro", passed a thousand signatures in four days, and after Chega's convention backed a motion for a central hospital in Aveiro. The wording secures a political commitment, not a sum: the financing of the works is still to be found.

Berlin · Die Linke

Berlin's Left votes today on expelling Vanessa Emde, the case that hangs over its coalition talks

The Linke group in the Abgeordnetenhaus meets this afternoon to decide whether to throw out the Neukölln MP, 45. Expulsion needs a two-thirds majority of the 44 members, at least 30 votes; the leadership around Elif Eralp says there is no longer any basis for working with her.

Emde signed the declaration all Linke mandate holders made, which includes Israel's right to exist, then distanced herself from it: she argues that only people, not states, have a right to exist, which critics read as antisemitic. She is also accused of not distancing herself from Ibrahim Ibrahim of the VPNK, an umbrella the Verfassungsschutz says includes Hamas and the PFLP; he rejects the charge. The SPD has demanded her expulsion as the Left, the Greens and the SPD negotiate a coalition, and at the last party conference she spoke at a pro-Palestinian rally against that coalition.

Portugal & the Coast

Politics · Budget 2027

Chega says it will vote against the 2027 budget in this afternoon's final vote

Asked on the eve of the vote whether Chega would vote against, even after getting several changes through, André Ventura answered "Of course". He says Chega approved 70 per cent of the PSD and CDS amendments, and accuses Luís Montenegro's minority government of "artificial victimisation" and of trying to provoke a crisis. With Chega voting no, the government needs the PS at least to abstain.

Deputies voted on more than 2,800 amendments in the committee stage, which ended in the early hours. PS and Chega together raised GNR and PSP shift and residence supplements and widened the social energy tariff against the government's wishes. PSD and CDS amendments halved the golden-visa minimum for investment in scientific research to €250,000, and VAT on childminders falls from 23 to 6 per cent. Three unions have called a general strike for the day of the vote.

Cascais · Paula Rego

Paula Rego's Dogs of Barcelona, unseen for 18 years, goes back on show

The 1964 painting is unveiled on Wednesday at 18:00 at the Casa das Histórias Paula Rego in Cascais, where it will stay on deposit. It grew from a Times report of 25 November 1963 on hundreds of stray dogs poisoned in Lloret de Mar, near Barcelona, and the women who tried to stop it; Rego tied it to the dictatorships of Salazar and Franco.

First shown in London as The Dogs of Barcelona, it hung in her first solo show in Lisbon in 1965 as "Cães vadios", stray dogs, to get past the censor. The date marks 63 years since the article.
