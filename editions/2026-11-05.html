Germany · Bavaria · Berlin-Mitte

Hubert Aiwanger, Bavaria's deputy premier, is found dead in a Berlin hotel room on the day his party opened its capital office

The Free Voters' leader, 55, skipped the opening because he felt unwell and stopped answering his phone. Berlin's prosecutors and homicide squad have opened a routine death inquiry.

tagesschau · ARD-Hauptstadtstudio · rbb24 · 5 November 2026

Aiwanger had come to Berlin for the opening of the Free Voters' office in the capital on Wednesday, but did not turn up, saying he felt unwell, and stayed in his hotel in Mitte. Later calls to his mobile went unanswered, Brandenburg's Free Voters leader Péter Vida told rbb24; he was found lifeless in his room. Bild broke the news; Berlin's public prosecutor confirmed the death late in the evening. Together with the State Criminal Police Office's homicide unit it has opened a death inquiry, to establish "the specific cause and course of death" and rule out third-party involvement. Police were outside the hotel in force on Wednesday evening.

He had made his party matter. The Free Voters first entered the Bavarian Landtag in 2008, with Aiwanger as group leader; ten years later they went into coalition with Markus Söder's CSU, and he became economy minister and Söder's deputy. Only this spring, under his leadership, the party took many district chief posts from the CSU in Bavaria's local elections. In 2023 he was at the centre of the "leaflet affair", over an antisemitic leaflet from his schooldays that he denied writing; Söder kept him, his brother later said he was the author, and the party won its best result weeks later.

"The sudden death of Hubert Aiwanger is an absolute tragedy. We are all deeply shocked." Markus Söder, Bavaria's minister-president, on X

"Bavaria will be different without him," said the Free Voters' group leader Florian Streibl. Political opponents spoke of a combative man: "contentious, but undeniably a born politician who shaped Bavaria," said the CSU's Alexander Hoffmann; Baden- Württemberg's premier Cem Özdemir called him "a contentious politician with rough edges who fought for his convictions". The chair of the Bavarian party he led is now vacant, and Söder must fill the economy ministry and the deputy's post.

Espinho · Feira · Transport

Porto's metro is told to head south, to Espinho, Feira, São João da Madeira and Oliveira de Azeméis, but it will stop at the Ovar line

The infrastructure minister, Miguel Pinto Luz, said on Wednesday that he has mandated Metro do Porto to expand the network into the southern municipalities of the Porto metropolitan area, naming Espinho, São João da Madeira, Santa Maria da Feira and Oliveira de Azeméis. Bringing their 400,000 residents onto the metro would be "absolutely transformative", he said: "We often forget that there is a metropolitan area to the south, reaching as far as Vale de Cambra."

Ovar is not on the list, OvarNews notes drily: "if you thought the metro could reach Ovar, think again." The concelho belongs to the Aveiro region, not to Porto's. And it is a start, not a plan: the metropolitan area itself speaks of "studies" to assess the expansion, which it lists as São João da Madeira, Oliveira de Azeméis, Feira and Vale de Cambra. For Cortegaça the nearest stop, if one ever comes, would be in Espinho, a few kilometres up the coast.

Köpenick · Industry

Tesla opens a €100 million engineering centre in an old Köpenick factory, with 250 staff now and 500 planned

Tesla has opened a European development centre in a converted factory in Berlin-Köpenick, not far from its car plant in Grünheide. Engineers and technicians work in labs on materials, drivetrains and vehicle development, and a large hall tests parts such as airbags and battery cells for safety. The site starts in partial operation with more than 250 staff, who come from Tesla's existing centre in Tilburg in the Netherlands and other labs.

A permit for the second stage has been applied for, which would take the site to about 500 engineers and technicians; the total investment is €100 million. Berlin's mayor Kai Wegner (CDU) was expected at the opening. Grünheide is still Elon Musk's only car factory in Europe, and is due to grow too.

Lisbon · Education

Maths for everyone to the end of school, and university fees unfrozen: the education minister's budget package

Every secondary pupil will take mathematics from the 10th to the 12th year, whatever course they choose, the education minister Fernando Alexandre told parliament's budget and education committees on Wednesday. "We will have a common trunk in which maths is compulsory," with flexible options after, vocational courses included. The new curriculum goes to public consultation by the end of 2026 but only takes effect in 2028/29; a merger of the first and second cycles, the first six years of school, will be piloted in some schools next September.

From 2027/28 the government also wants to lift the freeze on bachelor's tuition fees, unchanged since 2020, and index them to inflation; universities would have to put 15% of fee income into student support, which the minister calls "some justice within the system". The Communists' Paula Santos accused him of making students pay for their own grants. He also said 400 new enrolment requests reached public schools last week alone, many for children not yet in the country.

AI · Developer tools

OpenAI buys Pierre, the Git-infrastructure start-up already inside Codex, and edges onto GitHub's turf

OpenAI is acquiring the Pierre Computer Company, a US start-up founded in 2023 that builds cloud infrastructure for software development. It is best known for Code Storage, Git hosting for huge repositories and very fast updates, which OpenAI had already wired into Codex, its coding agent. Pierre's other libraries, "Trees" for searching a project's files fast and "Diffs" for showing changes, come too. "The systems around Git need to evolve," OpenAI says.

This week OpenAI also showed "Pando", a Git back end built for coding agents. Together it starts to look like a GitHub of its own, which would pit OpenAI against Microsoft, its biggest investor and GitHub's owner since 2018. Terms were not disclosed; Pierre's open-source projects stay open.

Germany · Obituary

Janosch, who drew the little tiger, the little bear and the Tigerente for generations of German children, has died at 95

The illustrator and writer died on Tuesday. His "Oh, wie schön ist Panama", in which the little bear and the little tiger set off for a country that smells of bananas, made him famous; he also gave the world the striped wooden "tiger duck" and Onkel Popov, the loner who can fly a little. He wanted his stories to make children resilient. Of "Panama" he once said, gruffly, that he had set out to make a kitsch book: "There has to be a cuddly bear, the bear has to go on a journey, and he has to have a friend."

His own childhood in Upper Silesia was brutal: his father, a drinker, named him Horst after the Nazi Horst Wessel. In later life he withdrew to a crooked red house in the hinterland of Tenerife. President Steinmeier called him "a companion through life for countless children".

Classical · Obituary

Herbert Blomstedt, who recorded all of Beethoven's symphonies again at 90, has died at 99

Conductor · Staatskapelle Dresden 1975–85 · Leipzig Gewandhaus 1998–2005

The Swedish conductor died at home in Gothenburg on Wednesday, with his family, the Leipzig Gewandhaus said. As a young man he pioneered historically informed performance in Sweden; he went on to lead the Dresden Staatskapelle and to serve as Gewandhauskapellmeister in Leipzig, and with the Gewandhaus Orchestra he re-recorded the complete Beethoven symphonies at 90. Germany gave him its Grand Cross of Merit with Star in 2022.

The older he got, the more audiences were drawn to a famously modest man: a Seventh-day Adventist who never drank and lived strictly vegetarian, beginning each day with water and Swedish porridge. "I am a musician among musicians," he said. "I give something, and they give me something back."

Hong Kong · Courts

Joshua Wong, face of Hong Kong's democracy movement, gets another ten years, two months before he was due out

Wong, who turned 30 last month, was sentenced on Thursday for colluding with foreign forces under the national security law Beijing imposed in 2020. He had already spent six years in prison and was due to be freed in January for an earlier subversion sentence; only two months and two days of the new term run concurrently with it. He pleaded guilty in September. Prosecutors said he conspired with other campaigners, including the self-exiled Nathan Law, to lobby for foreign sanctions against Beijing.

Dozens of Hongkongers queued for hours for a seat in the public gallery; Wong, visibly thinner, nodded to them and took notes as the judge read the reasons. Hundreds of activists and ordinary residents have been arrested under the law. Critics say it has silenced dissent; the authorities say it keeps the city stable.
