Portugal · Aviation

Lufthansa beats Air France-KLM to TAP, paying €1.2 billion for up to half the airline

The Council of Ministers chose the German group on Wednesday. The State keeps 50.1% for now; Lufthansa's bid already prices a second step that would hand it the rest by 2028, partly in its own shares.

Government of Portugal · Forbes Portugal · Expresso · ECO · Observador · Lisbon, 14 October 2026

"With this decision, the government successfully concludes the first phase of the privatisation of our airline," Luís Montenegro said after Wednesday's Council of Ministers. Lufthansa offers €1,201 million for 44.9% of TAP, plus the 5% set aside for staff if they do not take it up. Air France-KLM, the only other bidder left, offered €1,038 million; the government says the German bid was 16% higher and carried less uncertainty.

The offer values the whole company at €2,552 million. A second, separate phase, which the government says could come by 2028, would bring another €863 million in cash and €488 million in Lufthansa shares, making the Portuguese State the combined group's second-largest shareholder with about 5%. Forbes Portugal puts the price at 6.4 times TAP's 2025 EBITDA; independent valuations since 2023 ranged from €700 million to €1,700 million.

"This is the best choice for the company's future and the one that best protects the national interest." Luís Montenegro, Prime Minister

The commitments matter close to home. Lufthansa promises to keep the TAP brand, its headquarters and the Lisbon hub, to base eight aircraft in Porto, and to invest in a pilot school at Tancos and an innovation centre in Aveiro; Lufthansa Technik is already building a components plant of about €300 million and 700 jobs at the Lusopark in Santa Maria da Feira, which the company says goes ahead regardless. The government counts 5,500 direct and indirect jobs in all. The State appoints five of nine board members, including the chair.

Final negotiations and regulatory approval still lie ahead and could run into 2028. Montenegro's stated aim is to win back the more than €3.2 billion of public money put into TAP; the PS accused the government of rushing by already dangling a full sale, and Air France-KLM promised to compete hard from the outside.

Válega · Farming

The district's farmers drive their tractors from Válega to Aveiro: diesel up 50% in six months

Crop growers, cattle breeders and dairy producers gathered in Válega on Wednesday morning and set off on a horn-blaring tractor convoy down the EN109 to Aveiro, called by the União dos Agricultores e Baldios do Distrito de Aveiro. In the afternoon they handed their demands to the Secretary of State for Agriculture as he opened the AgroVouga fair.

Their president, Carlos Alves, said farm diesel has risen 50% in six months while Portugal imports 70% of its food, and accused the government of abandoning agriculture. The list: stop the fuel rises, lower input costs, fair prices at the farm gate, "a dignified wage" for small farmers, less paperwork, and no imports, such as South American beef, made to lower standards than their own. No organiser or outlet gave a turnout.

Berlin · Children online

The cabinet wants no social media under 13, with age checks through a state wallet from 2027

Family Minister Karin Prien's cornerstones, adopted on Wednesday and built on 56 recommendations from an expert commission, work on two levels. In Brussels, Germany will argue in the EU Kids Act talks for no social-media access below 13, "safety by design" and recommendation algorithms switched off by default.

At home, an amendment to the Youth Protection Act would bar under-13s from social platforms unless a service is shown to be suitable for children, with graded limits from 13 to 18. Ages would be checked from 2027 through the EUDI wallet app "d-you", which heise notes is still troubled and will start with limited functions.

Justice Minister Stefanie Hubig wants the civil code to oblige parents explicitly to supervise their children's media use, and the package adds an "AI swimming badge" for primary pupils and new check-ups at ages nine to ten. The industry group eco calls it a patchwork; Bitkom says the under-13 ban goes too far.

Security · Crypto

Bugged Ledger wallets: $102 million gone from 324 wallets, and MediaMarkt stops selling them

The tampered hardware wallets, fitted before sale with an extra chip that reads the screen and an LTE modem with an eSIM, are a bigger problem than first thought. Bitquery counts $102 million stolen from 324 wallets on six blockchains up to Tuesday; Tether froze $10 million of it. The attacker tested for two weeks, then automated: 25 TRON wallets emptied in three seconds.

It is not just the Nano X; Ledger Flex devices turned up in Singapore, while Ledger's warning covers only devices from one reseller. In Germany a buyer found bugged units bought at MediaMarkt Saturn, supplied by an Austrian distributor, and the chain has pulled Ledger from its shelves.

Lisbon · Photography

João Pina takes his grandfather's Tarrafal into the national museum

"Terás visto o mar? Memórias do Tarrafal" opens on 22 October at the MNAC in Lisbon: about 250 photographs, letters, objects and three films in nine sections, curated by Emília Tavares. It grows out of the family archive of Pina's grandfather, Guilherme da Costa Carvalho, a political prisoner in the Cape Verde camp in 1949–51; a central corridor shows his great-grandmother laying flowers on the 32 graves.

Pina calls today's clamour for more repression in Portugal and elsewhere "frightening". The show runs to 24 January, then moves to the Alfândega in Porto from March to July 2027.
