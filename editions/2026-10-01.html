Germany · Prices

German inflation jumps to 3.3%, its highest since 2023, and Berlin pays more still

Diesel and heating oil cost half as much again as a year ago. Food barely moved and butter got cheaper. From today a three-month fuel discount takes about 17 cents off every litre.

tagesschau · rbb24 · Destatis · Amt für Statistik Berlin-Brandenburg · 30 September

Consumer prices in Germany were 3.3% higher in September than a year earlier, up from 2.9% in August, the Federal Statistical Office said in its first estimate. It is the first time since the end of 2023 that inflation has been above 3%. Prices rose 0.6% in the month alone. The cause is fuel: with the war in the Middle East pushing oil above $100 a barrel at times, the ADAC recorded several record prices for diesel and petrol in September.

In North Rhine-Westphalia, diesel cost 48.7% more than a year earlier, petrol 33.8% and heating oil 49.9%. Food, by contrast, rose only 0.4% and services 2.7%, slightly less than in August. Core inflation held at 2.4%. In the capital region it is worse: Berlin's rate reached 3.4% and Brandenburg's 3.6%, with fuel up 41.3% and 37.8%. Food in Berlin actually got 0.2% cheaper, and butter 25.7% cheaper.

"Fuel is by far the biggest price driver." Daniel Hartmann, Bantleon

The government's answer starts this morning. Until the end of the year, a new Tankrabatt cuts fuel tax by about 17 cents a litre, and heise reports E10 already under €2 a litre at some stations at dawn. The first discount, in May and June, held inflation down, but prices climbed again when it ended, and critics have not let up. Experts quoted by rbb expect the rate to fall back below 3% while it lasts.

The risk is what comes after. Import prices rose by more than 8% in August, the most since late 2022, and the drought that has left the Rhine low makes shipping dearer. "Fuel and heating oil have already become noticeably more expensive; in winter energy suppliers are likely to raise electricity and gas prices," said Timo Wollmershäuser of the ifo institute.

Ovar · Health

Ovar's hospital has had no working X-ray for two months, and Cortegaça's health centre may be outgrown

The radiology equipment at the Hospital Dr. Francisco Zagalo has been broken for about two months, so Ovar patients are being sent to the basic emergency service in Arouca for X-rays, including extra-long film. The local health authority, ULS Entre Douro e Vouga, told a PS delegation (councillor Emanuel Oliveira and MPs Hugo Oliveira, Filipe Neto Brandão and Susana Correia) that a repair has been ordered. The meeting also covered the family health units João Semana in Ovar, Laços in Cortegaça and Barrinha in Esmoriz: their buildings may not meet future needs, and the PS argues for two new health centres. The ULS said it is open to new uses for the old units in Maceda and Arada.

Industry · Wolfsburg

Volkswagen tears up most of its wage agreements, and the union calls it a foul

VW has given notice on a large part of its in-house collective agreement, effective 31 December 2026, including the framework contract that sets basic terms for more than 100,000 employees in Germany. The house agreement consists of 13 contracts covering working conditions, hours and bonuses; IG Metall says ten are affected. VW would not give a number and says it wants to "open room for negotiation" under cost pressure. The 2024 "future" agreement, with its job guarantee to the end of 2030, stays in force.

Workers could feel the effects from January 2027. "A nasty foul on the workforce," said Lower Saxony's IG Metall chief Thorsten Gröger; works council head Daniela Cavallo expects "a fierce fight at the turn of the year". The same day heise reported that VW's software arm Cariad may cut another 1,000 jobs.

Portugal & the Coast

Housing · Parliament

The new rent law passes its first vote, with faster evictions and Chega's price

Parliament approved the government's overhaul of the urban rental regime in principle on Wednesday: PSD, IL and CDS for, Chega abstaining, PS and the left against. The bill makes contracts more flexible and evictions quicker: a landlord could end a lease after two months' arrears. It also lets landlords ask for up to three months' rent in advance and move pre-1990 contracts onto the new regime. Chega's abstention was bought with a signed deal between André Ventura and PSD whip Hugo Soares to change the bill in committee: limits on advance payments and deposits, protection for poorer elderly tenants on old contracts, and more social support and emergency housing money. Reuters notes the contrast with Spain, which is blocking evictions.

Arts
Restitution · Ceramics

Milwaukee hands two of Dave the Potter's jars back to his family

The Milwaukee Art Museum has transferred ownership of two 19th-century stoneware vessels by David Drake to his descendants, under an agreement that keeps them on long-term display. Drake was born into slavery around 1801 and worked in the potteries of Edgefield, South Carolina, the South's ceramics centre. He threw unusually large pots and, at great risk, learned to read and write when the law forbade it, then signed his work and cut rhyming couplets into the clay. "It is about recognizing David Drake as our ancestor whose voice, expertise, and creativity have endured for over 175 years," said Yaba Baker, his fourth great-grandson.

Switzerland · Climate

Swiss glaciers lost 5.5% of their ice in a single year

Until recently, Alpine glaciologists treated a 2% loss in one year as extreme. Glacier Monitoring Switzerland estimates that 5.5% of the country's remaining ice disappeared in the last 12 months, a near-record. "It's really just disastrous," said its director, Matthias Huss. The glaciers went into summer under less snow than usual after a mild winter, and intense heatwaves did the rest. The scientists warn of the knock-on effects: water for farming, hydropower and river transport in the decades ahead, and slopes made unstable as ice and frozen ground thaw.
