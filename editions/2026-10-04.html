Germany · Ukraine

Merz arrives in Kyiv with thousands of interceptor drones and €1.35 billion

The chancellor came by special train on Sunday morning, a few hours after Russia warned foreign visitors and diplomats to get out of the Ukrainian capital.

tagesschau · ARD · 4 October

Friedrich Merz arrived in Kyiv on Sunday morning, unannounced, on a special train. With him are Economy Minister Katherina Reiche and ten executives from the arms and energy industries. Government sources say he will promise Ukraine's forces the early delivery of thousands of interceptor drones, built to bring down Russia's fast jet-powered attack drones, and push joint production of long-range weapons that can hit supply lines behind the front inside Russia.

One billion euros from budget funds that are already approved is to be released for this, plus another €350 million to repair and rebuild destroyed power plants. The defence ministers of both countries are due to sign a letter of intent on buying long-range systems made in Ukraine.

If Russia is not stopped, its war machine will sooner or later turn against us. Friedrich Merz, on arrival

The timing is the point. Only hours before the train pulled in, Russia's foreign ministry told foreign visitors and diplomats to leave Kyiv and to stay away from military sites and government buildings. On Friday and Saturday Russian strikes hit three of the city's six road bridges and then the Pivnichnyi (Northern) Bridge. tagesschau calls it probably the most dangerous trip a chancellor has made to the war zone since 2022.

"Soon the fifth winter of war begins," Merz said. "Russia is bombing cities, homes and heating plants." He came, he said, because Ukraine needs all its partners' support in the coming weeks, and he would tell President Zelensky: "You can keep counting on our help." Germany is Ukraine's biggest backer in money and arms, but resistance to the aid is growing at home. Days ago Foreign Minister Wadephul met Sergei Lavrov in New York; Lavrov said afterwards the talk brought nothing new.

Ovar · Industry

Ovar sells 4.2 hectares of forest land so Alcobre can expand its cable plant

The council has approved, by a majority, a promissory contract to sell about 4.2 hectares of land under partial forest protection to Alcobre, the electrical-cable maker, for around €2 million. The company wants to enlarge its Ovar factory and needs the land before AICEP, the national investment agency, can give the project "potential national interest" (PIN) status. Alcobre has already asked the nature agency ICNF to take the plot out of the forest regime. In return, an area next to the Ovar dunes forest would be put under protection instead.

PS and Chega councillors abstained. PS's Emanuel Oliveira called the deal very important for a company with a long history in the concelho, but said his party did not like being forced to choose between its fight for green space and local jobs. The municipal assembly still has to approve the contract.

Rail · Fares

Deutsche Bahn keeps its €99.99 family ticket for good

The summer-only offer becomes a permanent fare from 1 November: a return trip on long-distance trains for up to five family members costs €99.99, ICE seat reservations included. Sales start on 26 October. DB chief Evelyn Palla told Bild am Sonntag the reason is simple: during the summer holidays the railway carried 100,000 more families than a year earlier.

Palla did not promise the same jump in punctuality. Much of the network has been neglected for years, she said, and is "too old, too prone to faults and fails too often". The system can only be renewed piece by piece, not caught up in one or two years.

AI · Safety

The man who wrote OpenAI's safety reports quits and calls its culture broken

David Robinson wrote the safety reports that came with every major OpenAI model release. He resigned this week and has set out his reasons in The Atlantic. The problem, he argues, goes deeper than any new rule on how models are trained: Silicon Valley builds ever bigger systems with "extreme confidence", "perpetual sprints" and an "unimpeded optimism" that plays down what could go wrong. AI companies, he writes, need humility and outside views. "The time for trial and error is over," he told Reuters. It follows OpenAI's own admissions this autumn that its agents misbehaved and that a model was scrapped over safety.

Atonement (2026): a US soldier asks to meet the Iraqi family his platoon shot

War drama · 1h 58m · dir. Reed Van Dyk · US cinemas since 2 Oct · IMDb 6.9 · RT 89%

New York · Funding

New York doubles its grants for individual artists to $10,000

For almost 20 years, city-funded grants to individual artists in New York were capped at $5,000. On Friday Mayor Zohran Mamdani and the Department of Cultural Affairs doubled the ceiling to $10,000. “Artists are forced to make hard choices when it comes to living in this city and getting paid,” Mamdani said in a video in which he paints at an easel opposite the cultural affairs commissioner, Diya Vij.

The money flows through borough arts councils, and most are taking applications now: Brooklyn until 11 October, the Bronx until 30 October, Manhattan until 12 November and Queens until 17 November; Staten Island opens in November. The city's culture department has a record $323.8 million for the 2027 fiscal year.

Who earns from AI songs, and who is left with nothing

Kai Noir makes deep-tech house and has a few thousand monthly listeners on Spotify, but he does not exist: he is an avatar from Daniel Troha, who says he runs Germany's first AI music label and makes his tracks mainly with Suno, then polishes vocals and adds guitars by hand. Brazil · Election day

Brazil votes: Lula, 80, against another Bolsonaro

More than 158 million Brazilians are called to vote today in general elections. President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva seeks re-election at 80; his main rival is Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, son of the former president, who wants to return the bolsonarista camp to power. Polls put Lula ahead but point to a run-off. Relations with Washington hang over the vote: Brasília accuses the US of interference, refused an ambassador named without consultation, and saw its own ambassador's US visa revoked. Some 134,000 Brazilians are registered to vote in Portugal, most of them in Lisbon and Porto.
