Mathematics · Algorithms · AI

Matrix multiplication can never beat an exponent of 9/4: an OpenAI model claims to settle a 57-year-old question

A paper written by an unnamed internal model proves a lower bound that matches the best known algorithm, so the matrix multiplication exponent ω would be exactly 2.25, not the 2 most theorists hoped for. No human has reviewed it yet.

OpenAI math repository · ECCC · Hacker News · heise · Ars Technica

The paper, "The matrix multiplication exponent is nine fourths", proves that any arithmetic circuit multiplying two n×n matrices needs at least ⌈n9/4/2⌉ multiplication or division gates, at every size from n = 2 upwards. Since earlier OpenAI manuscripts this autumn had already pushed the upper bound down to 9/4, the two bounds now meet: ω = 2.25 over every field. Until this year the record was about 2.371, and the only lower bound anyone had was the trivial 2, because every entry has to be read. Most researchers expected ω to turn out to be 2.

For working code nothing changes. Strassen's 1969 algorithm and Pan's of 1978 are still the only ones in this line practical enough to use; Shpilka's exposition of the 9/4 bound builds explicit algorithms whose base matrices grow to 2 to the power (1/ε)O(1), far beyond any GPU. What changes is the map: the hunt for a near-quadratic algorithm, which has driven the field since Coppersmith and Winograd, would be over, and with it the group-theoretic programmes that set out to prove ω = 2.

"These manuscripts are best understood as a form of raw research data, rather than polished academic papers."OpenAI, introducing the repository

The proof came in a batch of nine posted on Tuesday, all from the same unnamed model and built on a new geometric theory of tensors. The others claim a strongly polynomial algorithm for linear programming, Smale's ninth problem; a disproof of the polynomial Hirsch conjecture for simple polytopes; an exponential bound for sunflowers; and circuit lower bounds for the permanent that separate Valiant's classes VP and VNP. Anthropic had posted its own VP ≠ VNP proof, by a Claude model and checked in Lean, on Monday, the same day OpenAI released one it says its model found on 6 November.

The caveats are OpenAI's own. Its repository now holds 730 manuscripts, about 42 per cent of the headline results formalised in the Lean proof assistant, and it warns that unformalised ones "could have issues". For the matrix result, Lean checks the exact exponent in the division-free model, counting additions and multiplications; the paper's wider claim, with division, rests on the text. Days after the Riemann claim, mathematicians now have more machine-written proofs to read than people to read them.

Arada · High-speed line

Arada's landowners say they are being pushed to give up farmland for the high-speed line at €8 a square metre

A reader's letter in OvarNews describes how the expropriations for the Porto–Lisbon line are going in Ovar. It says AVEX's expropriations specialist presses owners to take the first offer, and remind them that some people expropriated for the A29 motorway are still waiting to be paid, which the writers call psychological pressure on people already in a weak position.

Land classed as Reserva Agrícola Nacional is being valued at €8/m², apparently as a flat rate rather than plot by plot. The letter cites the Expropriations Code: article 23 promises a "fair compensation" at real market value, article 27 asks for each plot's actual aptitude to be weighed, and article 29 says that when only part of a property is taken, the loss in value of what is left, cut up, harder to reach or less farmable, must be added to the payment. The owners say that is not happening.

Politics · Budget 2027

The 2027 budget passes, but PS and Chega changes turn its surplus into a deficit

The budget was approved in Tuesday's final vote with PSD and CDS in favour, the PS and JPP abstaining, and Chega, IL, Livre, PCP, BE and PAN against. Security · AI agents

Amazon says attackers' AI agents found and exploited a zero-day on their own

The hackers, who wrote their prompts in simplified Chinese and set the model to China Standard Time, gave the agents a list of IP addresses and one goal, "obtain shell access", against a university server in East Asia. The agents found an unknown hole in an open-source medical-imaging AI application on one of the machines, exploited it and took full control, with no further human input.

They ran on Cairn, an open-source, model-agnostic penetration-testing framework that farms tasks out to containers of parallel agents; back ends include Claude Code and Codex, and Palo Alto Networks has seen it driven by GLM and DeepSeek models. "Agentic offensive operations are already happening," Amazon says; defenders should prepare for their "speed and persistence".

Russia · Ukraine war

Russia's own death records show at least 490,000 soldiers killed in Ukraine

BBC Russian and Mediazona analysed more than 11 million death records from 2021 to September 2026, leaked from the statistics agency Rosstat after a hack by the Belarusian Cyber Partisans and Ukraine's Silent Crow; Rosstat admits an attack but denies anything protected leaked. They count at least 490,334 military deaths, and 90 per cent of the soldiers on their own open-source list appear in the files.

In 2025 the war killed 65 per cent of the Russians aged 18 to 24 who died, and 59 per cent of those aged 25 to 34; only from 45 to 54 does heart disease kill more. Some 20,000 Russian soldiers died fighting for ten Ukrainian villages whose combined pre-war population was 12,000.
