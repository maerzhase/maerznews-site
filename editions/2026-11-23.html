Rail · Strike · Linha do Norte

CP's train drivers strike all week, and on Tuesday and Wednesday only about one train in four is guaranteed

The SMAQ union's stoppage began at midnight. Monday and Thursday are partial strikes with no minimum service at all; on the two full days an arbitration panel has named the trains that must run, roughly 25 to 30 per cent of CP's timetable, bunched into the rush hours.

Público · Observador · RTP · Tâmega TV · as read at 06:30 on 23 November 2026

The strike was called by SMAQ, the train drivers' union, and its notice runs from 00:00 on Monday 23 to midnight on Friday 27 November. The heavy days are Tuesday and Wednesday, when drivers stop completely. Monday and Thursday are partial strikes, and the arbitration panel set no minimum services for them because it expects fewer cancellations. CP nonetheless warns of "strong disruption" all week, with the last trains on Monday night and the first on Thursday morning already exposed.

On the two full days the panel did not set a percentage but named the trains, which works out at more than 330 a day across the country. Urban and suburban lines get priority at the busiest hours, as do interurban links with no reasonable road alternative, and the first and last train of each day in each direction. Around midday only Alfa Pendular and Intercidades are obliged to run. On the Braga line, for example, trains to Porto São Bento are guaranteed only from 05:26 to 07:48 and again from 17:30 to 19:35.

Strong disruption to rail traffic, with the cancellation of various trains across all CP services. CP's passenger notice

For this coast the number that matters is 72: the trains a day the Porto urban network, which includes the Aveiro line through Ovar, Esmoriz and Cortegaça, must run on Tuesday and Wednesday. No breakdown by line has been published, so expect a morning and an evening window and little in between. SMAQ wants lost purchasing power back, better careers and rosters, full application of the company agreement and, it says, railway safety; it will obey the minimums while contesting them. Tickets for affected trains can be refunded in full or swapped free of charge, online up to 15 minutes before departure. Tuesday is also the day of parliament's final vote on the 2027 budget.

Cinanima's 50th edition ends with a French train ride of fears, azulejos for women's rights and a bordello in the Sertão

Germany · Berlin & Brandenburg

Security · Space

Western spies expect Russia to launch a nuclear-armed satellite, perhaps in December, and Germany has no plan for losing its satellites

According to WDR, NDR and the Süddeutsche Zeitung, Western intelligence services, the Bundeswehr and the BND believe Russia intends to put a satellite carrying a nuclear warhead into orbit this year. The satellite, reportedly called "Sputnik-S", is said to have sat finished near Moscow for several years; warnings shared among NATO states in early November said it could launch within 60 days, and the Bundeswehr's space command thinks it could go up in December. A nuclear blast in orbit could disable or destroy large numbers of satellites, and would break the 1967 Outer Space Treaty.

The same reporters found that Germany has no crisis plan for a large-scale satellite failure, which would knock out navigation, banking, power and mobile networks. A Bundeswehr wargame on the scenario at district level ran into supply problems quickly; its project lead warned of possible looting, and pointed to Finland as far better prepared. The defence ministry would not comment; Russia's embassy called the reports propaganda.

Archaeology · Lisbon in Egypt

Portuguese archaeologists map 75 tombs at Akoris and argue its Serapis temple was torn down, not left to crumble

The University of Lisbon's first official mission in Egypt, led by Egyptologist Rogério Sousa and Roman archaeologist Catarina Viegas with colleagues from the University of Minya, spent three weeks at the end of 2025 at Akoris, a city on a limestone ridge above the Nile some 250 km south of Cairo. Using LiDAR, photogrammetry and 3D scans they inventoried 75 funerary structures, some unknown to science.

The city was lived in from the third millennium BC to the seventh century AD. The team reads the ruin of its main sanctuary, a Roman-era temple to Serapis in the forum, as deliberate destruction as Christianity rose, as at Alexandria. "We think Pharaonic culture is monolithic," Sousa says, "but it is a mosaic." They return in early 2027.

Climate · COP31

The longest climate summit ever ends in Antalya with no declaration at all

After two weeks, 44 hours of overtime and four versions of the text, and sharp criticism from the EU and Germany, several countries rejected the Turkish-Australian presidency's "Antalya Pact" in the closing plenary on Sunday. The draft named no exit from coal, oil and gas, only a cross-reference to the 2023 Dubai promise to transition away from fossil fuels.

"We did our best," said the conference president, Murat Kurum: 196 countries decide, and the text was imposed on no one. UN Secretary-General Guterres blamed the oil and gas industry in part; Portugal's government said it regretted the outcome, and environmental groups called it a failure.
