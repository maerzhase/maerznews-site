Germany · Federal government

Bärbel Bas quits as labour minister and SPD leader, and the party will re-elect its entire leadership

She named the death of her stepdaughter and "public attacks" from the CDU. Alexander Schweitzer takes the ministry; a party congress in December or January will choose everyone else, Lars Klingbeil included.

tagesschau · Dagmar Pepping, Nicole Kohnert · Berlin, 12 October 2026

Bärbel Bas resigned on Monday morning as federal minister of labour and social affairs and as co-chair of the SPD. She has been grieving her stepdaughter and had cut back or cancelled her appointments for weeks; she said she needed time to come to terms with the loss. But she also named "public attacks" from the ranks of the coalition partner, made during that time. For months Union politicians had accused her of stalling reforms already agreed on pensions, more flexible working hours and the basic income system; Christian von Stetten, who chairs the Union group's influential Mittelstand caucus, had called her "no longer tenable".

Within hours the SPD had a successor for the ministry: Alexander Schweitzer, until recently premier of Rhineland-Palatinate. Party chief Lars Klingbeil then went further. The presidium decided unanimously that the whole leadership will be re-elected at a federal party congress in December or January, "so that we have clarity about who leads us and about the SPD's programme". He will stand himself, and the party keeps its male-female double leadership.

"To expose a politician to political attacks in a time of personal grief is indecent. That poisons political debate." Lars Klingbeil, SPD leader

Chancellor Friedrich Merz said he regretted but respected the decision and that the two had worked together "very well and trustingly" for a year and a half, then added that he hoped Schweitzer would get the ministry's open bills through cabinet quickly. Klingbeil rejected the charge that Bas had braked anything: "We were on schedule, as agreed." The CDU leadership denies her accusation of attacks.

In Berlin and Brandenburg, SPD state leaders Steffen Krach and Dietmar Woidke thanked her; Krach also levelled serious charges at the Union. She leaves a party in a slump: the SPD won 16.4% in 2025 and now stands at 11–14%, behind the Greens in all eight national polls and at roughly half the AfD's share.

Cortegaça · Maceda · Beach

The tides dig up the 20-tonne whale buried on the beach in May

The carcass of a whale of about 20 tonnes is lying exposed again on the sand at Maceda. It is almost certainly the same animal that washed up in May between São Pedro beach and the south jetty at Cortegaça. The council had no means to remove it then, so it was buried where it lay; the force of the tides has since scoured away part of the beach and uncovered it. Ovar's environment and mobility division is assessing the site, and the council says the carcass will be covered again today, Tuesday, in principle in the same spot. The story has travelled well beyond Ovar, from Jornal de Notícias to Correio da Manhã.

Economy · IMF

The IMF almost doubles its forecast for Germany, to 1.3% growth this year

In its new World Economic Outlook the International Monetary Fund expects German GDP to grow 1.3% in 2026, 0.6 points more than it forecast in July and now level with the federal government's own figure. For 2027 it sees 1.1%, up 0.1 points. The euro area is raised 0.2 points to 1.1% despite the Iran war, and the world 0.1 points to 3.1%: rich countries have proved resilient to the higher energy prices, and many are riding investment in AI. Poorer countries outside that boom face hard choices, so the Fund calls the outlook "two-track", and chief economist Silvana Tenreyro warns that risks lean to the downside. In Berlin the better numbers are already being spent: after a CDU leadership retreat, Merz promised to cut electricity tax for every household to the EU minimum, a pledge so far kept only for parts of industry, "if growth firms up".

Porto · Housing

Police clear the housing protest camp from Praça D. João I, and the campers sleep there anyway

Porto's municipal police, backed by the PSP's riot unit, took down all of the roughly 50 tents pitched in Praça D. João I on Monday morning, in a few hours and with moments of tension. The camp had stood since Saturday, after the "Não dá mais para viver assim" march against rising rents and living costs. The city council called it an "illegal occupation" of public space; the movement called the police action disproportionate, and one protester asked an officer how much he pays in rent. By evening the protesters had decided to spend the night in the square again without tents. Meanwhile residents have gone to court against a new overnight shelter for homeless people, and Público reported that the tourism-pressure indicator has risen in Lisbon and even more in Porto.

Python · Release

Python 3.15 is out, with lazy imports that cut a test script's start from 44 ms to 11

Released on 9 October, a week late after a third release candidate fixed last-minute lazy-import bugs, 3.15 carries 5,643 commits from 1,012 contributors. The headline is a soft keyword: lazy import json defers loading a module until its name is first used. In heise's measurement on an Apple M4, a script importing 13 standard-library modules started in a median 44 ms, and 11 ms with lazy imports, barely more than no imports at all. It works only at module level, and setting PYTHON_LAZY_IMPORTS=all tries it on old code, at the price of errors surfacing later. New built-ins frozendict, an immutable and hashable dict first rejected in 2012, and sentinel arrive, the JIT compiler advances, and UTF-8 becomes the default encoding, which heise says needs testing.

France · Protest

France's school revolt returns to the streets as the budget debate opens

Pupils march again today, the fifth act of what tagesschau calls the biggest school revolt since May '68. It began in mid-September with a blockade in Créteil, south of Paris, spread to the banlieues in the north and then to the better-off city-centre lycées; students, teachers' unions and parents have joined the demand for better chances for the banlieues and young people. The violence is new too, with masked groups attacking police with stones, fireworks and iron bars. Separately, on Monday, a 14-year-old was arrested after a principal and a teacher were shot and injured at a middle school. Parliament starts on next year's budget today, with a government trying to cut a deficit that leaves France "with its back to the wall", half a year before the presidential election.
