Germany · Opinion poll

Only one German in ten is satisfied with Merz's government, a record low

The October DeutschlandTrend has the government and the chancellor both at 10% approval, the worst ever recorded. The Union slips to 20% and the AfD stays clear at 27%.

tagesschau · ARD-DeutschlandTrend · infratest dimap · 1 October

Just 10% of Germans are satisfied with the work of the federal government, and 10% with the chancellor's. Both are the lowest readings in the history of the ARD-DeutschlandTrend, which infratest dimap carried out among 1,303 voters from Monday to Wednesday. In the Sunday question the CDU/CSU loses a point to 20%. The Union has only once polled that low in the series, in September 2021.

The AfD is unchanged at 27% and remains the strongest party. The SPD holds at 13%. The Greens gain a point to 16% and the Left loses one to 11%. The FDP stays at 4%, below the threshold. Together the two coalition parties now poll 33%.

77% say they feel more and more burdened, even though the government promised relief. ARD-DeutschlandTrend, October

The mood behind the numbers is about money. Around half of those asked fear they cannot keep their standard of living. Asked how to fund the welfare state, 41% would accept higher contributions and 37% would rather see benefits cut; six in ten want any cuts staged by income. Only 17% trust the government to make the social systems secure. On energy, 74% say its measures do not go far enough. The fuel discount that started this week splits the country: 47% think it right, 46% wrong.

The worries reach beyond the wallet. Two thirds fear people could lose control of artificial intelligence, the same share worries about Donald Trump's policies, and 54% fear Russia will attack further European countries. The biggest worry of all, at 84%, is that the parties find no common answers on the central questions.

Ovar · Town hall

Ovar's new police station gets the Court of Auditors' green light

The Tribunal de Contas has given its prior approval to the contract for the new PSP station in Ovar, the last step before building can start. The job was awarded for about €2.15 million, with 540 days to finish, and the station will go up on the former site of the municipal water and sanitation services. The same council meeting approved €249,341.91 for the carnival groups and samba schools, paid in advance because next year's Carnaval falls earlier than usual. It also revised rents in council-owned social housing from 1 November: each household's rent will be reset to its income and size, so some will pay more, some less.

Kreuzberg · Court

Görlitzer Park stays open at night after the Senate loses again in court

The Higher Administrative Court of Berlin-Brandenburg on Thursday rejected the Senate's appeal in the urgent proceedings over the night closure of Görlitzer Park. Like the lower court in June, it found the Senate's environment department had no authority to set opening hours: that power lay with the Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg district, and the law was only changed in July 2024, too late. The ruling cannot be challenged, though the main case is still to come. The CDU-SPD Senate decided on the closure in 2023 to curb drug dealing and violence and had 16 gates built around the park.

Residents and the alliance "Görli zaunfrei!" brought the case. "A fence solves no problems, it at most moves them into the surrounding streets," said their lawyer, David Werdermann, who wants the new Senate to drop the plan for good.

Platforms · Open web

Reddit will switch off its RSS feeds in November and its public API by March

Reddit's RSS feeds stop on 13 November 2026, and the public API follows by March 2027. The company blames scraping and abuse; the news came almost in passing, in an r/modnews post about tools for moderators, who are told to use a "Discord Relay" app instead. Reddit sells data licences to Google and OpenAI and is suing Anthropic and Perplexity for scraping, so AI firms that read Reddit through the API will now need a commercial contract. Social-listening and research tools lose access too. It is the next step after 2023, when API fees killed third-party apps such as Apollo.

Rediscovery · Sculpture

A £300 find in a Wiltshire salvage yard turns out to be a lost Bracci

A two-foot marble bought at a reclamation yard has been identified by the auction house Woolley & Wallis as the only surviving model for Pietro Bracci's 1736 bronze of Pope Clement XII. The life-size bronze stood in the Great Hall of the Palazzo Nuovo, in the Capitoline Museums that Clement opened to the public that year, until it was melted down under the French-imposed Roman Republic of 1798–99. Since then the only record of it was an engraving. Bracci (1700–1773), best known for the colossal Oceanus of the Trevi Fountain, trained under Camillo Rusconi. The model is estimated at £5,000 to £10,000.

Review · The Quietus

Susanne Sundfør sings a medieval mystic with nothing but a church organ

Sacred art song · seventh album · with Kit Downes

Revelations of Divine Love, the Norwegian singer's seventh album, grew out of recent motherhood and her bond with the writing of Julian of Norwich, the 14th-century anchoress whose book is thought to be the first written in English by a woman. Sundfør recorded it live in an empty Norwegian church, just her voice and a pipe organ, with songs written alongside the English composer Kit Downes, a collaborator of Squarepusher and Mica Levi. The texts come from Grace Warrack's 1901 translation. The Quietus hears "What Are We" as spectral and mesmerising and calls the whole thing beautiful, and quietly subversive.

Energy · Fuel

Trump threatens to ban US diesel exports and tells Europe to open its reserves

With US voters paying more for fuel before November's midterms, Donald Trump says he may ask European countries to release diesel reserves while he weighs a ban on American exports. "We may do that. They have some diesel," he said on Thursday; Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent wants Europe to make supplies available "immediately". The US exports 1.2 to 1.5 million barrels of diesel a day, so a ban would lower US pump prices and raise them elsewhere. Britain, which imports over half its diesel and 31% of that from the US, already sees record prices near 200p a litre. The European Commission speaks of "lots of calls, lots of meetings". Russia's export ban and China's reported halt of some October fuel exports add to the squeeze.
