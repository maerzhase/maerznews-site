Germany · Security · Spyware

Apple warns of "mercenary spyware" on the iPhones of Bundestag members and ministers, and Berlin tells them to assume the worst

Digital minister Karsten Wildberger confirms that "certain devices" may have been compromised. Who was targeted, by whom, and whether the attack worked: nobody is saying.

tagesschau · ARD-Hauptstadtstudio · heise online · 1–2 November 2026

The Federal Office for Information Security (BSI) has written to members of the Bundestag and of the federal government about a possible spyware attack on their phones. According to its letter, which the ARD's Berlin bureau has seen, iPhones have been receiving Apple threat notifications since 29 October saying that an attack with so-called mercenary spyware had been detected, aimed at compromising the device. "We currently have no further information on the specific cause and background of the notifications," the BSI writes, and it advises those affected "as a precaution to assume that the device may be compromised".

On ARD's "Bericht aus Berlin", digital minister Karsten Wildberger (CDU) confirmed a report from Apple "that certain devices may have been compromised with so-called spyware", said the BSI had reacted "super fast" and written to everyone affected, and would go no further: "It's purely speculative at this point." Which politicians were targeted is not public. Apple calls these notifications high-confidence warnings that a person was individually targeted, but they do not show whether an attack succeeded, and the company never names the attacker.

"That is still something that, of course, worries us greatly." Karsten Wildberger, federal digital minister, on ARD

Mercenary spyware is the trade in tools like NSO's Pegasus, sold mostly to states. Once installed, the BSI warns, it can read messages including encrypted messengers, calls, contacts, calendars and location, and switch on the microphone and camera. The timing is awkward: today Wildberger hosts the government's two-day digital summit in Berlin, sold out, with the French, Dutch and Canadian digital ministers and a visit from Chancellor Merz, on how Germany and Europe can keep up in AI.

Rail · Data

The Berlin–Hannover detour already adds up to 80 minutes, and almost two in three trains were late on top

Since early October the Deutsche Bahn has been rebuilding the line between Berlin and Hannover, a closure that will last 14 months, and long-distance trains run a detour that adds up to 80 minutes. A Railwise analysis of the first three weeks, seen exclusively by rbb24, finds that almost two thirds of the direct trains in both directions were late as well, by about 23 minutes on average. The railway had not explained why by the time of publication.

Commuters feel it most. Marco Stypmann used to ride from Berlin to Volkswagen in Wolfsburg four times a week in an hour; now the direct link is cut, he changes to regional trains in Magdeburg or Braunschweig, and a single trip takes about four. The late ICE usually misses the connection, adding another 45 minutes on the stopping train. His employer now lets him come in twice a week.

Frontend · AI agents

What it costs an AI agent to build the same app in 12 JavaScript frameworks: Hono+htmx cheapest, Angular dearest

Tired of "better for agents" claims with no evidence, Nolan Lawson had Claude Code build one small Pokémon app five times in each of twelve stacks and checked every result against a conformance suite. Median cost per build ran from $2.67 for Hono+htmx, $2.76 for Astro and $2.82 for Nuxt up to $4.28 for SvelteKit, $4.65 for Gracile+Lit and $4.97 for Angular; Next.js sat at $3.14 and React Router at $3.81. Time followed cost: Astro took a median 8.2 minutes, Angular 17.2.

Part of the gap is training data: SvelteKit's agents read the v3 migration guide on every run, Gracile's read its source. Framework MCPs and skills were often ignored. Lighthouse scores were near-saturated, with MPAs ahead on LCP, and "vanilla" Vite did worse than several frameworks. One content-heavy app, he stresses; lines of code are the number to watch, because they cost tokens on every future edit.

Berlin · Obituary

Regina Ziegler, the Berlin producer behind more than 500 films, has died at 82

Film & TV producer · 1944–2026 · Ziegler Film, Berlin

Born in Quedlinburg in 1944, Ziegler began in the mid-1960s as a production assistant at Sender Freies Berlin, where she met the director Wolf Gremm, later her husband. In 1973 she raised the money for his first feature, "Ich dachte, ich wäre tot", which won the federal film prize, and at 29 she set up her own company in an industry run by men.

Over more than 50 years she produced over 500 films and series and won the German Film Award, an International Emmy and the Grimme Prize, with lifetime-achievement honours in 2012, 2016 and 2018. She died in Berlin, her company confirmed to dpa.

The Louvre buys peace with its unions: 100 new hires by 2028 and a raise for the guards

A year after the daytime theft of the crown jewels, the Louvre has signed a deal with the unions that had kept a strike notice open since December, closing the museum fully for four days and partly for four more. Detroit · Obituary

Juan Atkins, who named techno and built it with a Korg in Detroit, has died at 64

Techno · electro · Cybotron, Model 500, Borderland · Metroplex

One of the Belleville Three with Derrick May and Kevin Saunderson, Atkins bought his first synth, a Korg MS-10, at 15 and formed Cybotron at 18 with Richard "3070" Davis. Their 1983 single "Clear" sold 50,000 copies and became a genre template, later sampled by Missy Elliott. As Model 500 he founded the Metroplex label in 1985, and he was due to play Mexico City's Yu Yu Club on Saturday night.

Berlin was his second home: with Moritz von Oswald he made two albums as Borderland, in 2013 and 2016, and in 2023 revived Cybotron with Laurens von Oswald. "In Detroit, we don't have any real reference point," he said of the sound. "All the Detroit stuff was pure futuristic."

Bulgaria · Election

Bulgaria looks set to elect its first woman president, the Russia-friendly Iliana Iotova, with up to 58%

Exit polls from Sunday's run-off give the acting president, Iliana Iotova, up to 58% against up to 39% for the former caretaker prime minister Andrey Gyurov. Turnout was about 27 to 29%, a little up on the record low of 23% in the first round on 25 October. The official result is expected today.

Iotova, 62, took over automatically as vice-president when Rumen Radev resigned this year; Radev is now prime minister, and his centre-left party backed her independent run. She calls herself a "president of peace": she supports Bulgaria's membership of the EU and NATO but opposes military aid to Ukraine.
