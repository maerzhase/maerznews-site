Magdeburg · Saxony-Anhalt Landtag

The AfD wins a German parliament's chair for the first time, with four votes nobody owns up to

Tobias Rausch was elected president of Saxony-Anhalt's state parliament with 48 of 82 votes. The AfD and its new BSW allies have 44 seats. In Berlin, the SPD wants the CDU to explain the rest.

tagesschau · MDR · rbb24 · Magdeburg, 6 October

At the first sitting of the parliament elected on 6 September, the AfD's Tobias Rausch, 35, won in the first round: 48 votes for, 29 against, five abstentions, with 82 of the 83 members present. The AfD, which won the election with 43.8% but no majority, holds 39 seats. The five MPs of the BSW, which renamed itself on 1 October, had promised their votes. That still leaves at least four votes from the CDU, SPD, Greens or Left. The SPD, Greens and Left, 22 MPs together, had said they would vote no; the CDU leadership told its 15 MPs to abstain, but only five abstentions were cast.

Because the rules don't count abstentions, the CDU's plan lowered the bar anyway: a majority of yes-and-no votes was enough. The state's intelligence service has classed the Saxony-Anhalt AfD as confirmed right-wing extremist since 2023. As president, Rausch runs the sittings, hands out reprimands, holds police powers in the parliament's buildings and hires and moves its staff. He was himself reprimanded in 2024, and he was criticised earlier this year over nepotism. Later the same day his party colleague Hans-Thomas Tillschneider became vice-president, and the SPD candidate failed.

"It is a taboo to elect right-wing extremists to such offices." SPD leader Lars Klingbeil

In Berlin, SPD MPs marched from the Bundestag through the Brandenburg Gate to Bebelplatz, where the Nazis burned books in 1933, and Boris Pistorius started the national anthem. Klingbeil and general secretary Tim Klüssendorf want the CDU, their coalition partner, to say who voted for Rausch. CDU parliamentary leader Thorsten Frei said in a secret ballot "anyone" could have.

The pressure falls on Sven Schulze, the CDU's acting premier, whose group did not follow him. AfD leader Ulrich Siegmund will stand for premier in December and says he will find the votes. Brandenburg, where the AfD could also come first, is already debating whether its parliament's rules need changing.

Aveiro · Agrovouga

Aveiro's farm fair goes ahead without its cattle, for fear of a disease in Spain

Agrovouga 2026 runs from 14 to 18 October, but with no cattle on show. There will be no live Friesian dairy cow and no national contest for the Marinhoa, the local breed raised mostly on farms in the Baixo Vouga and Mondego. The breeders' associations chose to stay away after cases of lumpy skin disease in Spain. No outbreak is known in Portugal, but Elizabete Pires of the Aveiro dairy-cattle support association says gathering animals from many farms is not advisable now. The Marinhoa will still feature through a producers' conference and food tastings, and horses take the main stage. The fair has also moved: it used to be held in November and is now half as long. Last year bird flu disrupted it. Entry is free.

Espionage · Federal prosecutor

A former head of the BND is arrested on suspicion of treason

Federal police arrested August Hanning, 80, BND president from 1998 to 2005, at his home near Münster on Tuesday, and his former office chief Manfred D. in Bavaria; homes and offices in Berlin were also searched. Prosecutors say D., who ran the office of successive BND presidents until March 2026, passed Hanning about 2,000 documents for money. Many were briefings for the weekly security meeting at the Chancellery, reportedly on Iran's nuclear programme, the Russian army and material from partner services. Hanning is suspected of using them to write at least one analysis for a foreign intelligence service; WDR, NDR and the Süddeutsche report it was not Russia. Investigators found the papers while searching his house in 2025 over the attempted abduction of businesswoman Christina Block's children, which he denies taking part in. A judge at the Federal Court of Justice decides on custody today. No former German official this senior has ever been accused of treason.

Lisbon · Budget 2027

The Socialists will let Montenegro's 2027 budget pass

The PS confirmed on Tuesday that it will abstain on the 2027 state budget, enough to get it through whatever the rest of the opposition does. The party's André Moz Caldas said the country must not start the year on duodécimos, the month-by-month spending used when no budget is passed; the PS says it wants to avoid a "political precipice". The PS says it may change its vote if the government springs surprises.

Security · Web PKI

Attackers hijacked three country domains to get fake TLS certificates for Google

Google said on Tuesday that attackers took over the .gh (Ghana), .sl (Sierra Leone) and .as (American Samoa) top-level domains and changed the authoritative DNS records for selected names inside them. Controlling DNS let them pass the automated domain checks that certificate authorities use, and obtain certificates for "several Google domains" and other big brands and services. With such a certificate an attacker can impersonate the real site even over HTTPS. Google has updated Chrome to block every unauthorised certificate it found and worked with the issuing authorities to revoke those for its own properties. The case shows the weak link in the chain: a certificate is only as safe as the DNS behind it.

France · Theft

Thieves take Dalí's 150-kilo bronze cover of the "world's heaviest book"

The cover of the Livre de l'Apocalypse, bronze set with precious stones, seashells and, this being Dalí, cutlery, was stolen on Sunday night from a small private museum in Issoire, central France. The book was made between 1958 and 1961 by the publisher Joseph Forêt with contributions from Cocteau, Buffet, Foujita, Fini, Zadkine and Giono; Picasso refused to join if Dalí did. Its 150 sheepskin pages were not on display and are safe. Complete, it weighs about 210 kilos and was once valued at two million francs. The thieves broke the gate and the display case. "They assassinated France last night," said its owner, the restorer Christian Karoutzos.
