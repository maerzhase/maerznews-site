Technology · JavaScript runtimes

Deno joins Cloudflare, and Ryan Dahl gives his runtime one more year

The whole Deno team is moving to Cloudflare to build on Workers and Durable Objects. Deno Deploy shuts in six months; the runtime gets a year of fixes, then its own development stops.

Deno blog · Cloudflare blog · Hacker News · announced Friday 9 October

The entire Deno team is joining Cloudflare, Ryan Dahl announced on Friday. Instead of developing a separate runtime and hosting service, they will put their work into a shared platform built on the Workers programming model, merging Deno's new celld with workerd, Workers' open-source runtime. Dahl does not soften it: "We know this is a consequential change for people who have built on Deno."

The terms are concrete. The Deno runtime gets another year of monthly releases with bug fixes and security updates; after that, Deno ends its development, though the code stays open source and others are welcome to carry it on. Deno Deploy keeps running for six months and then shuts down, with migration help to Cloudflare Workers for paying customers. JSR, the package registry, carries on with its infrastructure moving to Cloudflare, and the team keeps supporting rusty_v8, its Rust bindings to V8, with a view to using it inside workerd.

"Long ago, I stood in a Berlin warehouse and introduced Node.js with a 500-line JavaScript IRC server." Ryan Dahl, on the Cloudflare blog

The reason is celld, which the Deno team released in August: a single Rust binary, with object storage as its only external dependency, that runs Workers and Durable Objects spread across many machines. Each Durable Object is a small, addressable server with its own SQLite database, which Dahl calls the answer to the scaling problem his single-threaded IRC demo never solved. Workerd is already open source but runs Durable Objects only on a single instance; the merger is meant to let anyone operate the distributed version on their own hardware. Cloudflare's Kenton Varda used the joint post to rebut the charge that Workers' unusual design is a lock-in trap: an open, self-hostable implementation, he argues, is the proof.

For a TypeScript shop the practical question is how much code leans on Deno-only APIs or on Deploy. Deploy apps have until roughly April to move; everything else has a year. Dahl's pitch is aimed squarely at AI: Durable Objects' cheap serverless execution, persistent state and WebSockets are, he writes, what agent harnesses need. On Hacker News the announcement passed 1,100 points and almost 600 comments within a day.

Aveiro · High-speed rail

Building work on the Porto–Lisbon high-speed line starts in Aveiro on Monday

Construction of the first section, Porto to Oiã in Oliveira do Bairro, formally begins on Monday 12 October, when the AVAN Norte consortium led by Mota-Engil marks the start of works at a public session in Aveiro at about 15:30. Infrastructure minister Miguel Pinto Luz is expected; Infraestruturas de Portugal's chairman Paulo Carmona will present the works, and Philippe Chantraine speaks for the European Commission's transport directorate, DG MOVE. Earlier this month 1,076 plots in Oliveira do Bairro, Aveiro and Albergaria-a-Velha were declared of public utility for expropriation, and site set-up has begun at the Rio Largo and Vouga bridges. Drivers will notice first: geotechnical surveys bring night-time restrictions on three stretches of the A29, the motorway past Ovar, for about a month. The first phase is due in 2030, the second in 2032. At the northern end, Gaia's council is still fighting the planned station site (see Portugal).

Rail · Renovation

Deutsche Bahn cuts its 2031 rebuild from 21 main lines to 13

The general overhaul of Germany's busiest rail corridors is shrinking. Deutsche Bahn and the federal transport ministry said on Friday that only 13 corridors, instead of the 21 planned, will be fully modernised by 2031, to make the huge construction volumes more reliable given scarce capacity on the network and in the building industry. "The idea of corridor renovations is right," said transport minister Steffen Bilger. "In the past, though, the implementation too often did not deliver the results we wanted." A five-point plan promises better-prepared diversion routes for passengers and freight, renovated corridors that reopen in stages, and the European train-control system ETCS as the new standard, prepared in every renovation and fitted directly from the early 2030s. Chief executive Evelyn Palla said the railway had learned from the first rebuilds and wants to keep the burden on passengers during works as small as possible. In September she had already conceded that four big renovations a year were too many.

Porto · Culture

Porto agrees in principle to buy the Stop's two old cinemas

Porto's council has a "pre-agreement" to buy the two cinema halls inside the Stop shopping centre, culture councillor Jorge Sobrado announced on Friday: one of about 240 seats, the other about 120, closed almost since the centre stopped working as a mall. They are to join the city's new network of creation centres. Sobrado would not give the price, only that it is well below market value. The deal with the owner, NOS, is settled, but safety conditions, including self-protection measures and a safety easement, are still being worked out. The Stop, on Rua do Heroísmo, has become home to hundreds of musicians who rent its shops as rehearsal rooms; after the 2023 fight over its licence, the council classified it a building of municipal interest in February 2025, and it now also wants the Stop's use recognised as intangible cultural heritage.

Web · React

Next.js 16.4 makes Cache Components the default for new apps

Vercel now recommends its new caching model for every Next.js app, and create-next-app switches it on by default; it becomes the framework default in Next.js 17. A 'use cache' directive, tuned with cacheLife(), works like a component-level Cache-Control header, replacing the App Router's implicit caching with explicit, composable caching on the client, the server or at build time. Existing apps enable it with the cacheComponents and partialPrefetching flags. For migrations, a new next upgrade --agent command gives coding agents version-specific guidance, backed by dedicated Skills. Every app also gets lower dev-server memory and disk use, faster compiles, smaller production bundles and React 19.3.

Schlingensief's Church of Fear comes to the Gropius Bau

Sixteen years after his death, Christoph Schlingensief gets a Berlin show whose title quotes him: "Es ist nicht mehr mein Problem, macht eure Scheiße alleine" (it's not my problem any more, sort your own shit out). Live debut · 43 years on

Duet Emmo, Mute's boss and two of Wire, will play live for the first time

Experimental electronic · Mute · ICA, London, 27 Oct

Daniel Miller, who founded Mute, and Wire's Graham Lewis and Bruce Gilbert made one album together, Or So It Seems, in two winter sessions at Blackwing Studios, a bombed and deconsecrated Southwark church that became the lab for Depeche Mode, Yazoo and much of Mute and 4AD. The name is an anagram of Dome, Lewis and Gilbert's duo, and Mute. Ignored at the time, the record was reissued by Mute in 2022, and the trio now play it live for the first time, at London's ICA on 27 October. Don't call it ambient: they reject the word, and the Quietus agrees there is nothing soothing about it.

Nobel Peace Prize · The Hague

Navi Pillay wins the Peace Prize; hours later the US sanctions her old court

The Norwegian Nobel Committee gave the 2026 Peace Prize to Navi Pillay, the South African lawyer who grew up under apartheid and went on to be a judge at the International Criminal Court from 2003 to 2008. Her prize, she said, comes as international law is under growing pressure, nowhere more visibly than in the attacks on the ICC. Within hours Washington proved the point: Secretary of State Marco Rubio announced sanctions on the court itself, freezing its assets, to "ban all business with this rogue court", after earlier sanctions on individual judges and prosecutors. Germany's justice minister Stefanie Hubig called it "a dark day for international law", and the UK is among member states that "strongly disagree". The European Commission says the sanctions take effect only after six months and hopes for talks. Trump told a rally he had "never heard of" Pillay and called the award a stain on Norway.
