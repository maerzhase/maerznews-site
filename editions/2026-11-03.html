Portugal · Europe · Cyber-attack

Hackers turn Europe's supermarket freezers into ovens overnight, and seven Intermarché stores in Portugal are among the victims

The same trick that hit hundreds of French shops in October spreads to eight countries in one night. Tonnes of food go in the bin, and Brussels calls it a deliberate attack on the food supply chain.

RTP · Lusa · BBC · heise online · 2–3 November 2026

In Portugal, hackers broke into the refrigeration system of seven Intermarché supermarkets during Sunday night and the early hours of Monday and switched off fridges and freezers; in some, RTP reports, temperatures climbed to around 30 to 40°C. Several tonnes of food were spoiled. It is the same method used against France three weeks ago, where more than 300 supermarkets were hit, among them, heise reports, 150 Intermarché branches and 80 Carrefour stores.

The same night the attack reached about 40 Delhaize supermarkets in Belgium and Luxembourg, 15 Carrefour and five Spar stores and an Ikea in Mons, 13 SuperValu shops in Ireland, at least 17 companies in the Netherlands, Coop and ICA stores in Sweden, and shops in Estonia and Finland. The method, according to the Belgian refrigeration firm Sabcobel, is to switch the units' evaporators into defrost mode so they heat up. In France the targets were older Danfoss controllers, the AK-SM 800 and AK-SC 355, which no longer receive security updates; Sabcobel's chief says the fix needs physical changes, not just software. Nobody has claimed it.

"A deliberate attempt to disrupt the food supply chain and to undermine consumers' confidence in it." Christophe Hansen, EU agriculture commissioner, to Lusa

Hansen calls the repeated attacks "deeply worrying" and says EU food security is not at risk, but the waste and the damage to businesses are real; he will raise it with fellow commissioners. Belgium's and Sweden's cyber-security agencies are monitoring, and in the United States investigators are looking at whether refrigeration failures on several military bases in August are linked. Most supermarket cooling in Europe is now managed over the internet, which is what makes remote maintenance cheap, and this possible.

Santa Maria da Feira · Industry

ECCO will close its shoe factory in São João de Ver for good, putting 632 people out of work

The Danish shoemaker told its workers on Monday that it is starting the legal procedure to shut its plant in São João de Ver, in the neighbouring municipality of Santa Maria da Feira, "totally and definitively". The closure affects 632 people. It is part of a global reorganisation of ECCO's production that also closes its factory in Vietnam and considers cutting about 400 regional and global jobs, including in Denmark.

"For more than 40 years, the workers in Portugal were an important part of the ECCO family," said Gustavo Kremer, managing director of ECCO Portugal, insisting the decision is no reflection on their work. Notícias de Aveiro reports staff will get one month's salary per year of service. Feira's mayor, Amadeu Albergaria, says the council was "caught by surprise" and is assessing the consequences with the company, workers' representatives and the state; the PCP has asked the government whether the legal grounds for a collective dismissal are met.

Berlin · Coalition

"Not at any price": almost 1,200 SPD members, Giffey and Pistorius among them, sign against governing Berlin under Die Linke

Red-green-red coalition talks began on Monday, six weeks after the Berlin election, and on the same day an appeal titled "Nicht um jeden Preis" passed about 1,175 signatures. They include Berlin's economics senator Franziska Giffey, Neukölln's mayor Martin Hikel, defence minister Boris Pistorius, Karl Lauterbach and Sigmar Gabriel. The signatories say they respect the result but doubt that Berlin's Linke can govern, citing antisemitism incidents in the party. The SPD has about 360,000 members.

Inside the room the mood was better: after almost seven hours, Linke leader Elif Eralp, who could become the first Linke mayor of the capital, spoke of "very good talks", mostly on the budget. The 24-strong top table meets again on Wednesday, 14 working groups start at the end of the week, and the aim is a deal by Christmas. Today CDU leader Stefan Evers meets the Greens and SPD and wants to offer them exploratory talks of his own; both have said they are not interested and will only discuss the handover, a possible supplementary budget and a court ruling on civil servants' pay.

Lisbon · Housing protest

A Lisbon court rules the 00:30 limit on protests is not "absolute and automatic", and the Rossio camp takes its case to President Seguro

The Lisbon Administrative Court has allowed protester Renata Cambra to keep a tent on the Rossio through the night, finding that the 00:30 cut-off applied to the housing protests is not "absolute and automatic". The camp called it a victory for the right to protest; the PSD's parliamentary leader, Hugo Soares, insists it covers one tent only. There was no mass camp in Lisbon last night: the 17th popular assembly voted to sleep on the square tonight instead, and by a large majority to accept an invitation from President António José Seguro, with no date yet. In Porto, 13 tents went up on Praça D. João I just after 00:30. At Monday's Council of State, Cavaco Silva defended the government and warned of the radical left exploiting the protests; housing minister Miguel Pinto Luz says he is ready to negotiate his rental law with Chega.

C++ · Standards

C++26 has been voted down: for the first time, ISO's national bodies reject a draft C++ standard, over Contracts

It was meant to be a formality. After a twelve-week ballot, the Draft International Standard vote on C++26 closed on 23 October with the verdict "Disapproved". A DIS needs a two-thirds majority, with no more than a quarter of votes against, and C++26 missed that bar. The national bodies' comments point to one feature: Contracts, which several of them consider not ready to ship.

Andreas Fertig, who sits on the committee and helps cast Germany's vote, writes that it is the first failed DIS ballot in C++'s history; the only other he could find in ISO's system, C++/CLI in 2006, ended with the project being stopped. The convention was that national bodies vote no only on new information or a process error. That changed on 23 October, and how WG21 now gets C++26 out is open.

Paris · Art fairs

French galleries accuse Art Basel Paris of "disrespect, even contempt" and want paying for the two days it cut

On 22 October, Art Basel Paris emailed exhibitors that, "in light of potential demonstrations across Paris", it would close on 23 October, two days before its scheduled end at the Grand Palais. The fair had already been briefly locked down during installation on 17 October, amid the protests over education and public services that have swept France since mid-September.

Now the Comité Professionnel des Galeries d'Art, which speaks for more than a quarter of the exhibitors, has written to the fair's new director, Karim Crippa: "The decision to close the fair was made by Art Basel Paris, and by Art Basel Paris alone." The galleries say they were never consulted and demand "concrete proposals for compensating exhibitors". Crippa has promised a "concrete update in the coming days".

Leipzig · Organ

Kali Malone recorded her new album on the organ of Leipzig's St. Thomas Church, above Bach's grave

Experimental · drone · pipe organ · Burning Song · with Stephen O'Malley & Lucy Railton

"Burning Song" is the Stockholm-based composer's first solo album since "All Life Long" (2024), and it reunites her with guitarist Stephen O'Malley and cellist Lucy Railton, her partners on "Does Spring Hide Its Joy" (2023). They recorded it at the Thomaskirche, where Bach was cantor for decades and lies buried at the altar. "There's a composer buried at the altar, not a saint," Malone says. "That made it feel like a church for music."

Where her work usually sprawls on the scale of landscapes, these are, Pitchfork writes, "finely filigreed miniatures": melodies that coil and bubble under her fingers, and not one track over seven minutes.
